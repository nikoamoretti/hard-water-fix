Look for a salt cap. Pull the lower rack and look at the floor of the tub, usually by the filter or the drain. You either see a screw-on reservoir or you do not. No cap means no ion-exchange tank. Pouring Finish salt in the detergent cup, or onto the bottom of a US Whirlpool, GE, or Maytag tub that has no tank, does not soften the water.

## What that cap feeds

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heat those minerals and they drop out as calcium carbonate, the spots on glass. Finish UK: dishwasher salt is sodium chloride. It does not wash the load. It flows through the machine’s softener, then drains. Sodium regenerates ion-exchange resins that trap calcium and magnesium. No tank, no resin. The cap is the test.

## Which machines have a tank (look, do not assume)

Bosch US: “Some models use softener salt.” “Refer to your dishwasher’s user manual to learn if your model uses salt.” Mixed lines, not all Bosch. US spec sheets list Water Softener: No on some 100 Series and 300 Series units. Bosch Benchmark marketing names a built-in water softener. Round screw cap on the tub floor: tank. No cap: salt has nowhere to go.

Miele US manuals assume a salt reservoir after you pull the lower basket. Beko US: below and to the left of the bottom rack. Whirlpool, Maytag, and KitchenAid: “not all dishwashers have a salt reservoir.” Some of their lines do. GE’s typical US support is detergent and a rinse-aid dispenser in the door, not a salt tank. Older GE sheets that mention hardness point you at a house water softener at 12 grains or more, not a scoop in the tub.

Check the manual. Look in the tub. Not “all Bosch.” Not “all Whirlpool.”

## How to fill (only after you found the cap)

Follow **your** manual. Live Finish how-to plus Bosch US clock. No invented grams.

1. Empty the machine. Pull the lower rack. Find the screw cap on the tub floor. Finish: usually at the bottom by the drain. Beko US: often below and to the left. No cap? Stop.
2. Unscrew the cap. First use, if the manual says so: fill the reservoir with water so the salt is in solution. Finish: salt has to be in a solution to regenerate the softener. Bosch US tank manuals: on initial start-up, fill the dispenser right up with water. Later refills already have water. Salt displaces it. Overflow is normal.
3. Pour [Miele dishwasher salt](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004ETS67W?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) with a funnel if you have one. Bosch US: fill until water is displaced and runs out. Finish: leave about 1 cm under the lip. Wipe the threads and the tub. Beko: spilled salt can scratch glass. Bosch: leftover granules can corrode stainless.
4. Cap tight. Bosch US: run a load right after so residual salt does not sit on the metal. Finish: if you are not running now, wipe displaced water.
5. Set water hardness if the machine asks. Bosch US: harder water, higher setting, more salt. The table is in **your** book. Bosch manuals: 0 to 8 °E can skip special salt.

## Never table salt

Finish: dishwasher salt is coarser and free from iodine and anti-caking agents, which are often in table salt. Those additives can interfere with the softening system. The finer grain can block the softener. Bosch US: coarse granules so they will not clog the tank; do not use table salt. Bosch manuals: special dishwasher salt only, no salt tablets. Whirlpool (tank models): no table, sea, rock, or de-icing salt. Anti-caking agents or impurities can clog the softener.

Use [Miele dishwasher salt](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004ETS67W?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). Not the kitchen canister.

## All-in-one tabs are not the tank

Finish Quantum FAQ: the tablets combine detergent, rinse aid, and water-softening agents. In hard water they do **not** entirely replace dedicated salt. Bosch US: many all-in-one tabs contain dishwasher salt. In a hard water area, salt is still essential. In moderately soft water, Bosch says tabs can suffice.

The reservoir regenerates resin. A tab dissolves in the wash. If this machine has a cap and the tap is hard, keep the tank filled.

## What not to do

## If the spots come back

The tap is still hard. USGS: calcium and magnesium. Salt only treats water that passes the resin, and only if the tank exists and is filled. [How to test water hardness at home](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home) is the Ca+Mg check. This page does not size a whole-house softener.

## FAQ

**My US Whirlpool / GE / Maytag has no salt cap. Do I still buy salt?**
No. No cap, salt does nothing. Use rinse aid for sheeting. Test the tap if spots keep returning.

**Can I use table salt in a pinch?**
No. Finish: iodine and anti-caking agents; grain size can clog. Bosch: coarse granules only. Whirlpool tank models: table, sea, rock, and de-icing salts are out.

**I use Quantum / 3-in-1 tabs. Can I skip the salt tank?**
Not in hard water, if this machine has a tank. Finish: Quantum does not entirely replace dedicated salt. Bosch US: in a hard water area, the tank still matters.

**Is dishwasher salt the same as rinse aid?**
No. Salt (if present) treats incoming water via ion exchange. Rinse aid is a surfactant on the last rinse. Finish wants both in very hard water. Rinse-aid how-to: [best rinse aid for hard water](/articles/best-rinse-aid-for-hard-water).

**Why Finish if the US site does not list salt?**
It is the queued coarse dishwasher NaCl, not a ranking. Reckitt’s salt instructions are the UK Finish pages. Finish US currently sells detergent, Jet-Dry, and cleaner, not a salt SKU. If you found a cap, that is still the bottle class for the tank.
