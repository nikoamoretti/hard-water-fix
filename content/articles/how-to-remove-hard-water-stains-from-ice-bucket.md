## Vinegar short soak. Soft sponge or soft cloth. Rinse until no smell. Dry fully.

1. Empty the bucket. Dump old ice and melt. Rinse crumbs and slime. Do not start a mineral soak on top of thick freezer sludge.
2. Identify the material. Plastic or stainless: the full **5–15 minute** short soak is fine. **Aluminum:** stay near **5 minutes**. Do not overnight-soak aluminum. Do not use CLR on any of them — this is food-contact.
3. Identify white mineral haze vs slime or mold film. White chalk gets this vinegar pass. Slime needs a mild dish-soap wipe first. Mold needs a hygiene wipe first.
4. Mix **1:1** distilled white vinegar (5% pantry bottle) and water. Fill the bucket so the mix covers the chalk line (and soak a removable lid in a basin if it does not fit).
5. **Short soak 5–15 minutes.** Stay in the room. Swirl or wipe once so the walls, floor, and rim see the mix. Aluminum: pull it at **~5 minutes**. Do not overnight-seal strong acid as a habit on thin plastic or aluminum.
6. After the soak, wipe with a **soft sponge or soft cloth** only. Soft nylon / cellulose sponge or a clean cotton cloth. No steel wool. No green pad on plastic or coated buckets. No metal pick. No green pad on aluminum — it scratches and the next chalk packs faster.
7. Dump the vinegar mix. Rinse thoroughly with clean water until the bucket **does not smell like pickle**. Food-contact path — rinse until no vinegar smell remains. Rinse the lid the same way.
8. Optional pantry **distilled water** wipe so tap spots do not flash-dry back onto the walls — grocery jug, pantry text. No Amazon distilled URL (NONE_QUALIFY).
9. **Dry fully** before the next ice goes in. Air-dry or towel-dry the walls, floor, rim, underside of the lid, and any liner. Spots return if you refill a wet bucket. Damp chalk plus melt is the next white film.

One short 1:1 soak clears most fresh haze. Raised chalk that survives one pass may need a second **short soak** (still **~5 minutes** on aluminum) and another soft cloth. ## Hard-water signs on an ice bucket

Know the film before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze on the walls or rim after hard-tap melt sits and dries: early mineral film. Vinegar **1:1**, **short soak** (shorter on aluminum), soft sponge or soft cloth, rinse until no smell, dry fully.
- Raised white chalk a fingernail catches on the floor, the waterline, or the lid underside: carbonate scale. Same short soak. Soft cloth. Full rinse. Dry fully.
- Cloudy “dirty ice” look in the bin that soap alone does not clear: often mineral, not dirt. Honesty: chalk reads as dirty ice until vinegar softens it.
- Aluminum that darkens or looks dull after a long acid sit: that is the metal reacting, not leftover calcium. Honesty: shorten the clock. Do not reach for CLR.
- Scratched plastic that re-hazes fast after every clean: surface harbors film. Prevention (dry fully; distilled pantry wipe) matters more than stronger acid.
