## Find the arms. Read the manual. Then detach.

Most machines have a lower arm on the tub floor and an upper arm under the top rack. Some add a third on the ceiling. Bosch US: clean spray arms every few months. Hold each arm up to a light. Dark holes are blocked. White crust is scale. A seed or label scrap is food. Both stop the jet.

**Your booklet first.** Twist or clip. Do not force. GE: if the owner's manual has no lower-arm instructions, the arm is not owner-removable. Wait until the heating element and interior have cooled.

Empty the tub. Pull the racks.

**Lower arm.** Bosch: grasp and lift; press until it snaps back. GE (standard removable, no extending wash tower): lift while rotating counter-clockwise about 90 degrees; replace clockwise until it locks. Whirlpool / iFixit: rotate the lower-arm base counterclockwise. Fred's: some Whirlpool arms pull up and snap; spin after the click. LG: pull the lower rack forward and pull the arm up.

**Upper / middle arm.** Bosch: empty the top rack, turn it over, locking nut counterclockwise. LG: nut under the rack counterclockwise, pull down. iFixit Whirlpool: rotate the top-arm base clockwise.

If it binds, stop. Look for a clip, knurled nut, or quarter-turn hub. Axis Repair: do not force the plastic. GE: if the book is silent, leave it on.

## Toothpick the jets first

Bosch US: while rinsing with warm water, use a toothpick to clear the holes. LG: toothpick; tap the arm on a flat surface; pour water through to confirm. Family Handyman: toothpick the openings. Tricity Repairs: work from the outside in, pushing debris into the hollow interior. Axis Repair: wooden toothpicks.

**Wooden toothpick or nylon.** Not a metal needle. Axis Repair: never use metal needles, safety pins, or drill bits — enlarging or scratching the holes ruins the spray pattern. Tricity: avoid metal that could enlarge the openings. Fred's names a paper clip. Skip it.

Food bits and calcium both block jets. Citrus seeds, grit, label scrap. White crust is calcium carbonate from heated hard water. Poke every hole. Do not shove a seed deeper; break it so it falls into the chamber. The toothpick is the plug. The soak is the crust.

## Vinegar soak of the detached arms

**Detached arms only.** Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Not a vinegar dump in the tub. Maytag: vinegar is an acid and using it too often could damage your dishwasher. This soak is in a sink or bowl, plastic off the machine.

Axis Repair: submerge the jet holes in warm distilled white vinegar for **30 to 45 minutes**. Acetic acid breaks down calcium carbonate without degrading the plastic. Tricity: 50/50 vinegar and warm water, 15–30 minutes, then a soft brush. Use the longer Axis clock if the plastic and your booklet allow it. If the book forbids vinegar on that part, stop at the toothpick and a hot-water flush.

1. Sink or bowl. Arms fully under the liquid.
2. Distilled white vinegar from the pantry. Warm is fine. Not boiling.
3. 30–45 minutes if booklet and plastic allow.
4. Soft toothbrush around the holes. No scouring powder on the orifices.
5. Never mix vinegar and bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help 1-800-222-1222.

No citric on this page.

## Flush from the hub. Reinstall. Confirm it spins.

LG: fill the arm with hot water and shake; pour water through to double-check. Axis Repair: hold the center hub against the faucet, full blast, so water shoots from the jets and debris exits the hub. Bosch manuals: flush nozzles under running water.

Every hole should spit a stream. A dark hole goes back to the toothpick. Shake the tips. Scale and seeds hide there.

Reinstall the way the book says. Bosch: press the bottom arm until it snaps. LG: push the lower arm down until it clips; upper nut clockwise until it locks. GE: clockwise until it locks. Hand-tight. Do not over-torque a plastic nut.

**Spin test.** Bosch US, LG, Fred's, and Axis Repair want the arm to turn freely. Flick it. Zero drag. If it binds, it is not seated, or debris is in the hub. Axis: do not run a bound arm.

Dishwasher salt regenerates an ion-exchange tank, and only if you found a cap on the tub floor. It does not pick food out of a nozzle. That job is [dishwasher salt for hard water](/articles/dishwasher-salt-hard-water). This page does not recopy it.

## What not to do

- Do not treat a tub tablet as an unclog. Jets come off first.
- Do not force a stuck arm. Manual first. GE: no instructions means not owner-removable.
- Do not use a metal needle, paper clip, sewing pin, or drill bit. Wooden toothpick or nylon.
- Do not soak the arms in the machine. Detach, then vinegar in a sink.
- Do not mix vinegar and bleach. Chlorine gas.
- Do not pour dishwasher salt in the detergent cup to “fix” a clogged arm.
- Do not use citric on this page.
- Do not drop washer Affresh in the dishwasher. Different tablet.

## FAQ

**Can I just run a cleaner tablet instead of pulling the arms?**
No. A tablet circulates. A clogged jet is a plug. Toothpick, then vinegar soak of the detached arm, then flush from the hub.

**Wooden toothpick or a metal pin?**
Wooden toothpick or nylon. Axis Repair and Tricity: metal can enlarge or scratch the orifice and ruin the spray pattern.

**How long is the vinegar soak?**
30–45 minutes on detached arms if the booklet and plastic allow. Axis Repair. Distilled white vinegar, pantry. Not in the tub.

**Why are only the top-rack dishes dirty?**
The upper or middle arm feeds that rack. Bosch and LG both tell you to pull that arm too.

**Will vinegar in the dishwasher unclog the jets?**
Maytag: vinegar is an acid and using it too often could damage your dishwasher. Soak the detached arms in a sink. Do not dump vinegar in the tub as this how-to.

**Can I mix vinegar and bleach to get scale and germs?**
No. Poison Control: bleach plus an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar. Fresh air. 1-800-222-1222.

**Is dishwasher salt the same job?**
No. Salt is the tank, if the tank exists. [Dishwasher salt for hard water](/articles/dishwasher-salt-hard-water). Not a jet poke.
