## Unplug. Shut both faucets. Catch the hose water. Pull the screens.

The screens sit in the valve ports, not in the drum. SlashGear: on most washers they live where the supply hoses connect to the back of the unit. Appliance Repair Shymon: a small mesh screen is seated inside that threaded port. Its job is to catch debris before water reaches the valve. Most machines have two — hot and cold. Shymon: the cold side handles nearly every load on modern cycles, so it clogs first.

LG USA: press POWER, unplug, then turn off both water taps completely. SlashGear: turn off the washer, unplug it, and close the water valves before you touch a hose.

1. Unplug the washer. Do not skip this. Water and a live machine do not mix.
2. Shut **both** faucets. Hot and cold. Clockwise until they stop. Do not crank past the stop.
3. Pull the machine out enough to reach the back. Bucket and towel under the hose fittings. LG USA: after the supply is off, water is still in the hoses. Place a bucket beneath them.
4. Unscrew the hot and cold hoses from the back of the washer. SlashGear: rotate the connection counterclockwise, one hose at a time. Catch the water. Do not let it run into the motor.
5. Look into each valve port. LG: small circular inlet filters sit in those water inlets. Recessed mesh. That is the screen.
6. Needle-nose pliers. LG USA: use needle-nose pliers, or another grabbing tool with small tips, and pull the filters out of the inlet valves. Shymon: light, even pressure. **Pull straight.** Do not twist the mesh. Do not crush it. LG: be careful not to damage the filters while cleaning them.
7. Photograph the orientation if the screen has a plastic tab or a cup face. It goes back the same way.

A secondary screen can live in the hose end. Check that too. Rinse it. The valve-port mesh is the one that starves the fill.

SlashGear: some machines also have a drain-pump filter. Front-loaders often hide that behind a small panel at the bottom. That is lint and coins, not this job. Leave it.

## Vinegar soak of the detached screens

**Detached screens only.** Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Pantry. No Amazon link.

LG owner’s manuals: soak the filters in white vinegar or a lime scale remover. Vinegar is the default on this page. The lime-scale bottle is the next section, and only if **your** booklet allows it.

Shymon: rinse under warm running water and scrub with an old toothbrush. Heavy mineral buildup — white or grey chalky crust — responds to a **20-to-30-minute** soak in white vinegar. USGS: vinegar is an acid; the acidity helps dissolve mineral so water can carry it away.

1. Small bowl. Screens fully under the liquid.
2. Distilled white vinegar from the pantry.
3. 20–30 minutes on a chalky mesh (Shymon). Longer only if the crust is still locked on after a rinse. Not overnight in a metal bowl you cannot rinse.
4. Soft toothbrush. Circular, light. Do not mash the mesh flat.
5. Rinse under a strong cold tap until water runs through the holes. LG: rinse thoroughly before reinstalling. Wash thoroughly in warm water until the buildup is gone (LG USA).
6. Hold it up to a light. Dark holes are still blocked. Poke debris out with a wooden toothpick if one grain is stuck. Not a nail. Not a screw.

**Orange grit is iron or sediment, not only calcium.** Shymon: rust-coloured deposits usually mean sediment from older pipes; those often rinse away. Do not keep soaking orange grit in more acid hoping it is lime. Rinse. Brush. If the mesh is packed with rust flakes and will not pass water, replace the screen.

**A deformed screen is a replacement, not more acid.** Shymon: if the mesh looks distorted — bent out of plane or pushed to one side — do not reinstall it. A bent screen passes debris it should catch. LG: if the filters cannot be cleaned or are damaged, do **not** run the washer without them. Get a new screen.

Never mix vinegar and bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids, and rust-removal products too. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

No citric on this page. No Lime-A-Way. No Calgon in the wash as a screen fix.

## Reinstall the screens fully. Open the faucets. Check for leaks. Test the fill.

LG: rinse thoroughly, then press the filters back into place. Shymon: seat the screen straight. Pressing it in at an angle bends the mesh and turns a five-minute job into a parts order.

**Reinstall the screens. Do not run without them.** LG WT7100 and matching LG top-load books: the inlet screens protect the delicate parts of the inlet valve. Operating the washer with these filters removed could cause a valve to **stick open**, resulting in flooding and/or property damage. Do not use the washer without the inlet filters — leaks and malfunction. LG WM3001: do **not** operate the washer without the inlet filters. If they are damaged, call service. Shymon: the machine will run without the mesh, but the screen protects the solenoid seat from particles that can jam it.

1. Press each screen fully home, cup or tab the same way it came out. Flush. No proud edge.
2. LG: before reattaching the hoses, flush the hoses and water lines by running several gallons into a bucket or drain, faucets wide open. Sand and scale in the hose will reclog a clean screen in one fill.
3. Check the rubber washer in each hose fitting. Shymon: a cracked or flattened washer causes a slow drip. Hardware-store washer. Not tape.
4. Hand-start the hose nuts. Snug. Do not cross-thread. Do not crush the plastic coupling.
5. Open both faucets slowly. Watch the connections. Drips get another quarter-turn, then stop. A spray means the washer is missing or the screen is not seated.
6. Plug the washer back in. Run a short fill or rinse. Watch the drum. Cold should come in at a normal clip. Shymon: a washer that fills slowly on cold but normally on hot is almost always the cold-side screen or valve. If both sides are still dead after a clean screen, that is supply pressure or a solenoid, not more vinegar.

SlashGear: LG recommends cleaning the inlet filters every six months, and more often in hard water. Samsung, same idea. Shymon: once a year on typical municipal water; every six months on well water or a notably hard supply. Check **your** book.

If the screens are clean and the fill is still dead, stop. Shymon: a clean screen that does not restore fill points to a failing inlet-valve solenoid or household pressure below what the valve needs. That is a technician, not a second acid bath.

## FAQ

**Is a slow fill a dead pump or clogged inlet screens?**

Look in the valve ports first. LG: clogged inlet filters make the washer fill slowly or not at all, and can throw a fill error. Shymon: cold-only slow fill almost always traces to the cold-side screen or valve. A drain-pump filter is a different part.

**Where are the screens?**

In the inlet-valve ports on the back of the washer, behind the fill hoses. SlashGear: where the supply hoses connect. Some hose ends have a second screen. Pull both hoses. Check both ports.

**Vinegar or CLR on the mesh?**

Vinegar first. Grocery bottle. 20–30 minutes (Shymon). LG allows white vinegar or a lime-scale remover. CLR is the optional booklet-allowed path: 50/50, **2 minutes**, hard rinse. If **your** book is silent or forbids a lime-scale dip, stay on vinegar.

**Can I run the washer without putting the screens back?**

No. LG: operating without the inlet filters can cause a valve to stick open, flooding, leaks, and malfunction. Shymon: the screen protects the solenoid seat. Reinstall fully, or replace a damaged one.

**The grit is orange. Same soak?**

Not as calcium. Shymon: rust-coloured deposits are usually sediment from older pipes and often rinse off. Brush. Rinse. If the mesh stays packed, replace it. Do not keep adding acid.

**How often should I clean them?**

SlashGear: LG says every six months, more often in hard water. Samsung follows the same idea. Shymon: yearly on typical city water; every six months on well water or a very hard supply. USGS: hard at 121 mg/L as CaCO3; very hard above 180. [Test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home) if you do not have a number.

**Will cleaning the screens fix stiff towels or white residue on clothes?**

No. That is the tap and the wash chemistry, not this mesh. [How to remove hard water stains from clothes](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-clothes). The drum clean is [how to clean a washing machine](/articles/how-to-clean-washing-machine-hard-water).

**Can I mix vinegar or CLR with bleach to get scale and germs?**

No. Poison Control: bleach plus an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar and rust-removal products. Leave the area. Fresh air. **1-800-222-1222**.
