## What you are dissolving

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heat them and you get solid calcium carbonate — scale — on heaters and inside pipes. USGS even names the home coffee maker: mineral buildup happens there too, which is why people run vinegar through the pot. The acid charges the mineral so water can carry it away.

Same job in a Keurig tank, a drip reservoir, or an espresso thermocoil. Scale is non-toxic. Left alone it slows the brew and clogs the path. A descale light is usually a volume counter, not a hardness sensor. Hard-water houses should not wait for it.

## Vinegar: when the booklet still says yes

Distilled white vinegar. Household bottles are about **5% acetic acid** (Harvard: that is the pantry stuff; FDA requires at least 4%). Not apple cider. Not cleaning vinegar at 6% unless the sheet says so.

Many basic drip machines still print vinegar in the decalcify section:

- **Cuisinart** DCC-3200 PerfecTemp booklet: fill the reservoir with **1/3 white vinegar and 2/3 water**, hold CLEAN until it flashes, then run a full tank of fresh water. Pull the charcoal filter first.
- **Mr. Coffee**'s own how-to: white vinegar and water (about half as much vinegar as water), brew or use Clean, sit 15–30 minutes, then **two** water-only brews.

If *your* drip sheet names vinegar, use that dilution. Run the sit if it asks for one. Rinse until steam does not smell like salad. Vinegar works. It just lingers.

Keurig-specific steps live on [how to descale a Keurig with vinegar](/articles/how-to-descale-a-keurig-with-vinegar). This page is the comparison, not a second how-to.

## Brand bottles: when the manual names them

Buy the house bottle when the sheet names only that bottle.

**Keurig.** Current keurig.com/descale: scale is calcium, non-toxic, descale every 3 to 6 months or when the light says so, and they shop [Keurig Descaling Solution](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00FOT9W5Q?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). Older official PDFs still on keurig.com list **white vinegar as Method 2**. Both are real. Keurig does not publish a one-line "vinegar voids the warranty" on the current descale page. In-warranty, or a light that will not die: their bottle.

**Nespresso.** Official US guides: machines "should be descaled with a Nespresso Descaling Kit." Then: "Other solutions (such as vinegar or store-bought descalers) may cause damage to your Nespresso machine." Every 3 months or 300 capsules. Use their kit. Do not swap in Dezcal — they named store-bought descalers in the same sentence.

**De'Longhi** super-automatics. Official FAQ: "Use the DeLonghi descaler and never vinegar!" Care page: DIY vinegar "can incur corrosion and leave behind a lingering aftertaste." They want EcoDecalk (lactic acid). Check the manual; do not invent a citric workaround.

**Jura.** Official J10 twin instructions: "Non-recommended descaling agents (e.g. vinegar) may contain substances that could damage the machine or leave residues." Then: "Use only original JURA descaling tablets."

**Breville / Sage espresso.** Split by model. Current Barista Express (BES870) US booklet: dissolve **one Breville Descaling powder sachet into 34 fl. oz (1 L)** and run descale mode. Vinegar is not mentioned. Older Breville espresso booklets (Duo-Temp Pro, Compact Café, Infuser BES840) *do* list a white-vinegar mix. Absence of a ban is not permission on a powder-only sheet. Check yours.

## One citric powder that covers drip and espresso

Urnex Dezcal is the cafe descaler, not Keurig's official bottle. Official Urnex: citric-acid based (ingredients sheet: citric as the main cleaning agent, plus a little sulfamic), odor-free, and it "rinses away thoroughly, unlike vinegar." Consumer packets and a liquid. No NSF claim on the pages we fetched. Do not invent one.

Consumer powder (Urnex home-packet sheet): dissolve **one 28 g packet in 32 oz (1 L) of lukewarm water**, brew the tank through, then **three** full tanks of clean water. Liquid: **1 oz per liter** of tank capacity. For espresso, Urnex says follow the machine's own descale program.

Use Dezcal when the manual allows a citric / generic coffee-machine descaler, or names no house brand. One jar for a drip pot and a home espresso machine is the reason to keep it. If the sheet names only a Nespresso kit, Jura tablets, or EcoDecalk, buy that.

Official Dezcal pages do not print a hard aluminum ban. Any acid left sitting can pit aluminum. Do not soak an aluminum boiler overnight.

Kettles are not coffee makers. Citric kettle steps: [how to descale an electric kettle](/articles/how-to-descale-an-electric-kettle).

## What not to do

- Do not pour vinegar into a machine whose official page says not to (Nespresso, Jura, De'Longhi super-autos).
- Do not skip rinses. Vinegar smell is leftover acid, not leftover scale.
- Do not put CLR, Bar Keepers Friend, Lime-A-Way, or dishwasher rinse aid in a brew path. CLR is for surfaces — [vinegar vs CLR](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains).
- Do not descale with a pod, capsule, or basket of grounds in the holder.
- Do not mix any descaler with bleach.
- Do not treat Dezcal as Keurig's official cycle. If the light is the problem, their bottle matches the script.

## FAQ

**Does vinegar void the warranty?**
We will not invent that sentence. Nespresso, Jura, and De'Longhi tell you not to use vinegar. Keurig's current page names their solution; older official PDFs list vinegar as Method 2. Breville depends on the booklet in the box. In-warranty: use what the sheet names and keep the receipt.

**Do both acids actually work?**
Yes. Acetic acid (vinegar) and citric descalers both dissolve calcium carbonate. Same mineral job. Vinegar smells and tastes for a day. Citric rinses cleaner. No "% removed" scores — we did not run that test.

**Can I use Dezcal in a Keurig?**
Chemically yes, if you follow Urnex's packet. It will not automatically be the cycle a Keurig descale light is waiting for. In-warranty, or light stuck on: [Keurig Descaling Solution](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00FOT9W5Q?tag=hardwaterfi04-20).

**How often?**
Keurig: 3 to 6 months. Nespresso: 3 months or 300 capsules. Hard water: the short end. A reminder light is not a hardness meter.
