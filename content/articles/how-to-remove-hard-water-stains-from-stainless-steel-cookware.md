## Vinegar 1:1 simmer. Cool. Wash. Dry.

Pantry distilled white vinegar. Equal parts water. No Amazon vinegar URL. No “improve it” with lemon, baking-soda grit, CLR, or a second acid as the default. This is the Made In / All-Clad-style pantry path for mineral film on food-contact stainless.

1. Empty the pot or pan. Cool enough to handle safely. Do not pour vinegar into a screaming-hot empty pan on a burner.
2. Mix **1:1** distilled white vinegar (5% pantry bottle) and water. Pour enough to cover the chalk bloom on the cooking surface.
3. Bring to a gentle **simmer** on the stove. A few minutes is enough for fresh white film. Stay in the kitchen. Do not boil dry.
4. Kill the heat. Let the pot **cool** until you can handle it. Pour out the vinegar water into the sink with the tap running.
5. Wash with normal dish soap and a soft sponge. Rinse thoroughly so no vinegar taste remains.
6. **Dry immediately** with a clean towel. Standing rinse water flash-dries into fresh mineral spots on stainless. Do not air-dry hard tap on the cooking surface.

One simmer pass clears fresh white bloom. Thick raised chalk may need a second 1:1 simmer. If the mark is rainbow/blue heat tint, stop expecting vinegar to erase it — that is not scale.

## Hard-water signs inside stainless cookware

Know the film before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze after boiling pasta, potatoes, or eggs in tap: early mineral film. Vinegar 1:1 simmer and a dry-towel habit after each wash stretch the gap.
- Raised chalk a fingernail catches on the floor or waterline ring: carbonate scale. On-stove 1:1 vinegar simmer. Soft wash. Immediate dry.
- Cloudy spots that appear only after the pan air-dries on the rack: rinse water, not “permanent damage.” Dry the pan; do not leave hard tap to evaporate on stainless.
- Rainbow, blue, or gold sheen after searing or empty-pan overheating: heat tint, not calcium. Dissolves poorly or not at all in vinegar. Do not keep simmering acid chasing a color that is oxide, not scale.
- Brown or rainbow plus stuck-on food: clean food first with normal wash; then decide if white chalk remains for a vinegar simmer.
- White ring at the former waterline after a long stock simmer: classic hard-water carbonate. Same 1:1 path. Cover the ring fully when you pour.
