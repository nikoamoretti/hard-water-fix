## Tannin honesty: brown tea film is not chalk

Do the stain test on a dry, uncleaned patch, in daylight. Run a fingernail across the film. Dampen that same patch. Look again.

- White crust that feels chalky and fades while wet is **mineral film**. Distilled white vinegar **soak for chalk** is still the job. Soft detail brush for mesh and seams. Distilled pantry rinse until no vinegar smell or taste.
- Brown or rust-tea tint that stays brown after a short vinegar pass is **tannin** — leaf stain on the mesh, not calcium. Honesty: **brown tea film ≠ chalk.** Baking soda or mild dish soap for tannin. Vinegar for mineral. Do not reach for CLR “to get the brown out.”
- Both can live on the same ball. Vinegar will clear the chalk and leave the brown. Believe the brown. Soap or a baking-soda paste on the tannin, then a separate vinegar pass if chalk remains. Never mix leftover soap-and-bleach “sanitizer” with the vinegar soak.

A dishwasher can bake tannin and chalk onto fine mesh. If the booklet allows the top rack, that is wash, not a restore. Do not keep aciding a brown infuser that already had its mineral pass.
