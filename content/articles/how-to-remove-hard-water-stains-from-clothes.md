## Gray or orange

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Soap plus calcium is scum. Hard water takes more detergent. Iowa State Extension (Janis Stone): nonphosphate granular detergents can combine with hard water and leave residue that makes fabrics stiff, harsh, and dull. That is gray. It is not rust.

|---|---|---|
| What it is | Calcium/magnesium plus detergent residue | Iron on fabric |

| Chlorine bleach | Do not mix vinegar with bleach (K-State: harmful vapors) | Sets the stain (UGA). Clorox: bleach plus iron becomes rust and deposits on clothes |

Orange on laundry is iron, a different metal. EPA secondary (nuisance) standard for iron is 0.3 mg/L: rusty color, reddish or orange staining. A hardness strip is not an iron test. [How to test water hardness at home](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home) is the Ca+Mg check.

## Vinegar soak (gray, stiff, dull)

Iowa State: soaking stiffened clothing in white vinegar and water — **1 cup vinegar per gallon of water** — may help restore them. Test colorfastness on a hidden seam first. Vinegar may weaken cotton, rayon, acetate, triacetate, or silk, and may change some dyes.

1. Read the care label. Skip dry-clean-only.

2. Spot-test distilled white vinegar on a hidden seam.

3. Mix 1 cup distilled white vinegar per gallon of water. Submerge the gray/stiff items.

4. Soak. Iowa State does not publish a minute clock. Do not invent one. When the fabric feels less harsh, lift it out. A second fresh soak if it is still stiff — not an overnight acid bath.

5. Rinse thoroughly. Residual acid plus later bleach is a bad mix.

6. Wash as usual. Kansas State Extension: 1 cup white vinegar in the washer’s **final rinse** can dissolve alkaline soap and detergent residue. Do not use vinegar if chlorine bleach is in the rinse. Harmful vapors.

7. Inspect wet. Iowa State: if a stain is still evident, do not dryer-dry. Heat tends to make stains more permanent. UGA (Pamela Turner): stains gone before the dryer. Air-dry until the gray film is gone.

If it is still stiff after soak and rinse, leftover residue is the optional [laundry additive](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00CNJREKK?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) path — a borax or Calgon-style water conditioner in the wash, the way Iowa State and K-State describe those products. Not a rust remover.

If the mark is now orange, stop the vinegar path. That is iron.

## Iron Out (orange rust on fabric)

Follow **your** box. Summit’s white-laundry method:

1. Ventilate. Gloves. Eye protection — SDS: causes serious eye damage. Pre-test in an inconspicuous area. Stubborn stains: increase dosage and/or contact time (still on the label).

2. Do not use peroxide, bleach, bleach-detergents, oxi-stain removers, or oxi-detergents with this product.

3. Add ½ cup [Iron Out](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07N4KGV2Q?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) powder to warm water as the washer is filling. Front-loaders: add it to the detergent drawer.

4. Add clothes. Soak **5 minutes** (soak cycle, or stop the machine).

5. Add detergent. Resume wash and rinse.

6. Inspect wet before any dryer. If the orange is gone, Summit says dry as usual. If it remains, air-dry and repeat the labeled soak — do not bake leftover rust in. Iowa State: do not iron stained fabric either. Heat sets most stains.

## What not to do

- Do not use chlorine bleach on orange rust. UGA: it will set these stains. Clorox: bleach plus iron in well water converts iron to rust, which then deposits on clothes.
- Do not mix vinegar with chlorine bleach. K-State: harmful vapors. Washington State DOH: bleach plus an acid gives off chlorine gas.
- Do not mix Iron Out with bleach, peroxide, oxi, or CLR. Reducer plus acid → toxic gas (SDS).
- Do not dryer-dry or iron until the stain is gone.
- Do not treat this as a washer-cleaner page. The Affresh empty cycle is the machine, not the shirt.
- Do not pour kettle descaler or citric powder into the laundry.

## If it comes back

Gray that returns is still hardness plus detergent. USGS: more soap is needed in hard water. Test Ca+Mg. This page does not size a softener. Orange that returns is iron in the supply (EPA 0.3 mg/L is a nuisance stain line, not a health MCL) or a rusty washer part. Drum cleanup is the [washing machine](/articles/how-to-clean-washing-machine-hard-water) page. Do not bleach the next load to “whiten” it.

## FAQ

**Can vinegar remove orange rust from clothes?**
Not the job. Vinegar soak is Iowa State’s mineral and detergent-residue path. Orange is iron. UGA: commercial rust remover, following the label. That labeled laundry path is Iron Out powder.

**Why did bleach make it worse?**
Chlorine bleach is an oxidizer. UGA: it sets rust on fabric. Clorox: bleach active plus iron in the water becomes rust and deposits on clothes.

**Can I dry it to see if the stain is gone?**
No. Inspect wet. Iowa State: dryer heat tends to make remaining stains more permanent. Air-dry until it is gone.

**Is Iron Out safe on colors?**
Summit: test every color; colorfast dyes only; wash items separately; may irreparably damage colors and embroidered logos. Whites are the labeled default.
