Cloudy glasses out of the dishwasher are two problems with the same face. One is a mineral film you can wipe off with vinegar. The other is etching: the glass surface itself is pitted, and no cleaner, polish, or “restore” bottle brings it back.

Do the test before you buy anything. If it is etch, skip the product section. That is the whole page.

## The one-minute test

Whirlpool: dip a cloth in distilled white vinegar, rub a small patch in a circle. Maytag: same rub. The Spruce: a five-minute vinegar soak. Any of those is enough.

Hold the glass up to daylight after you wipe it dry.

| Result | Diagnosis | Next move |
|---|---|---|
| The rubbed patch is clear; the rest is still milky | Hard-water **film** (calcium/magnesium sitting on top) | Soak the set, then change the wash. Products below apply. |
| The patch is still cloudy, even while wet | **Etching** (or silica film Maytag also flags) | Stop. No restore product. Cut detergent, heat, and pre-rinsing. |
| Rainbow / oil-slick shimmer in the haze | Treat as etch until proven otherwise | Same as etching. Acid has nothing to dissolve. |

A fingernail that catches chalk is film. A smooth frost that vinegar cannot touch is damage. USGS hardness explains film. It does not explain etch. Whirlpool’s etch recipe is the opposite water: **soft** water + plenty of detergent + high heat + a light soil load.

Do not skip the test because you “know” you have hard water. Softened houses etch glasses all the time.

## If the test says film

The haze is minerals. Acid removes it. Prevention is chelation in the wash and sheeting in the rinse.

**Rescue the set.** Sink, glasses on their side, warm distilled white vinegar, 15–30 minutes. Soft sponge. Hot rinse. **Hand dry.** Two soaks max.

## If the test says etch: do not buy a restore product

There is no honest cream, polish, or descaler for dishwasher-etched glass. Whirlpool, Maytag, and The Spruce all say the damage is permanent. Anyone selling a “glass restore” for this is selling hope into a sanded surface.

You can stop it getting worse. You cannot un-frost the set.

1. **Use less detergent**, especially if you have a softener.
2. **Stop pre-rinsing.** Scrape, do not wash.
3. **Drop the heat.** Skip sanitize and heated dry.
4. **Do not keep turning rinse aid up** if the water is already too soft.
5. Hand-wash the glasses you still care about.

## What not to do

- Do not buy a glass-restore kit for this.
- Do not pour CLR on dinner glassware.
- Do not mix vinegar or rinse aid with bleach.
- Do not add more detergent because the glasses look dirty if the test already failed.

## FAQ

**Can etching and film happen on the same glass?**
Yes. Vinegar will clear the coat and leave the frost. Believe the frost.

**I have a water softener and cloudy glasses.**
Start with the etch path. Run the vinegar test anyway.

**Will a new dishwasher fix etched glasses?**
No. The glasses are damaged.
