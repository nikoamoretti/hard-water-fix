## Vinegar how-to (film)

Light haze you can barely feel. Not raised crust. The film-versus-crust split is on [vinegar vs CLR](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains).

1. Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle, about 5% acetic acid.
2. Good Housekeeping Institute mix for glass shower doors: equal parts vinegar and water, plus 1 teaspoon dishwashing liquid, in a spray bottle.
3. Spray. Sit 15 minutes. Wipe. Rinse. Dry.
4. Repeat a timed pass. Do not leave it overnight. Two failed film passes means crust, not a longer soak.

If it is greasy and filmy, take soap scum off first. Scum shields the mineral.

## Baking soda how-to (what it is actually for)

Baking soda paste is a mild abrasive for grease and soap scum. Good Housekeeping: mix baking soda with water to form a paste for soap scum on glass shower doors. USGS: in hard water, soap reacts with calcium to form soap scum. That is not the same as white mineral film.

1. Mix baking soda with a little water to a spreadable paste.
2. Spot-test. Stay off marble, limestone, travertine, and other calcareous stone.
3. Apply to greasy film or soap scum. Soft cloth or non-scratch sponge. Light pressure.
4. Rinse. Dry.
5. If white mineral film remains, that is the vinegar path above — not more paste, and not a vinegar pour on the paste.

Do not claim the paste lifts limescale. It does not.

## Why mixing is a wash

Acid plus bicarbonate makes a salt (sodium acetate), water, and carbon dioxide. Nathan Kilah, Senior Lecturer in Chemistry at the University of Tasmania, writing in The Conversation: the fizz is a visual “placebo”; the reaction forms water, sodium acetate, and CO2; sodium acetate is an even weaker base than baking soda and is not typically a cleaner. A stoichiometric mix leaves you with salty water and gas.

The Conversation also: many DIY recipes dump a large excess of baking soda, so after the fizz, over 99% of the baking soda can remain — leftover bicarbonate plus scrubbing, not a new “volcano” acid. SimplyRecipes, quoting chemist Chem Thug (synthetic organic chemistry) and chemist Zhang: mixing is not different from adding table salt to water; the bubbles are CO2; vinegar weakens baking soda on grease, and baking soda weakens vinegar on mineral deposits. Use them separately. Do not mix as a boost for scale.

The Conversation notes a rare mechanical use of the bubbles in a blocked drain. That is not a limescale method. Do not run a volcano on mineral crust.

## FAQ

**Which wins?**
We did not run a lab. Vinegar is the mineral-film acid. Baking soda is a mild abrasive for scum, not a limescale solvent. Leftover crust is the gray-jug job. Not a ranking.

**Can I mix them?**
No. Acid + bicarbonate → salt + water + CO2. The fizz is gas. Kilah (The Conversation) and SimplyRecipes’ chemists: use them separately.

**Can I use either on marble?**
No. NSI: vinegar and other acids may etch calcareous stone. Wirecutter: stay off baking soda on natural stone. CLR’s FAQ: no natural stone, especially marble including synthetic.

**Baking soda on a glass shower?**
Paste can help grease and soap scum (Good Housekeeping). White mineral film is the vinegar / CLR job. Do not mix them on the pane. We did not run a scratch test. Haze that stays cloudy when wet is etching — acid will not polish glass back (CLR FAQ; same split as vinegar vs CLR).
