## Why acid etches travertine

The crust you want gone is mostly calcium carbonate. The stone is calcium carbonate too. D and G Floors: acidic cleaners — vinegar, lemon, most bathroom sprays — dissolve calcium carbonate on contact, creating dull, rough etch marks that no amount of cleaning can fix. Tucson Tile and Grout: the instinct is a descaler or vinegar; on travertine they dissolve the stone along with the deposit.

Dynamic Stone Tools: white vinegar is acetic acid, pH about 2–3; it etches and permanently damages the surface on contact. Lemon is citric, same damage. CLR is highly acidic and causes severe etching. Commercial bathroom descalers are almost all acids.

Do not use vinegar “just for a second.” D and G Floors: even diluted vinegar etches. Olson: acetic acid instantly dissolves the calcium carbonate binders. The dull patch after acid is a hole in the finish. Cleaning will not fill it.

Never mix leftover vinegar or a rust remover with bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids, and rust-removal products too. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

## Honed vs filled vs unfilled

Honed travertine is matte on purpose. Polished is a mirror. Filled pits are cement or resin. Unfilled pits stay open.

D and G Floors: filled travertine is smoother and easier to clean; unfilled retains pits that trap dirt, grout residue, and moisture — use a soft brush in the holes. Honed hides etch marks better because it is already matte. Polished shows every etch, water spot, and scratch. Dynamic Stone Tools: grit and cleaning pads scratch this stone; honed still etches, it just shows less.

Friction that lifts crust on a polish can leave shiny spots on honed. Light pressure. Stop early. A shiny island in a honed field is finish wear. Stone pro.

On unfilled: paste in the pits, then a soft brush, then distilled. Do not pack dry grit into the voids.

On filled: if filler is missing, dirt and mineral collect in the hole. Clean the crust. Do not grind the fill out.

## Etch vs crust. Cleaning will not polish an etch.

White crust is mineral sitting on sealed stone. A dull patch after acid is a hole in the finish.

OUTERclé: mineral buildup sits on top and can often be removed; etching is a chemical reaction with the stone, a dull or rough area that cleaning products cannot restore. Tucson: if the spots have etched rather than sitting on top, cleaning won’t resolve them; restoring that finish requires professional honing and resealing. Dynamic Stone Tools: when the scale is finally removed, etch marks underneath may be more damaging and more visible than the original scale. Olson: removing an etch requires diamond abrasives to level down to the bottom of the chemical burn.

Do the wet/feel test before you pick a tool.

1. Wet the mark with a drop of distilled water.
2. Feel it with a fingernail.
3. Raised, chalky, catches a nail: crust on top. You can try to lift it.
4. Smooth and dull, same wet and dry: the finish is gone. That is an etch. Stop. Stone pro.

Do not keep scrubbing a smooth dull patch. A cloth will not restore the gloss. More acid will make the hole bigger.
