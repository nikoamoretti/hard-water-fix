## Name it first. Aquarium glass, not windows or shower doors.

The white band sits where the water meets the air. Calcium and magnesium dry there as the level drops and gets topped off. Same minerals as faucet scale. Different room. Different rules.

Identify the surface before you wet it.

- Flat aquarium panel, glass or acrylic, white chalky ring at the old waterline: this page.
- House window with outdoor spray and rain: [windows](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-windows). Different job.
- Shower door with soap scum and steam: [shower doors](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-glass-shower-doors). Different room.
- Bathroom mirror fog haze: [bathroom mirror](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-bathroom-mirror). Not a tank.

If fish are still in the water, do not flood the interior with vinegar. Exterior waterline wipe is fine only if nothing drips in.

## Why vinegar belongs on empty glass, not in the water column

The crust is mostly calcium carbonate. Pantry white vinegar is acetic acid. On empty, fish-out glass it softens the ring. In the water column it crashes pH and stresses livestock. CLR is a stronger acid product built for household descaling. Not for aquarium water. Not for a half-full tank “just this once.”

Algae is not scale. Green slime scrapes soft. White chalk catches a fingernail. Name which one you have before you soak.

Never mix leftover vinegar with bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

## Fish out. Vinegar 1:1. 5–15 minutes. Exhaustive rinse.

Interior waterline. Tank empty of livestock. Decor out or wiped separately. Pantry vinegar. No Amazon link for the vinegar.

1. Move fish and inverts to a holding tub with tank water. Power off heaters and filters as your setup needs.
2. Drain or lower water so the ring is dry and reachable. Wipe loose grit with a soft cloth first.
3. Mix white vinegar and water 1:1. Apply to the stained glass only. Paper towel compress or spray on empty glass. Not a pour into standing tank water with fish in it.
4. Sit 5 to 15 minutes. Keep the patch wet. Do not let acid dry into a new film.
5. Plastic scraper on acrylic. Aquarium glass blade on glass only — stop about 1/4 inch from silicone seams. Never dig a razor into silicone corners.
6. Rinse hard. Multiple passes of clean water until the vinegar smell is gone from the glass. Exhaustive rinse is the safety step. Residual acid belongs nowhere near livestock.
7. Final wipe with distilled water. Dry the glass above the new fill line.

Grocery distilled water, one gallon, is the final-wipe and top-off class. Tap wipe puts the same calcium and magnesium back on the glass.

If one 15-minute pass lifts the chalk and the panel looks clear wet, stop. Repeat only on remaining raised crust after another rinse.

## Exterior waterline with fish still in

Outside of the glass only. Soft cloth. Vinegar 1:1 or plain distilled wipe. Nothing drips over the rim into the water. Angle the cloth down and away. If a drop falls in, stop and do a partial water change with your normal conditioned water.

This is a cosmetic outside wipe. It is not a substitute for emptying the tank when the interior ring is thick.

## Glass blade vs plastic scraper. Acrylic rules.

Glass aquariums can take an aquarium scraper blade on the flat panel. Keep the edge off the silicone. Stop about 1/4 inch from every seam. Digging into silicone opens leaks and traps grit.

Acrylic scratches. Plastic scraper only. No razor. No steel wool. No kitchen knife. Work almost flat. Lift crust, do not gouge the plastic.

Raised chalk that survives vinegar: damp plastic scraper first. Then another short vinegar compress. Then rinse. Then distilled wipe.

## Distilled top-off slows the ring. It does not sanitize.

Evaporation leaves minerals behind. Topping off with tap rebuilds the waterline crust. Topping off with distilled water adds volume without adding calcium and magnesium. That slows the ring. It does not kill algae. It does not replace a water change. It does not replace cycle chemistry.

Keep regular water changes on your normal schedule. Distilled is for evaporation top-off and the final glass wipe after vinegar, not a free pass on tank hygiene.

## FAQ

**Will vinegar remove hard water stains from a fish tank?**

Yes on empty, fish-out glass. Mix 1:1 with water. 5 to 15 minutes. Exhaustive rinse. Distilled final wipe. Never pour vinegar into an inhabited water column.

**Can I use CLR in my aquarium?**

No. Not in the water. Not as a “quick soak” with fish present. CLR is a household descaler. Keep it out of the tank.

**Is the white ring algae?**

Often no. Algae is green or brown film. Hard-water scale is chalky white mineral at the waterline. USGS hardness numbers describe dissolved calcium and magnesium that dry as that crust. Scrape soft film as algae. Soak chalk as scale.

**Can I clean the outside with fish still in the tank?**

Yes if nothing drips in. Exterior wipe only. Angle the cloth away from the rim. Interior acid work needs fish out.

**Glass or acrylic — which scraper?**

Glass: aquarium blade on the flat panel, stop 1/4 inch from silicone. Acrylic: plastic scraper only. No razor on acrylic.

**Does distilled water remove the existing ring?**

No. Distilled is the final wipe and the evaporation top-off. Vinegar on empty glass removes the crust. Distilled slows the next ring. It does not sanitize.

**Can I use Rain-X or cerium oxide on the tank?**

No. Rain-X and 3M-style cerium polish are not aquarium waterline methods. Stay with vinegar on empty glass and a distilled wipe.

**Can I mix vinegar and bleach to get scale and algae?**

No. Poison Control: bleach plus an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Leave the area. Fresh air. **1-800-222-1222**.
