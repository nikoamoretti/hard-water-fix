## What that film is

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heat those minerals and they drop out as calcium carbonate. Soap plus calcium is scum. Rinse the basin and walk away, and leftover water evaporates. The Ca and Mg stay as the white film. That is the stain this page is for.

Ruvati and Elkay want the same daily habit: wash, rinse, **dry with a soft cloth**. Ruvati: drying after use is how you stop limescale. Skip the towel and USGS minerals redeposit before dinner.

## How to remove the film (1-minute clock)

Follow **your** label. Live Soft Cleanser clock plus sink-maker grain rules.

1. Clear the basin. Pull the grid if you use one (Ruvati and Elkay: a grid protects the floor). Wash food off with mild dish soap first. Scum shields mineral.
2. Find the grain. Those fine parallel lines are the finish. Ruvati: scrub in the same direction as the natural grain. Elkay: rub with the grain lines, then dry completely. Cross-grain strokes haze the finish.
3. Gloves if skin is sensitive. BKF: oxalic stings an open cut. Ventilate. Do not mix this cream with bleach or ammonia. Washington State DOH: bleach plus an acid gives off **chlorine gas**. BKF Soft says the same.
4. Wet the stained steel. Shake [Bar Keepers Friend Soft Cleanser](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00B28ZYPU?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). Squeeze a small amount onto the wet basin or a damp sponge. Thin coat. Use less than you think.
5. Rub with a damp sponge **with the grain**. Walls, floor, drain, the back ledge where water sits. Corners: a soft toothbrush, still with the grain.
6. **Rinse thoroughly within one minute.** Start the clock when the cream hits the steel. Work a section if the bowl is large. Rinse until the cleanser film is gone. BKF: leftover abrasive can haze if you skip the rinse.
7. **Wipe dry** with a soft cloth, with the grain. This keeps USGS minerals from drying back on.
8. Repeat a **new** one-minute pass on remaining crust. Same wet coat, rinse, dry. Do not leave cream sitting while you do dishes.

Stay off polished stone. BKF Soft: no marble or granite. Natural Stone Institute: calcareous stone is calcium carbonate; acids etch it. Cultured marble and stone vanity bowls: one-line stay-off, not a how-to. Mild soap only. Stone cousin: [hard water stains from a bathtub](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-bathtub).

## What not to do

- Do not scrub **against** the grain. Ruvati and Elkay both say with the grain.
- Do not leave [Bar Keepers Friend Soft Cleanser](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00B28ZYPU?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) on as a soak. Oxalic is a timed rub. BKF: **one minute**, then rinse. Metal can discolor past that.
- Do not reach for powder here. BKF: powder has more abrasive. Soft is the pre-diluted bottle. Powder vs liquid: [Bar Keepers Friend vs CLR](/articles/bar-keepers-friend-vs-clr).
- Do not use steel wool or a soap pad. Ruvati: steel wool can damage and discolor. Elkay: particles from steel wool or wire brushes adhere and rust. Elkay's pad, if you need one, is a blue no-scratch Scotch-Brite.
- Do not bleach the basin and walk away. Ruvati: bleach left on too long can pit stainless. Elkay: no chlorides; rinse immediately; do not store open chloride cleaners under the sink (vapors pit). British Stainless Steel Association: household bleach spills on 304-type sinks can pit overnight; rinse now.
- Do not mix bleach with this cream or any other acid (Washington State DOH: chlorine gas).
- Do not put this acid on marble, granite, or a cultured-marble vanity. Natural Stone Institute: acids etch calcareous stone.
- Do not skip the dry. Evaporation puts Ca and Mg back as spots the same day.

## FAQ

**Why does the grain matter?**
It is the brushed finish. Ruvati and Elkay: scrub in that direction. Cross-grain scratches catch light and look like haze.

**Can I use Bar Keepers Friend powder in the sink?**
Not as this page's default. BKF FAQ: they are not identical; powder has more abrasive. Soft Cleanser is the ready-to-use cling for routine mineral on stainless. Powder lives on [Bar Keepers Friend vs CLR](/articles/bar-keepers-friend-vs-clr). Same **one-minute** rinse if you already own powder.

**Can I just use vinegar?**
Vinegar is acetic acid. USGS notes vinegar can dissolve mineral particles. Elkay's home-remedy mix is 50/50, with the grain, rinse, dry, no soak. It is a pantry fallback, not the labeled stainless cling. See [vinegar vs CLR](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains) for the acid chemistry, not as the sink hero.

**Why do the spots return the same afternoon?**
You rinsed and did not dry. USGS minerals stay when the water evaporates. Ruvati and Elkay both put the towel in the care sheet. Recurring film also means hardness is still in the tap. Test it. Do not buy a third cleanser.

**Can I use this on a stone or cultured-marble bathroom sink?**
No. One line: stay off. Natural Stone Institute: acids etch calcareous stone. BKF Soft: no marble or granite. Cultured marble is a resin bowl, not stainless. Mild soap only. Tub-side stone stay-off: [hard water stains from a bathtub](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-bathtub).
