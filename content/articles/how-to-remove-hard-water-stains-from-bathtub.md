## Identify the tub first

A refrigerator magnet is the split. It sticks to porcelain enamel on steel or cast iron. It does not stick to acrylic, fiberglass, cultured marble, or natural stone.

Tap the wall. A hollow plastic knock is acrylic or fiberglass (fiberglass flexes more). A dull thud is cast iron. A metallic ring is enameled steel. A chip that shows dark metal is enamel. Color-through plastic or a peeling painted film is not original enamel. If you cannot name it, treat it as acrylic: no powder, no toilet gel, spot-test, soft cloth only.

## What that film is

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Soap plus calcium is soap scum. Heat those minerals and they drop out as calcium carbonate, the white or tan crust on the waterline and the floor of the tub. That is the stain this page is for. Orange or brown is iron, not hardness. Glass shower panes and grout are not this URL. Glass is [how to remove hard water stains from glass shower doors](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-glass-shower-doors). Grout stays off this page.

## 1. Acrylic and fiberglass: Brilliant Bath, not the gray bottle

[CLR Brilliant Bath](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DQFN65NQ?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) is the tub-labeled foam, not household Calcium, Lime & Rust in the gray jug. Live CLR Brands copy lists porcelain, ceramic tile, shower doors, sinks, **bathtubs**, white grout and caulk, toilet bowls, and **fiberglass**. It does **not** name acrylic. Still use this spray: the label lists bathtubs and fiberglass. Spot-test a hidden patch. Kohler acrylic care: soft sponge or cloth, no scouring pad, rinse immediately, do not let cleaner soak.

SDS and the ingredient panel: lactic acid (about 5 to 10 percent), ready-to-use, no dilution. pH about 2.4 to 2.7. It is not the lactic-plus-gluconic 50/50 mix in household CLR. Brilliant Bath will not remove rust.

Follow **your** label. CLR's published method:

1. Open a window or run the fan. Rubber gloves. Do not mix with bleach or other cleaners. Washington State DOH: bleach plus an acid gives off chlorine gas.
2. Prime the sprayer: nozzle ON, pull the trigger all the way back 3 to 5 times.
3. Spot-test. Stay-off list is on the CLR page: vinyl, natural stone, synthetic marble, mirrors, laminates, wood, painted or sealed surfaces, colored grout.
4. Spray **6 to 8 inches** from the wet film.
5. Let the foam work. Wipe with a damp sponge or cloth. **Never leave it on more than two or three minutes.**
6. Rinse promptly and thoroughly with **cold, clean water**. Repeat a fresh 2 to 3 minute pass if crust remains. Do not extend the clock.

Household CLR (lactic and gluconic, equal parts warm water, two minutes, cold rinse) does list fiberglass. It is still the wrong default on a plastic tub. Older sinks and tubs may etch. Do not put [Bar Keepers Friend Soft Cleanser](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00B28ZYPU?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) on acrylic or fiberglass. Oxalic plus citric plus a glass-oxide abrasive. BKF says test first on fiberglass and plastics. This page stays off those shells. Powder is coarser grit. That split is on [Bar Keepers Friend vs CLR](/articles/bar-keepers-friend-vs-clr).

## 2. Porcelain enamel: Brilliant Bath, then Soft Cleanser on intact glaze

Intact porcelain enamel can take the same [CLR Brilliant Bath](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DQFN65NQ?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) pass. Porcelain is on the use list. Kohler enamel care: test first, rinse immediately, do not soak, no steel wool or scouring pads. One Kohler enameled-cast-iron sheet wants commercial cleansers in pH 3 to 8 and no strong acids. Brilliant Bath SDS is pH 2.4 to 2.7, just under that window. Keep the **2 to 3 minute** cap, rinse cold, and skip any chip that shows metal. A chip is exposed metal, not glaze.

For stubborn mineral on **intact** glaze, [Bar Keepers Friend Soft Cleanser](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00B28ZYPU?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) is the porcelain cling. Official ingredients: oxalic acid, citric acid, glass oxide abrasive. Porcelain, ceramic, and bathtubs are listed. Stay off marble and granite. Official method:

1. Shake well. Wet the stain.
2. Squeeze a small amount onto the wet surface or a damp sponge. Use less than you think.
3. Rub with a damp sponge.
4. **Rinse thoroughly within one minute.** Wipe dry. Repeat a fresh one-minute pass if needed. Do not let it dry on the glaze.

## What not to do

- Do not skip the material ID. Acrylic is not porcelain.
- Do not put household CLR or toilet-bowl acid gel on a plastic tub. Toilet acid stays on the [toilet-bowl page](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-toilet-bowl).
- Do not use [Bar Keepers Friend Soft Cleanser](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00B28ZYPU?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) or BKF powder on acrylic or fiberglass.
- Do not leave Brilliant Bath past two or three minutes, or Soft Cleanser past one minute. Do not mix either with bleach (Washington State DOH: chlorine gas).
- Do not acid marble, granite, travertine, cultured marble, or a refinished coating. Natural Stone Institute: bathroom acids etch calcareous stone. Brilliant Bath FAQ: no marble, no synthetic marble. Mild soap only.

## FAQ

**How do I tell acrylic from porcelain without a label?**
Magnet first. It sticks to enamel on steel or cast iron. It does not stick to acrylic or fiberglass. Tap and flex next. A chip that shows dark metal is enamel.

**Can I use regular CLR on the tub?**
Not as the default. Household Calcium, Lime & Rust is lactic and gluconic, 50/50, two minutes. It lists fiberglass. [CLR Brilliant Bath](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DQFN65NQ?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) is the tub spray. Older fixtures may etch. Spot-test.

**Can I use Bar Keepers Friend on an acrylic tub?**
Not on this page. Soft Cleanser is oxalic plus abrasive. Porcelain only. Powder is the wrong grit.

**What about a stone or refinished tub?**
No acid. Natural Stone Institute: mild detergent or stone soap. Cultured marble is synthetic marble on the Brilliant Bath FAQ. A reglaze is a coating. Mild soap.

**Why does the ring come back after I clean?**
Hardness is still in the tap. Cleaners remove the film, not the minerals. Test hardness. Do not buy a third tub gel.
