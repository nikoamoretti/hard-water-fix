A weak shower is usually not a dying pipe. It is calcium and magnesium dried inside the nozzles. Distilled white vinegar (about 5% acetic acid) dissolves that scale. The bag-and-rubber-band method does it without a wrench.

The part most “overnight vinegar” posts skip: finish and time. Chrome will take a soak that will dull matte black or oil-rubbed bronze. Check the table, set a timer, stop if the color changes.

## Do this before the bag

If the face has rubber or silicone jets (Delta calls these Touch-Clean), wipe them with a finger first. Delta’s own care page: wipe the spray holes to break deposits loose. Wirecutter’s Tim Heffernan cleared his father’s hard-water heads in minutes by pulling the nozzles sideways and letting them snap back. No acid, no bag.

If pressure comes back, you are done. If it still dribbles, or the jets are hard plastic, soak.

Skip bleach, toilet cleaner, rust remover, and Magic Eraser. Kohler’s Tom Sindelar told Wirecutter those scratch or attack the finish. CLR is a lime remover with a **2-minute** label. Save it for a *removed* chrome or plastic head that vinegar already failed, not for a bag hanging on matte black.

## Finish table (this is the gap)

Delta says a 50/50 white vinegar and water soak will not harm *their* finishes, and their how-to soaks 30 minutes, then scrubs, then repeats. Moen allows a 50/50 vinegar wipe for water spots on most lines, rinsed off immediately, and tells you to avoid lime-scale removers on several finishes (including matte black and oil-rubbed bronze powder coats). Those two cards disagree on “leave it all night.” We follow the shorter clock and the brand of the head on your wall.

| Finish | Vinegar mix | First soak | Stop if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chrome or plastic | 50/50 vinegar + water | 30 minutes, then check (Delta) | Finish dulls, plastic clouds, gaskets swell |
| Brushed / polished nickel (LifeShine-type) | 50/50 | 15–30 minutes, rinse immediately (Moen) | Color shift, dark streaks |
| Matte black, oil-rubbed bronze, other powder coats | 50/50 only if the brand allows vinegar at all | 10–15 minutes, then rinse. Prefer finger-wipe + [citric] at the short chrome time, or skip acid. | Any fade to gray or bronze showing through |
| Brass, gold, unknown | 50/50 | 10–15 minutes, look, stop | You cannot name the finish |

Overnight soaking is Wirecutter’s method for a **removed** chrome or plastic head in a bowl. It is not the default for a bag on the wall, and it is a bad idea on specialty finishes. Repeat a timed pass. Do not extend the clock.

## The bag method (no tools)

1. Slide a thick rubber band or a zip tie onto the shower arm *first*. Wirecutter is right about this: you cannot hold a bag of liquid and fiddle a fastener at the same time.
2. Pour **equal parts distilled white vinegar and water** into a gallon zip bag. One cup of each is enough for a small head. Add more until the nozzle face will sit fully under the liquid.
3. Lift the bag over the head so every jet is submerged. Cinch the fastener around the arm, not the decorative flange. Keep acid off stone tile and off oil-rubbed or matte trims around the arm.
4. Set a timer from the table. Do not walk away for “until morning” unless you unscrewed a chrome/plastic head into a bowl.
5. Pull the bag down into the tub, dump the vinegar, run hot water 30–60 seconds.
6. Soft toothbrush on the face. Fingernail on rubber jets. Toothpick only on hard plastic holes, and do not gouge them wider.
7. Still clogged? Repeat **one** more timed soak. Then stop. Two failures is a replacement, not a third acid bath.

Handheld heads are easier: unclip, bag in the caddy or a bowl, same mix and time.

## If you can unscrew it

Cloth on the wrench so you do not chew the finish. Counterclockwise at the coupling. Peek at the inlet screen. Wirecutter: large debris comes out with needle-nose pliers; a toothpick on the screen, carefully, so you do not open the mesh. Soak the head face-up in 50/50 vinegar in a bowl so the solution runs inside. Chrome/plastic can sit longer (Wirecutter: several hours to overnight). Nickel and powder coats still get the short clock. Rinse, new plumber’s tape on the arm threads, hand-tight plus a snug turn.

## When to stop and replace the head

Replace, do not keep soaking, if:

- Two timed soaks later, several jets are still dead
- The silicone nozzles are torn or will not snap back
- The finish changed color during the soak
- The plastic body is cracked, or the head leaks at the coupling after you re-tape it

## What not to do

- Do not overnight-bag a matte black, bronze, or unknown finish.
- Do not mix vinegar, citric acid, or CLR with bleach. Acid plus bleach makes chlorine gas (Washington State DOH).
- Do not use a green scouring pad or Magic Eraser on the chrome. Wirecutter scratched a chrome-look plastic head in a few swipes.
- Do not soak natural stone surrounds. Towel them off.
- Do not punch rubber jets with a metal pin. You will split them.

## FAQ

**How long should a shower head soak in vinegar?**
Delta: 30 minutes in 50/50, then check and repeat. Wirecutter: hours to overnight, on a *removed* head. We use Delta’s 30 minutes on the wall and only go long on chrome or plastic in a bowl.

**Will vinegar damage a shower head?**
Not chrome or plastic on a timed 50/50 soak, per Delta. It can dull powder-coat and specialty finishes if you leave it. Rinse, dry, stop at color change.

**Vinegar or citric acid?**
Vinegar is already in the pantry. Citric is faster and does not stink. Same mineral chemistry, shorter contact, one bag of powder covering this job and the kettle.

**Why is my shower still weak after cleaning?**
Inlet screen packed with debris, a failing flow restrictor, or nozzles that are physically shot. Clean the screen. Then replace the head.
