## Name the stain. Then name the caddy.

White or tan chalk is calcium plus soap. USGS: vinegar is an acid; the acidity helps dissolve mineral so water can carry it away. Weekand: soap works into crevices, especially on the shelves and racks, and hardens into a thick layer. Theglossylocks: white haze is mineral buildup hiding in plain sight.

Orange streaks are rust. Theglossylocks rust post: that orange streak along the shelf is the caddy losing the battle. Calcium and magnesium deposits from hard water bond to metal and act as rust-nucleation sites. Weekand: remove rust spots with a commercial rust remover. Do not merge orange with the white chalk soak.

Black specks or pink slime are mold. Bleach can be a mold path. Bleach does nothing to calcium. Do not bleach a mineral job. Never mix leftover bleach with vinegar. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids, and rust-removal products too. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

Plastic or PEVA: vinegar soak. No CLR. Chrome or stainless: vinegar first, CLR only if crust remains. Cheap plated steel that is already rusting through: two failed soaks, then replace it. Do not invent a replacement ASIN.

## Vinegar soak. Plastic and metal.

Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Pantry. No Amazon link.

Empty the caddy. Take it down if it comes off the shower arm or the suction cups. Tension-pole shelves that snap off go in the soak. The pole and the spring stay out. That clock is later.

Weekand: fill the sink or tub enough to submerge the caddy. Mix up to 1 1/2 cups of white vinegar into the water, or about **1/2 cup per gallon**. Soak **15 to 20 minutes**, or until the soap scum and dirt begins to soften. Scrub with a brush. Rinse with clear water. House Digest uses the same 1/2 cup per gallon and a **20-minute** bath. Theglossylocks metal path: mix 2–3 cups of white vinegar with water and sit about **20 minutes**.

This how-to stays on that 15–20 minute vinegar soak. Repeat a fresh soak if chalk remains. Do not turn it into an overnight acid bath as the default. Do not pour baking soda into the tub and call the fizz a descale.

1. Distilled white vinegar. Gloves. Open a window or run the fan.
2. Weekand’s **1/2 cup per gallon** in enough water to cover. Plastic and metal both take this soak. Theglossylocks: acetic acid loosens mineral without a harsh-chemical soak.
3. Sit **15 to 20 minutes**. Re-wet any part that sits above the line.
4. Soft brush or old toothbrush in the joints. Rinse until it does not smell like salad.
5. Wipe dry with a clean towel before it goes back. Weekand: wipe dry so it doesn’t develop fresh spots. House Digest: dry thoroughly before returning it to the shower to prevent mildew growth and hard water stains. Theglossylocks: leftover moisture is what invites mold, mildew, and rust back in.

If someone already used bleach on this caddy, rinse with plain water until it doesn’t smell like bleach. Then start. Never mix.

House Digest’s leftover-stain step is a baking soda paste rinsed with vinegar until it fizzes. Weekand pours up to 1 cup of vinegar over a baking soda paste for the same bubble. That fizz is not extra scale power. It is CO2 leaving. Do not mix vinegar and baking soda as the play.
