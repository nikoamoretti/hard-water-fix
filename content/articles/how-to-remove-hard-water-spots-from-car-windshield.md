## Wet test before you spray

Park in shade. Glass cool. Chemical Guys: wash the glass first so you are looking at mineral, not road film. Dry it. Then do this on a patch in daylight.

1. Run a fingertip across the haze.
2. Dampen that same patch with water. Look again.

Light rings that feel smooth and vanish while wet are film. Heavier spots feel slightly rough. If the haze is identical wet and dry, you are already into the glass. Chemical Guys: mineral deposits bond to raw glass; the longer they bake in heat and UV, the harder the bond. Spots left for weeks begin etching.

## 50/50 vinegar on cool glass first

Faint white rings you can barely feel. Distilled white vinegar is about 5% acetic acid. The Conversation (Nathan Kilah): vinegar reacts with and dissolves limescale. AutoZone: equal parts white vinegar and water on hard-water spots; sit a few minutes; rinse. AutoZone: household vinegar mixed 50/50 with pure water is often successful when the spots are hard-water minerals.

Cool glass only. Hot glass dries the mix into a new ring. Keep acid off paint, rubber, plastic trim, and aftermarket tint.

1. Park in shade. Touch the glass. If it is warm, wait.
2. Wash loose dirt. Chemical Guys: road film blocks whatever you spray next. Dry.
3. Mix equal parts distilled white vinegar and distilled water in a spray bottle. Distilled rinse matters. Tap puts the same calcium and magnesium back on the pane.
4. Spray the *glass* only. Or wet a microfiber and park it on the spots so runoff does not hit paint or trim.
5. Sit a few minutes (AutoZone). Re-spray if it starts to dry. Do not leave it in the sun.
6. Wipe with microfiber. Rinse with distilled water. Buff dry.
7. Wet the patch again. Judge it wet.

If two wet passes lift the rings and the glass feels slick when wet, stop. You had film. Skip cerium.

If the glass is still rough or hazy **when wet**, vinegar is done. That is etch, not a longer soak.

## What not to do

- Do not skip the wet test. Haze that remains when wet is etch. More vinegar will not fill pits.
- Do not spray vinegar on hot glass or in direct sun. It dries into a new ring.
- Do not run household CLR, citric acid, or Lime-A-Way on a car. CLR stays off paint, tint, and trim. This page does not sell CLR for cars.
