## Vinegar wrap the nozzle first

Food-contact path is vinegar. Distilled white vinegar, about 5% acetic acid. The Conversation (Nathan Kilah): vinegar reacts with and dissolves mineral deposits like limescale. Use it alone. Do not mix it with baking soda. The fizz is carbon dioxide, not extra scale power.

1. Wipe the paddle dry so the wrap sticks.
2. Soak a paper towel in distilled white vinegar. Squeeze until it is wet, not dripping.
3. Wrap it around the spout. Rubber-band it so it stays on the chrome or plastic.
4. Leave it 30–60 minutes. Lemons, Lavender & Laundry: that is the dwell that loosens crust on a fridge spout.
5. Pull the towel. Wipe the loosened chalk with a cloth.
6. Run a straw brush up the nozzle. Short strokes. Last inch of the paddle, not the tubing behind the door.
7. Dispense water until a glass does not taste like a pickle.

Repeat the wrap once if the crust is still raised under a fingernail. A toothbrush on the collar is fine. A metal pick in the bore is not.

## Soak the drip tray

The tray is where splash dries into the same white crust.

1. Lift or snap the tray out. If it does not come out, do not pry the housing.
2. Set it level in the sink, not on natural stone. Vinegar etches marble and limestone.
3. Pour distilled white vinegar into the wells until the chalky rings are covered. 30–60 minutes.
4. Dump. Wipe remaining film with a cloth. Nylon on the grid is fine. Steel wool is not.
5. Last rinse is [distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). Tap paints minerals back onto a wet tray as it air-dries.
6. Dry, then seat it.

## Distilled rinse so you do not paint it back on

Tap is how the crust got there. [Distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) has no calcium or magnesium to dry as a new ring. Wipe the paddle with a distilled-damp cloth after the wrap. Rinse the tray with distilled. Do not finish with a tap splash.

## The fridge filter is not a softener

The cartridge behind the grille is a carbon fridge filter. CDC: pitcher filters and filters built into refrigerators often use activated carbon. Their main job is taste and smell, not hardness. NSF/ANSI 42 is chlorine, taste, and odor. NSF/ANSI 53 covers listed health effects such as lead or cysts — still not calcium and magnesium.

A [fridge water filter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00UB38V2A?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) is that class. Replace it on the booklet schedule for taste. Do not expect the crust on the spout to stop. Hardness minerals pass through carbon. A [shower filter](/articles/best-shower-filter-for-hard-water) is not a softener either. Softening is ion exchange or reverse osmosis, not this carbon brick.

## What not to do

- Do not treat white crust as mold. Scale is mineral. Vinegar wrap first.
- Do not unscrew this like a [faucet aerator](/articles/how-to-clean-a-clogged-faucet-aerator). There is no sink screen on this paddle.
- Do not pour CLR, Lime-A-Way, or citric acid into the drinking line.

- Do not skip the distilled rinse on the tray. Tap paints the minerals back on.
- Do not treat the fridge carbon filter as a softener. It is not.

- Do not leave CLR on chrome longer than two minutes.

## FAQ

**Is the white crust on the fridge spout mold?**
Usually no. It is scale: calcium and magnesium dried on the nozzle. USGS names those as hardness. This page is mineral crust on the paddle and tray.

**How long does the vinegar wrap take?**
30–60 minutes on the spout, rubber band so it stays put, then a straw brush. Same window on the tray wells.

**Can I put CLR in the dispenser line?**
No. Never. Exterior chrome or plastic crust only, two minutes, then a cold rinse. Food-contact path is vinegar. Rinse until the water does not taste like a pickle.

**Does replacing the fridge filter stop hard water stains?**
No. A [fridge water filter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00UB38V2A?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) is carbon, not a softener. CDC: taste and smell. NSF 42 is not hardness. The crust still needs the wrap.

**Is this the same as cleaning a faucet aerator?**
No. An aerator is a mesh you unscrew at the sink. This is a fridge paddle and a drip tray.

**What about the ice maker?**
Leave it. Different job. Do not pour vinegar or CLR into the ice bin from this page.

**How hard is hard?**
USGS: 0–60 mg/L as CaCO3 is soft. 61–120 moderately hard. 121–180 hard. Above 180 very hard. [Test at home](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home) if you want the number.
