## Name the part. Then stop stealing other URLs.

Body sprays sit in the wall, usually in a vertical row on a shower panel or as separate round jets. They shoot sideways at the body. ## Rub the silicone nozzles under low flow first

Kohler’s body-spray install guidance is the order: rub the nozzles while water is running. Do not blast the sprayface with a pressure washer, a hard brush, or a pin until you have rubbed. Soft silicone teats flex. Rubbing under low flow pops the mineral out of the openings without chewing the rubber.

1. Turn the body spray on low. Warm is fine. Not a full blast.
2. Rub each silicone nozzle with a fingertip or a soft cloth. Work around the ring. Feel for hard white rings at the tips.
3. If a teat is stuck closed, keep rubbing under flow. Do not stab it with a metal pin as the first move.
4. Shut off. Wipe the face dry with a soft cloth so wet mineral does not re-spot as it evaporates.

Aqualisa-style rub-clean teats follow the same idea: mechanical flex first, chemistry second. Many clogged body jets clear on this pass alone. Skip straight to acid and you skip the step the install sheet put first.

Do this after every shower for a week if the spray is only half dead. Fresh scale is soft. Week-old rings need more rubs. Month-old crust may need a removed-part soak the booklet allows — not a bag on the wall.

**Do not overnight-soak rubber teats in acid while the jet is still on the wall.** Rubber and long acid sits do not mix. Rub first.

**Read the sheet for your brand before any soak.**

- **Kohler** handspray and body-spray style care often allows a warm vinegar soak of a **removed screen washer** or a removed component the booklet names. If your PDF says soak the screen in warm vinegar, do that — screen in a cup, not a bag taped to the wall.
- **Hansgrohe** limescale / care guidance: citric paths, **no acetic acid** on the finish. Do **not** bag a Hansgrohe. Do not vinegar-wipe a Hansgrohe finish because a generic blog said vinegar is fine. Quote the sheet. Stay off acetic if the sheet bans it.
- Other Euro PVD / special finishes: many ban vinegar and HCl on the visible finish. A jet that will not unscrew is **not** a vinegar bag on the tile.

If the booklet allows a removed chrome or plastic jet / screen:

1. Shut off the shower supplies if the sheet says to. Cover the drain so a dropped washer does not vanish.
2. Unscrew only what the booklet shows. Photograph orientation before you pull a screen.
3. Soak the **removed** part in pantry distilled white vinegar (or warm vinegar if the sheet says warm) long enough for the crust to soften — typically 20–30 minutes for a screen; follow the booklet if it prints a clock.
4. Soft brush or old toothbrush on the screen and the back of the face. No steel wool. No scouring powder on a finish.
5. Rinse. Pantry distilled rinse if you want zero redeposit on the metal part. Reinstall hand-tight per the sheet. Run the jet and rub the nozzles again under low flow.

## Keep it from packing again

After a clean pass, rub the nozzles under low flow once a week if your tap is hard. Wipe the face dry when you leave the shower so wet mineral does not re-spot. A weekly fingertip rub beats a quarterly acid soak on silicone teats.

If the panel sheet allows a removable screen, rinse that screen when you deep-clean the rest of the shower. Do not wait until every jet is a single needle stream.

A stuck-in-wall jet that never unscrews stays on rub-clean only. More chemistry on a finish you cannot rinse off the wall is how PVD and matte black get ruined.
