## Why terry dies

Cotton terry is thousands of loops. Fine Linen and Bath: those loops are what you want plush and absorbent. Laundry with Olivia: detergent residue accumulates inside and between the loops and makes them stiff. That happens when you overdose, or when the load never rinses clean.

Hard water makes that worse. USGS: in hard water, soap reacts with calcium to form soap scum, and more detergent is needed to get laundry clean. Water Quality Hub: calcium and magnesium bond with leftover soap and sit between the cotton loops, so the pile stops flexing and wicking. Thirsty Towels: as moisture evaporates in the dryer, those minerals harden and crystallize in the fiber.

Fabric softener is the coating, not the fix. Consumer Reports laundry tester Rich Handel: fabric softener can leave a layer of residue on clothes and **reduce the absorption of your towels**. Fine Linen and Bath: never use fabric softener or dryer sheets on bath towels; they leave a waxy coating that diminishes absorbency and softness. Thirsty Towels: commercial softeners coat fibers in silicone and petroleum-based oils and essentially waterproof the loops — a tiny raincoat on every loop. Cotton Incorporated (technical bulletin ISP 1012): cotton is polar and hydrophilic, which is why it is useful for absorbent towels; most softeners used in the finish impart **poor wicking properties**. The mill note is finishing chemistry. The same trade-off shows up in the wash: a waxy film feels “soft” and drinks less water.

Skip fabric softener on terry. Skip dryer sheets too. They are the same coating.

## One-time strip: hot vinegar wash, then a separate baking-soda wash

Do not dump detergent into this. Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Pantry. No Amazon link. Baking soda is pantry too.

Laundry with Olivia, Thirsty Towels, Fine Linen and Bath, and Water Quality Hub all run the same two-cycle strip: vinegar first, baking soda second, **never the same cycle**. Why not one volcano load: acid plus bicarbonate makes salt, water, and CO2. The fizz is gas leaving, not extra power. That chemistry is already on [baking soda vs vinegar](/articles/baking-soda-vs-vinegar-hard-water-stains). Do not retread it here.

**Cycle 1 — vinegar, no detergent**

1. Towels only. Do not pack the drum. Fine Linen and Bath: heavy towels need room to rinse.
2. Pour **1 cup** distilled white vinegar into the drum (top-load) or the detergent/softener dispenser (front-load). Laundry with Olivia, Thirsty Towels, Fine Linen and Bath, Water Quality Hub: one cup. No detergent. No bleach. No Calgon in this empty-of-soap cycle.
3. Hot water if the care label allows it. Laundry with Olivia: hot is safe for white cotton; use warm for colored towels. Fine Linen and Bath’s restore pass is a warm vinegar wash. Follow **your** label.
4. Run the full cycle. USGS: vinegar is an acid; the acidity helps dissolve mineral so water can carry it away. Laundry with Olivia: acetic acid dissolves mineral deposits and breaks down detergent and softener buildup.

Empty. Leave the wet towels in the machine. Do not pour baking soda on top of leftover vinegar in the same water.

**Cycle 2 — baking soda, still no detergent**

1. Sprinkle **½ cup** baking soda over the wet towels. Laundry with Olivia, Thirsty Towels, Fine Linen and Bath, Water Quality Hub: half a cup. Directly in the drum. No detergent.
2. Run another hot (or warm, if that is the color-safe setting) normal cycle.
3. Inspect wet. If the pile still feels waxy after years of Downy, Laundry with Olivia: you may need two or three rounds over successive washes. Do not invent a 12-hour tub soak on this page.

Never mix vinegar and bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

No citric on this page. No Lime-A-Way. No Iron Out unless the towel is orange rust — that is [clothes stains](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-clothes), not this pile. Not bath beads. Not Calgonite.

## Dry: skip high heat if minerals remain

Heat-dry on high bakes leftover minerals in. Thirsty Towels: as moisture evaporates, minerals harden and crystallize in the fabric; the highest heat setting scorches cotton and flattens loops. Water Quality Hub: over-drying on high heat locks mineral deposits into the fibers and makes stiffness worse. Laundry with Olivia: high heat stiffens fibers; use medium; do not overdry.

1. Inspect wet after the strip. If the pile is still chalky, air-dry or low heat. Do not cook the crust in.
2. Fine Linen and Bath: tumble dry on low or medium. Shake the towels as they leave the washer so the loops open. Never iron terry — heat flattens the pile.
3. Thirsty Towels and Fine Linen and Bath: wool dryer balls beat the loops open without a waxy sheet. That is mechanical loft, not a coating.
4. Pull them while slightly damp if the dryer wants to bake them. Finish on a rack.

Once the strip has actually cleared the loops, medium heat is fine. High heat is the habit that locks the next film in.
