## Vinyl liner vs fabric curtain

They do not take the same wash.

The **vinyl or PEVA liner** is the inner splash sheet. Plastic. It takes the spray. Better Homes & Gardens: liners protect the bathroom from water; the outer curtain often is not waterproof.

The **fabric curtain** is the outer decorative panel. Often polyester. Sometimes cotton, nylon, or hemp. Good Housekeeping: wash fabric according to the care label.

Whirlpool: check the care tag before the washer. Plastic or vinyl hang to dry only. Do not assume a dryer is safe.

## Mineral haze is not mildew

White, cloudy, chalky film is calcium and magnesium plus soap. USGS: in hard water, soap reacts with calcium to form soap scum. That is the haze this page is for.

Black specks or pink dots are mold or mildew. Different job. Bleach can be a mildew path. Bleach does not dissolve calcium. Do not bleach a mineral problem.

Never mix vinegar and bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids. EPA: never mix chlorine bleach with other cleaning solutions or ammonia detergents — toxic fumes.

If the liner is black-speckled *and* chalky, do one job at a time. Rinse hard between.

## Vinegar soak is the path

Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Real Simple: for plastic curtains that cannot go in the wash, fill the bathtub or a large sink with equal parts water and white vinegar, submerge, soak overnight, rinse well, and rehang to dry. For fabric, add one cup of distilled white vinegar to the wash cycle.

That overnight equal-parts soak is the mineral pass on vinyl and PEVA. Not a bleach soak. Not a CLR soak.

### Vinyl / PEVA liner

1. Pull the liner off the rod. Take the rings off.
2. Mix **equal parts** cool or warm water and distilled white vinegar in the tub. Enough to cover. Not hot. Whirlpool: hot water may damage a plastic liner.
3. Submerge. Soak overnight per Real Simple.
4. Wipe both sides with a soft cloth. Rinse until it does not smell like salad.
5. Hang it on the rod to air dry. Whirlpool, Bob Vila, and BHG: do not put plastic or vinyl in the dryer. Heat warps it.

Washer optional if the tag allows: gentle cycle, towels in the drum, hang to dry. Still skip the dryer.

### Fabric curtain

1. Read the care tag. Wirecutter: some curtains are dry-clean only. Good Housekeeping: wash fabric according to the label. Do not assume every fabric can take vinegar.
2. If the tag allows a machine wash: cold, delicate cycle, a little detergent. Real Simple: add **one cup** of distilled white vinegar if the fabric can take it. Spot-test a hidden hem first.
3. Air dry on the rod unless the tag allows a low dryer. Wirecutter: curtains can shrink in the dryer. Do not soak a dry-clean-only curtain in vinegar.

## CLR last — vinyl/PEVA wipe only, not a soak

Household [CLR Calcium, Lime & Rust Remover](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) — the 80 oz gray jug — is not the path. Vinegar is.

CLR’s How-To FAQs: do not use Calcium, Lime and Rust Remover on plastics, laminates, Formica, clothing, or fabric. It should never be used on clothing, and it may break down the fibers. Stay-off also names aluminum, galvanized metals, nickel, brass, and copper.

That list includes plastics. CLR is **not** labeled for a vinyl soak. Do not soak a liner in it. Do not pour it on a fabric curtain. Do not park it on nickel, brass, or aluminum grommets.

After two failed vinegar soaks, a last short-dwell wipe is at your own risk. Spot-test a hidden corner. Two minutes. Rinse hard. Never fabric.

1. Ventilate. Gloves. Mix **equal parts** [CLR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) and warm water. Apply with a cloth. After 2 minutes, rinse with cold water. Never leave it on longer than two minutes. Never mix with bleach or other cleaners.
2. Spot-test a hidden corner first. If it clouds or dulls, stop. Replace the liner.
3. Wipe the stained vinyl/PEVA only. Do not soak it in CLR. Keep it off fabric and off nickel, brass, or aluminum grommets.
4. Clock **2 minutes**. Rinse thoroughly. Repeat a fresh 2-minute wipe only if crust remains.

## Two failed mineral soaks: replace the liner

A cheap vinyl/PEVA liner is a consumable. Good Housekeeping: stubborn stains, odors, or tears can mean it is time to replace. Two overnight vinegar soaks that leave the same chalky film are enough.

A standard vinyl/PEVA replacement liner is the class. This page does not name a specific liner SKU.

Hang the new liner open after each shower so it dries. Prevention on glass is a squeegee job — [how to prevent hard water stains on shower doors](/articles/how-to-prevent-hard-water-stains-on-shower-doors) — not a Rain-X how-to on a curtain.

## What not to do

- Do not mix vinegar and bleach. Chlorine gas.
- Do not use household CLR on a fabric curtain. CLR: never on clothing or fabric.
- Do not soak a vinyl liner in CLR. Plastics are on the stay-off list. Wipe only, two minutes, if you go there at all.
- Do not recopy the glass-door ladder. No cerium pad. Glass is [hard water stains on glass shower doors](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-glass-shower-doors).

## FAQ

**Can I use vinegar on a vinyl or PEVA liner?**
Yes. Real Simple: equal-parts water and white vinegar, soak overnight, rinse, rehang. Hang to dry. No hot dryer.

**Can I use CLR on a fabric shower curtain?**
No. CLR: never on clothing or fabric. [Household CLR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) is a last 2-minute wipe on vinyl/PEVA only. The stay-off list names plastics. Not a soak. Not fabric.

**Is the white haze mildew?**
No. White chalky film is mineral. Black or pink specks are mold or mildew. Do not bleach calcium. Do not mix vinegar and bleach.

**Can I put the liner in the dryer?**
No for vinyl or PEVA. Heat can melt plastic. Hang it on the rod. Fabric: only if the care tag allows.

**What about the glass shower door?**

**Can I mix bleach and vinegar to get both mildew and minerals?**
No. Poison Control: bleach plus an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help 1-800-222-1222.

**The liner is still chalky after two vinegar soaks.**
Replace it. A standard vinyl/PEVA replacement liner is the class. This page does not name a specific liner SKU.
