## Pull-down vs side sprayer. Unscrew the head. Clip the hose.

Two kitchen sprayers. Same mineral. Same soak.

- **Pull-down.** The wand lives in the high-arc spout. You pull it down. The hose wants to snap back into the neck.
- **Side sprayer.** A separate head on the deck. Hose still runs under the sink. ANZZI names the kitchen faucet with side sprayer on this same bag-and-scrub job.

1-Tom-Plumber splits old vs new. Older side sprays: a visible or hidden screw, then a perforated disk, sleeve, and washers. Newer heads: the spray nozzle unscrews from the hose. Modern sprays are often built into the faucet rather than sitting as a separate deck gadget. GROHE: remove the spray head from the hose by unthreading the hose coupling. Then the hose can be pushed through the neck of the spout. That is why you clip it.

1. Close the drain or drop a towel in the sink. Small parts go there.
2. Pull the wand out. **Clip the hose** — bag clip, clothespin, or hold it — so it does not disappear into the spout neck.
3. Unscrew the head. 1-Tom-Plumber, newer model: turn the spray head counterclockwise to remove it from the hose. Hand-loose if you can. Cloth on the collar if you need pliers. Bare jaws scar chrome.
4. Photograph the stack. Washer, screen, check insert. GROHE: pull-out faucets collect debris in the coupler and the check insert in the spray head. Rinse those. They go back in the same order.

The [clogged faucet aerator](/articles/how-to-clean-a-clogged-faucet-aerator) is a mesh screen in a tap tip. It is not this wand. [How to clean a shower head with vinegar](/articles/how-to-clean-a-shower-head-with-vinegar) is a different fixture. Do not merge the three.

## Vinegar soak of the detached head. Then a wooden toothpick.

**Detached head only.** Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Pantry. No Amazon link.

1-Tom-Plumber: white vinegar is 5–10 percent acetic acid. Soak the entire spray head in a bowl of vinegar for at least **1.5 hours**. Then poke the sprayer’s holes with a toothpick and rinse with warm water. Older models: soak the cover the same 1.5 hours, toothbrush, toothpick the holes.

GROHE soaks a kitchen spray head in a **50/50** mix of vinegar and water when the head is noisy or will not switch spray and stream. GROHE’s faucet-care note: do not put undiluted household vinegar or a limescale remover on the fitting; those are aggressive to the surface. If lime deposits occur, GROHE suggests 50/50 white vinegar and water. Chrome-only detached soak can follow 1-Tom’s straight vinegar clock. GROHE, PVD, and other specialty finishes stay on 50/50.

USGS: vinegar is an acid; the acidity helps dissolve mineral so water can carry it away.

1. Bowl in the sink. Head fully under the liquid.
2. Distilled white vinegar from the pantry. Or 50/50 with water on specialty finishes.
3. At least 1.5 hours on a detached chrome head (1-Tom-Plumber). 50/50 on a GROHE or PVD head.
4. Soft toothbrush on the face. No scouring powder on the orifices.
5. **Wooden toothpick** in each rubber spray button. Slow in-and-out. Do not ream the hole larger. Not a metal needle, sewing pin, or paper clip. A needle can tear the rubber and wreck the spray pattern.
6. Delta Touch-Clean: the soft rubber spray holes are meant to wipe. After the soak, rub the face with a finger so calcium and lime break off the nozzles.
7. Rinse with warm water until the holes run clear. GROHE: rinse debris out of the check insert.

Never mix vinegar and bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids, and rust-removal products too. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

No citric on this page.

## Bag-on-head if it will not come off

Painted-on scale. A collar that will not turn. A renter who cannot fight the wand.

ANZZI: fill a plastic bag with white vinegar, rubber-band it around the faucet head so the face is submerged, leave it **30 minutes to an hour**. Then scrub the nozzle holes and run water through.

This page’s bag clock is **20–60 minutes**. Not overnight on the faucet. Specialty finishes do not get an overnight vinegar sock. GROHE: undiluted household vinegar is aggressive to the surface. Moen: 50/50 household vinegar and water for a limited amount of time, then rinse and dry. Moen also lists Matte Black as a powder-coat finish and tells you to avoid lime-scale removers there.

1. Clip the hose first if the head is a pull-down. The bag is extra weight.
2. Sandwich bag. Distilled white vinegar, or 50/50 on specialty finishes. Enough to cover the spray face.
3. Rubber band or string. 20–60 minutes. Watch it. Bags slip.
4. Dump. Run water 30 seconds. Finger-wipe the rubber nozzles. Wooden toothpick any hole that is still dark.
5. If flow is still dead, you still have to get the head off. The soak of a detached head is the real unclog.

1-Tom-Plumber’s baggie pro tip soaks 1.5 hours. That is their clock on a bag. This page stays 20–60 minutes while the bag is hanging on the faucet, because the acid is sitting on the finish and the hose.

## Reinstall. Test. Weak stream with the head off is not this page.

Screw the head back onto the hose. 1-Tom-Plumber: dry first, then screw it on. GROHE: hand-tight. Do not crush a plastic coupling.

Pull the clip. Dock the wand. Open the tap. Stream and spray. Every rubber hole should spit. A dark hole goes back to the toothpick.

**If the stream is still weak with the head removed, stop.** That is not scale in the nozzles. That is a cartridge or a supply line. GROHE: trapped debris is the common slow-flow cause on pull-out faucets, and they want the coupler and the check insert in the spray head cleaned. GROHE: a sudden pressure drop is often debris in that coupler or check valve — rinse it. If those are clean and the hose itself still dribbles, this page is done. Cartridge, collapsed supply, or a diverter is a different job.

## FAQ

**Is a weak sprayer a dead cartridge or clogged nozzles?**

Look at the rubber holes. ANZZI: mineral in those tiny holes drops pressure and makes the spray uneven. 1-Tom-Plumber: in most cases, vinegar plus a toothpick in the nozzle holes unclogs it. If the stream is still weak with the head **off**, that is a cartridge or supply line, not this page.

**Pull-down or side sprayer — same steps?**

Yes. Unscrew the head. Clip or hold the hose on a pull-down. Vinegar soak. Wooden toothpick. ANZZI’s bag method names the side sprayer too.

**Wooden toothpick or a metal needle?**

Wooden toothpick. 1-Tom-Plumber names a toothpick. A metal needle can tear the rubber buttons and ruin the spray pattern. Delta Touch-Clean wants a finger wipe on the soft rubber holes, not a poke-through.

**How long is the vinegar soak?**

Detached chrome head: at least 1.5 hours (1-Tom-Plumber). GROHE and other specialty heads: 50/50 vinegar and water. Bag still on the faucet: **20–60 minutes**, not overnight.

**The head will not unscrew. Now what?**

Bag-on-head. ANZZI: vinegar bag, 30 minutes to an hour, then scrub the nozzle holes. This page caps that hang at 60 minutes. Then you still want the head off if the spray is dead.

**Can I use CLR on a matte black or brass sprayer?**

No. CLR lists chrome and bans brass, copper, aluminum, and coated or sealed surfaces. CLR can pit brass. Moen: avoid lime-scale removers on powder-coat finishes, including Matte Black. Two-minute chrome-only clock, then a hard rinse. Not overnight on the hose.

**Can I mix vinegar or CLR with bleach to get scale and germs?**

No. Poison Control: bleach plus an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar and rust-removal products. Leave the area. Fresh air. **1-800-222-1222**.
