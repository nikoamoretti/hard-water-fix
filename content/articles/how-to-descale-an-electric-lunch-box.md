## Name the part. Electric lunch box is not Instant Pot, rice cooker, or food steamer.

An electric lunch box is a small corded warmer. Food sits in a **removable pot** (stainless or coated) that nests on a heating base. Scale lives on the pot walls, the pot floor, the lid rim, and — on some models — a shallow **steam well** under the pot. The base stays dry except for the well **your** booklet names. Identify the heat style before you pour.

- **Electric lunch box removable pot** (this page): unplug and cool. Vinegar **often 1:3** in the pot per the model sheet. Soft cloth. Rinse until **no vinegar smell**. One plain-water heat after. Never immerse the heating base. Never pour liquid into the electric well unless **that** sheet names a steam-well wipe — and even then, wipe, do not flood.
- **Steam-base lunch box** (honesty): some sheets want a measured splash of water in a well under the pot so steam heats the food. Chalk can live in that well. Follow **your** sheet for a well wipe or a measured rinse. Do **not** invent a food-steamer reservoir fill. Do **not** dunk the base to “soak the well.”
- **Dry warmer lunch box** (honesty): the pot sits on a **dry contact plate**. No cook water belongs in the base. Soft-wipe the cool plate if chalky. Never pour vinegar or water into a dry well “to descale it.” That floods electronics. Different failure than scale.

1. **Unplug and cool.** Completely. No hot splash on a warm plate. Confirm the appliance is an **electric lunch box**, not an Instant Pot, not a rice cooker, not a food steamer. Never start a mineral pass on a live, hot base.
2. Empty leftover food and cook water. Lift out the removable pot, the lid, and any tray or divider. Soft-wipe loose flakes. Wash food bits first with mild soap and a soft sponge, then rinse. Do not start acid on top of leftover rice, sauce, or grease.
3. Mix distilled white vinegar and water **exactly as YOUR sheet prints**. Default when the sheet is silent or says “dilute vinegar”: **1:3** (one part vinegar, three parts water). Pour into the **removable pot only**. Do not overfill so the mix floods the exterior, the switch, or the cord. Do **not** pour vinegar into the electric base, the hinge, or under the heating plate.
4. **Steam-base honesty:** if the booklet names a steam well, treat that well the way **that** sheet prints — usually a cool wipe or a measured splash, not a dunk. Do not copy a food-steamer reservoir hour onto a lunch-box well. **Dry-warmer honesty:** leave the well empty. Wipe the cool plate with a barely-damp cloth if chalky. Never flood a dry warmer to “soak the heater.”
5. Soften: let the dilute vinegar sit in the pot **15–30 minutes** on a cool counter (or run a short empty heat / soak only if **your** booklet allows vinegar in a powered pass). Stay in the room. Do not overnight-soak a coated lunch pot in strong acid as a habit.
6. Soft cloth or soft sponge on the pot walls, the floor, the water-line ring, and the lid rim. No green pad. No steel wool. No powdered cleanser on a food-contact coating. Corners hide scale.
7. Empty the spent liquid into the sink. **Rinse until no vinegar smell.** Food path — rinse until the pot smells like water, not pickle. Repeat rinses as needed. The **base stays dry** except for the well wipe the sheet allowed. **Never immerse the heating base.**
8. **One plain-water heat after.** Return the rinsed pot. Fill with plain water to a normal lunch-box line (prefer pantry distilled for this rinse if the tap is hard). Run one empty heat / steam the way you would warm food. Smell the pot. Water only. Discard that water. Dry the pot, lid, and plate before the next lunch.

Skip that plain-water heat and the next leftovers taste like vinaigrette. If you still smell vinegar, rinse again and run a second plain-water empty heat before food goes back.

One measured 1:3 pot pass plus a full rinse clears most fresh chalk. Raised scale that survives one pass may need a second on-label soak and another soft-cloth wipe — still no CLR, still no citric money SKU, still no Dezcal. Vinegar will not rebuild a failed heater or switch. Do not invent a replacement ASIN on this page.

Never mix leftover vinegar with bleach. Poison Control: bleach plus an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Leave the area. Fresh air. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**. If bleach was ever used in the pot, rinse with plain water until it does not smell like bleach before any vinegar pass.

## Hard-water signs in an electric lunch box

Know the film before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze on the pot wall or floor after leftovers are emptied: early mineral film. Distilled rinse and a soft wipe may stretch the gap between full descales.
- Raised chalk a fingernail catches at the water line or on the pot floor: carbonate scale. Vinegar **often 1:3** in the removable pot per the model sheet. Soft cloth. Rinse until **no vinegar smell**. One plain-water heat after.
- White dust or rings on a **dry** heating plate under the pot: wipe the plate — do not pour acid into the base to “soak” it.
- White crust in a **steam well** under the pot: steam-base chalk. Follow the sheet for a well wipe. Do not dunk the base. Do not turn this into a [food steamer](/articles/how-to-descale-a-food-steamer) reservoir job (#96).
- Longer heat-up, weak steam, or a lunch box that stays lukewarm with heavy chalk in the pot: scale may be insulating the pot floor or the plate contact. Descale the pot. Soft wipe the plate or well per the sheet. Then one plain-water heat. Then judge the heater.
- Brown or rusty tint on a stainless pot or plate rim: can be iron or heater discoloration, not only calcium. Stay on pantry vinegar first. Do not jump to bathroom lime gel in a food pot.
- Food sludge or grease that soap lifts first: wash that before any acid. Vinegar’s job here is mineral, not leftover sauce.
