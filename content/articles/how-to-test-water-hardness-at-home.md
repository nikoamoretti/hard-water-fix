## What hardness is (and why the glass spots)

USGS: hardness is dissolved **calcium and magnesium**. Heat them and you get calcium carbonate — limescale — on heaters, kettles, shower jets, and glasses. USGS names the home coffee maker. Soap plus calcium leaves scum. Filmy hands after washing are the same minerals, not a number.

USGS: hardness is **not a health concern**. It is scale, spots, extra soap. After you know it is scale, use acid — [citric vs vinegar](/articles/citric-acid-vs-vinegar-for-limescale) — not bleach. Coffee-maker bottles vs pantry vinegar: [vinegar vs descaling solution](/articles/vinegar-vs-descaling-solution-coffee-maker).

We use **USGS classes**. WQA (NSF/ANSI 44) calls water “soft” only below **1 gpg / 17.1 mg/L**; USGS calls 0–60 mg/L soft. Same minerals; we pick USGS:

| USGS class (as CaCO₃) | mg/L (ppm) | Grains per gallon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft | 0–60 | about 0–3.5 |
| Moderately hard | 61–120 | about 3.6–7.0 |
| Hard | 121–180 | about 7.1–10.5 |
| Very hard | more than 180 | about 10.5+ |

**ppm ≈ mg/L** on tap water. USGS Water-Supply Paper 1895-B: below about 12,000 mg/L the two units match, and **grains per U.S. gallon = mg/L ÷ 17.1**. Hach’s 5-B uses 17.1 too. Older USGS papers sometimes print 17.12. Field math is 17.1.

## The free city report (CCR)

If you pay a water bill, start here. **EPA requires community water systems** to deliver a Consumer Confidence Report (annual drinking water quality report) **by July 1**. Free.

Find it without a bookmark that 404s: search the **utility website** for “consumer confidence report” or “water quality report”; start at EPA’s CCR hub ([epa.gov/ccr](https://www.epa.gov/ccr)) or [CCR information for consumers](https://www.epa.gov/ccr/ccr-information-consumers) and use the **CCR search tool** (state, then county or system; EPA’s tool does not list every system); or **call the supplier**. EPA: if you did not get a copy, call them or use the tool. Hotline **1-800-426-4791**.

Hunt **hardness**, **total hardness**, or hardness **as CaCO₃** (mg/L or ppm). Some reports split **calcium** and **magnesium** — same two minerals. Missing hardness is normal: EPA’s required list is source water, regulated contaminants, and health-standard violations — not hardness. Then a strip still answers the tap.

A CCR is last year’s **system average**, not this morning’s tap. Same community system = same report. No water bill? EPA still requires copies on request — ask the utility or building office.

**Private wells have no CCR.** EPA: private wells are not under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act. Home strip or drops first. A certified lab is for bacteria, nitrate, and drink-worry tests. This page is not a well-lab catalog.

## Strips: a range, not a lab value

A pad turns a color. You match a chart. That is a **bin** — “around 120,” not 117. Lighting, a wet chart, and **expired or damp pads** lie. JNW’s hardness booklet: reagents change color as they dry; store sealed with the desiccant, cap tight, cool and dry, **not in the bathroom**.

Buy a **total hardness** bottle — not a TDS pen, not chlorine-only, not a 14-in-1 that hides one hardness square. [JNW Direct-style hardness strips](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07B31M49N?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) are the cheap first kit (catalog: dedicated hardness pack, 150 strips, framed as a softener check).

Follow **the bottle in your hand**. Kits differ. JNW booklet: dip **2 seconds**, shake, read at **15 seconds**. Do not wait until the pad dries. Check whether *your* chart is **ppm or grains**. No color key we did not photograph. No invented “% accurate.”

Cold tap, run a few seconds. Dip for the labeled seconds, shake once, hold level, read on the labeled clock next to the chart in room light. Write the **range** and the date. Two strips a whole bin apart means a tired bottle or a late read. Use that to learn the USGS class, or to see that a softener outlet is obviously softer than the inlet. Do not size equipment from a color.

## Drops: when you need a number in grains

Softener valves talk in **grains per gallon**. Drops give a tap number without mailing a bottle.

A [drop-count hardness kit](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008FM7WLU?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) in the Hach 5-B style is the backup: EDTA titration, **1 drop = 1 gpg** as CaCO₃ (Hach: also **17.1 mg/L**). Hach range **1–30 gpg** (~17–513 mg/L), about **100 tests**. Field kit, not a certified lab.

Hach’s method: reagent turns the sample **red/pink** if hardness is present; add titrant **drop by drop** until **blue**; **drops = grains**. Follow the sheet in *your* box (Hach DOC326.97.80523). First drop blue means under 1 grain.

Use drops to compare two taps, check a softener **is working**, or turn a CCR in mg/L into grains. Do not pick a tank off a napkin. One titration skips iron, pH, and flow. Treatment needs hardness **plus** iron and pH from someone who sizes systems — not this page.

## What the number does not buy you

It does not size a whole-house softener. No resin-capacity math.

It does not pick a shower filter. An inline cartridge does **not** remove calcium or magnesium. Tests already cited on [best shower filter for hard water](/articles/best-shower-filter-for-hard-water) found **none reduced hardness**. Pitcher carbon does not soften either. Scale on glass, kettles, and coffee makers is still citric or vinegar.

It is not a TDS reading. USGS: **total dissolved solids** is everything dissolved — calcium and magnesium **plus** sodium, potassium, bicarbonate, sulfate, chloride, nitrate, silica. A TDS pen measures that pile (usually via conductivity). High TDS often rides along with hardness. It is not hardness.

The dish-soap “folk test” is not a number. USGS treats slimy hands as a *symptom*. Skip it.

## What not to do

- Do not treat a strip as a lab value, or skip the free CCR on city water.
- Do not skip a home test on a well, and do not treat this page as a well-lab store.
- Do not buy a TDS pen as a hardness kit, or shop shower filters / pitcher carbon to “fix hardness.”
- Do not size a softener from one dip.

## FAQ

**Is a test strip accurate?**
It is a color bin. Light, timing, and old pads move the match. JNW: read on the labeled clock; drying changes the color. No invented percent.

**City water — do I still need a kit?**
CCR first. [Hardness test strips](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07B31M49N?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) if the report omits hardness, or you want this tap this week.

**I have a well. Where is my CCR?**
There isn’t one. EPA does not regulate private wells under the Safe Drinking Water Act. Strip or drops at the tap; a certified lab if you need more than hardness.

**ppm or grains?**
Same water. Divide mg/L (ppm) by **17.1** for gpg (USGS; Hach). 171 mg/L ≈ 10 gpg — USGS “hard.” A [drop-count hardness kit](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008FM7WLU?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) reads grains directly.

**Will a shower filter or Brita lower the number?**
No. See the [shower-filter article](/articles/best-shower-filter-for-hard-water).

**Can I use this number to buy a softener?**
It tells you hard vs very hard. It does not size the tank.
