## White chalk vs orange iron vs efflorescence

Do this in daylight, on a dry uncleaned streak.

| What you see | What it is | Next move |
|---|---|---|
| White or tan chalky arcs from sprinkler heads | Calcium film from hard irrigation water | Dry-brush. Wet the wall. Vinegar 1:1. Short dwell. Rinse top-down. |
| White powder that returns after rain, not under a sprinkler arc | Construction efflorescence from salts in the wall | Not this page’s vinegar path. Dry-brush. Fix moisture. |
| Orange or brown streaks, well-water drip lines | Iron | Iron Out only. Never bleach. Never mix with vinegar or CLR. |

## Dry-brush first. Wet the wall. Vinegar 1:1 on white streaks.

Faint white film under a sprinkler arc. Not orange. Not powder blooming from the substrate after rain. Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. About 5% acetic acid. Mix **equal parts** vinegar and water — 1:1. Pantry only. No distilled Amazon jug. Distilled water is not a product on this page.

Work in shade if you can. Heat plus leftover acid is how people mark a finish.

1. Dry-brush loose chalk with a soft-bristle brush. Sweep it off. Do not grind mineral into the texture.
2. Wet the stucco with a garden hose first. Top-down. Gentle. Soak the working area so the acid does not flash-dry into the pores.
3. Mix pantry distilled white vinegar **1:1** with water. Spray the *white* sprinkler streaks. Or wet a soft cloth and dab the film. Keep it off plants, glass, and painted trim.
4. Short dwell. A few minutes. Do not let it dry on the wall. Soft-bristle brush only. No wire brush. No aggressive scrub that tears the finish.
5. Rinse thoroughly from the top down, as rain would descend. Flush until the vinegar smell is gone. Neutral leftover acid matters on porous stucco.
6. If the film softens but stays, one more short wet pass. Same 1:1. Same rinse. Do not park acid overnight on painted stucco.

That is the calcium path. The garden hose is the rinse. Do not buy a distilled jug as a required product for this job.

## Do not pressure-wash porous stucco. Wash the whole elevation.

A pressure washer can etch soft finish, drive water behind cracks, and leave a brighter rectangle next to dirty wall. A garden hose and a soft-bristle brush is the care path for sprinkler film.

Always rinse gently from the top down. A clean patch next to a dirty wall means wash the whole elevation. Spot-cleaning mineral on faded or textured stucco leaves a bright scar.

Stay off window glass, painted trim, and the plant bed. Cover shrubs if runoff will hit them. Fix the sprinkler heads so they stop hitting the wall, or the arcs return after the next dry afternoon.

If the wall has hairline cracks, do not force water into them. Treat mineral on the face only. Deep moisture behind stucco is a different repair.
