## Name the deck. Concrete is not laminate, granite, or Corian.

A concrete countertop is cementitious — poured in place or precast — usually sealed with a topical or impregnating sealer. It scratches under grit. Acid can etch the paste and strip the sealer. Identify sealed vs raw before you spray.

- **Sealed** poured or precast concrete deck: this page. Water drop beads on a dry patch. pH-neutral daily. Soft wipe. Reseal when beading fails.
- **Raw / unsealed** concrete: open pores darken under a water drop in a minute or two. Soft pH-neutral wipe only. Do not acid it. Reseal after the crust is gone if the maker wants a sealer on this mix.
- Thin plastic sheet over particleboard, repeating pattern, sink cutout seam: [laminate](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-laminate-countertop). Different wear layer.
- Speckled natural slab, sealer on a calendar: [granite](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-granite). No vinegar. No CLR. Different stone.
- Acrylic/polyester solid surface, color all the way through, no sealer calendar: [Corian](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-corian). Wrong surface class.

## Raised crust. Plastic scraper. Baking-soda paste mechanical only.

Raised white crust a fingernail catches near the faucet base or along the sink rim. That is carbonate scale on the sealer, not a dye in the concrete.

1. Soften with [Miracle Sealants Daily](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DJWS5TDF?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). Soft wipe.
2. Plastic scraper or plastic putty knife only — hold nearly flat. Lift crust. No metal blade. No razor on the sealer.
3. Mix baking soda with a little water to a thick paste. Peanut-butter thick. Mechanical only. Spread on leftover chalk. Sit a few minutes. Do not let it dry hard.
4. Soft wipe. Rinse with clean water. Buff completely dry.
