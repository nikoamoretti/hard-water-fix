## Unplug. Cool. Vinegar 1:1 on/in the plate or tank. Soft cloth. One plain-water steam after.

1. **Unplug.** Cool completely. No hot splash on a warm plate. Never start a mineral pass on a live, hot base.
2. Pull the lid, the egg tray, and the measuring cup. Soft-wipe loose chalk on cool plastic. Food and eggs stay out.
3. Mix **1:1** distilled white vinegar (5% pantry bottle) and water. Pour **onto the heating plate** or **into the tank** the way **your** model sheet directs. 4. Soften: let the dilute vinegar sit on the cool plate, or run the empty steam / sit the booklet allows — only as that sheet prints. Stay in the room. Do not overnight-soak a coated plate in strong acid as a habit.
5. Soft cloth or soft sponge on the plate, the rim, and the tank walls if the model has a tank. No green pad. No steel wool. No powdered cleanser on a coated heater. Corners and the fill-line ring hide scale.
6. Empty the spent liquid into the sink. Wipe again. Rinse removable trays under the tap. The **base stays dry** except for the plate or tank the sheet told you to treat. Never dunk the base in the sink.
7. **One plain-water steam after.** Fill with plain water the way you would for a normal cook (prefer pantry distilled for this rinse if the tap is hard). Run one empty steam cycle. Smell the chamber. Water only. Not pickle. Discard that water. Dry the plate, tray, and lid before the next egg batch.

Skip that plain-water steam and the next eggs taste like vinaigrette. If you still smell vinegar, run a second plain-water empty steam before food goes back.

## Hard-water signs on an egg cooker plate

Know the film before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze on the plate after the unit cools: early mineral film. Distilled fill and a soft wipe may stretch the gap between full descales.
- Raised chalk a fingernail catches on the heating plate or at the tank fill line: carbonate scale. Vinegar **1:1** on/in the plate or tank per the model sheet. Soft cloth. One plain-water steam after.
- Weak steam, longer heat-up, or a cycle that cuts early: scale may be insulating the plate. Descale on the booklet path. Then one plain-water steam.
- Brown or rusty tint on the plate: can be iron or heater discoloration, not only calcium. Stay on pantry vinegar first. Do not jump to bathroom lime gel on a food-contact plate.
- Peeling or scratched coating: honesty — vinegar lifts film; it does not rebuild a ruined plate. Stop scraping.
