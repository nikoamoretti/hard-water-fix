## CLR how-to (limescale path)

Household Calcium, Lime & Rust Remover. Not Brilliant Bath. Follow the jug.

1. Ventilate. Gloves. Mix **equal parts** with warm water. Spot-test.

2. Towel off natural stone, brass, copper, aluminum, galvanized metal, colored grout, and anything painted, coated, or sealed. FAQ adds marble (including synthetic), granite, terrazzo, nickel, oil-rubbed bronze, plastics, steam irons, ice makers, pool tile, cast iron.

3. Apply [CLR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) with a brush, cloth, or sponge. **No longer than 2 minutes.** Do not let it dry. Do not soak.

4. Rinse thoroughly with **cold water**. Repeat a fresh 2-minute pass if crust remains. Full strength only if 50/50 still leaves crust — still two minutes, still a cold rinse.

5. Never mix with bleach or other household cleaners. CLR: toxic fumes. Washington State DOH: bleach plus an acid gives off chlorine gas; rust removers are on that acid list.

Toilet bowls are on CLR’s overview; the clock is still two minutes. A white waterline ring that needs gel is the [toilet bowl](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-toilet-bowl) page. Light film versus crust is [vinegar vs CLR](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains).

## Iron Out how-to (rust path)

All-Purpose powder. Follow **your** box. Summit’s tubs, sinks, and tile method:

1. Ventilate. Gloves. Pre-test. Label reprint: vapors after mixing with water; skip it if you have respiratory problems or sulfite sensitivity. Do not mix with other chemicals.

2. Wet the area.

3. Apply [Iron Out](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07N4KGV2Q?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) powder to a damp sponge or cloth.

4. Rub lightly until stains disappear. Rinse.

5. Stay-off: wood, painted materials, exposed corroding metal, older worn or chipped porcelain **cast iron**. Atlas reprint lists porcelain, fiberglass, and acrylic among uses. Summit also: not coffee makers, water heaters, steam irons, pool water, cars, flatware, or a closed spray bottle (mixed powder off-gasses). Iron Out’s published stay-off does **not** name marble or stone. Pre-test. Stubborn stains: increase dosage and/or contact time — still on the label, not an invented clock.

Toilets are a labeled use. Summit’s bowl method is ½ cup **in** the standing water, scrub, stand until stains are gone, flush. That how-to lives on [how to remove rust stains from a toilet](/articles/how-to-remove-rust-stains-from-toilet-hard-water).

## Why mixing is bad

Do not pour one into the other. Iron Out SDS: **contact with acids liberates toxic gas.** Household CLR is an acid (lactic 10–20%). CLR’s SDS lists acids among incompatibles and says do not mix with other household cleaners.

The reducer is sodium hydrosulfite. Esseco hydrosulfite SDS: reacts with acids to form toxic sulfur dioxide. NIOSH: sulfur dioxide is a colorless gas with a characteristic, irritating, pungent odor. Iron Out SDS: hazardous decomposition includes oxides of sulfur.

Never mix either with bleach. CLR: toxic fumes. Iron Out: do not mix with other chemicals; laundry directions forbid bleach, peroxide, and oxi. Washington State DOH: bleach plus an acid gives off chlorine gas; rust removers are on that acid list. If it still smells like bleach, rinse first.

## What not to do

- Do not pick a lab winner. Different chemistry. Different stain color.
- Do not mix CLR with Iron Out, or either with bleach.
- Do not put household CLR on granite, marble, limestone, travertine, terrazzo, synthetic marble, colored grout, aluminum, brass, or copper.
- Do not treat Iron Out as a limescale bottle, or CLR’s two-minute acid as the hydrosulfite rust path.
- Do not swap in Brilliant Bath as household CLR.
- Do not enclose mixed Iron Out powder in a spray bottle.

## If it comes back

White crust that returns is still hardness in the tap. USGS: calcium and magnesium; heat drops calcium carbonate. A third acid does not change the supply. [How to test water hardness at home](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home) is the Ca+Mg check. This page does not size a softener.

Orange that returns is still iron in the water, or a part that is still rusting — not a reason to buy a third acid. That split is on the [toilet rust](/articles/how-to-remove-rust-stains-from-toilet-hard-water) page.

## FAQ

**Which is stronger?**
We did not run a lab. CLR is lactic/gluconic acid with a two-minute cap. Iron Out is a hydrosulfite reducer. Pick by stain color. Not a ranking.

**Can I use Iron Out on calcium?**
Iron Out is labeled for rust. White/tan calcium crust is the CLR / vinegar job.

**Can I use CLR on rust?**
CLR’s product page lists surface rust, and CLR publishes a 2-minute rusty-toilet how-to. That is still not a hydrosulfite reducer and not a lab winner. Dominant orange/brown: Iron Out’s labeled rust path. Mixed white ridge plus orange: do not mix the bottles; sequential jobs, each on its own clock.

**Can I mix them?**
No. Iron Out SDS: contact with acids liberates toxic gas. Hydrosulfite SDS names sulfur dioxide.

**Can I mix either with bleach?**
Never. CLR: toxic fumes. Iron Out: do not mix with other chemicals. Washington State DOH: bleach plus an acid gives off chlorine gas.

**What about the toilet?**
White/tan waterline = calcium = [toilet bowl](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-toilet-bowl). Orange/brown = iron = [toilet rust](/articles/how-to-remove-rust-stains-from-toilet-hard-water). This page is the chemistry split, not those how-tos.
