## What a rinse aid actually does

USGS hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. When the dishwasher’s last water beads and dries, those minerals stay as spots. Surfactants drop surface tension so the water sheets off. Chelating acids (citric, EDTA) grab leftover hardness so less of it plates onto the glass.

You want both actions in hard water. Sheeting without chelation still dries some calcium onto the pane. Chelation without sheeting still leaves droplets. The difference between bottles is which job they advertise, and how far down the ingredient list the citric sits.

## How to use whichever bottle you picked

1. Find the rinse-aid dispenser in the door, next to the detergent cup. Not the detergent cup.
2. Fill to the full line. Wipe the spill. Close it.
3. Turn heated dry **off** if you are on Shine+Dry. Jet-Dry still works with it on.
4. Run a normal load. If glasses still film, add Lemi Shine booster in the wash and confirm the vinegar wipe still says “film,” not etch.
5. Refill on the bottle’s schedule (Shine+Dry ~20 washes, Jet-Dry monthly).

Rinse aid does not descale the machine. A monthly dishwasher cleaner still matters. Never with bleach.

## FAQ

**Do I still need detergent booster if I use Shine+Dry?**
In hard water, yes, if spots survive the rinse. Wash-cycle citric and rinse-cycle citric are not the same event.

**Is Jet-Dry fine for hard water?**
Yes, as a sheeting agent with some citric. It is the runner-up, not a bad bottle.

**Can I pour vinegar in the rinse-aid dispenser?**
No. Vinegar is not formulated as a rinse aid and can damage some dispensers.
