## Baking soda follow-up for leftover film

Baking soda on this page is the **follow-up**, not a second acid and not a scour powder. After the vinegar pass and a rinse, leftover dull film, a faint waterline, or food haze that is not chalk can take a **baking soda paste** or a **short baking soda simmer**. Le Creuset / Staub-style enamel care already names baking soda for stubborn residue and a soft sponge — follow **your** sheet for clocks and heat. Pantry box only. No Amazon baking-soda URL.

**Paste — local leftover film**

1. Confirm the pot is rinsed and no longer smells like strong vinegar. Cool enough to handle.
2. Mix pantry baking soda with a few drops of water to a **spreadable paste**. Cover the leftover film on the floor, the waterline, or the lid underside.
3. Sit **10–15 minutes**. Soft sponge. No green pad. No steel wool. Baking soda is a mild alkali here, not a license to scour enamel.
4. Wash with dish soap. Rinse until there is **no grit and no sour film**. Food-contact — the next stew is food, not paste.

**Short simmer — a wider leftover haze**

1. Add a spoon or two of baking soda to enough water to **cover the film**. Gentle simmer a few minutes (brand sheets that print this path often sit around **8–10 minutes**). Stay in the kitchen. Do not boil dry.
2. Cool. Pour out. Soft sponge. Wash. Rinse. Distilled pantry rinse if you want. **Dry.**

Baking soda will not un-chip enamel and will not erase heat tint the way vinegar dissolves chalk. Honesty: **vinegar for white mineral; baking soda for leftover film.** Do not mix the leftover vinegar pot liquor with the baking-soda paste in one bowl “to make it stronger.” Rinse between. Do not mix either one with bleach. **1-800-222-1222**.
