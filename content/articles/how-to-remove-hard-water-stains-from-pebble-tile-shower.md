## Why acid etches stone pebbles

The crust you want gone is mostly calcium carbonate. The pebbles are often calcium carbonate too. Dynamic Stone Tools: white vinegar is acetic acid, pH about 2–3; it etches and permanently damages the surface on contact. Lemon is citric, same damage. CLR is highly acidic and causes severe etching. Commercial bathroom descalers are almost all acids.

Do not use vinegar “just for a second.” D and G Floors: even diluted vinegar etches. Olson: acetic acid instantly dissolves the calcium carbonate binders. The dull patch after acid is a hole in the finish. Cleaning will not fill it.

Tile Council of North America: cement grout is dissolved by acids. The valleys between pebbles are cement. Vinegar that “just hits the grout” still etches the stone next to it.

Never mix leftover vinegar or a rust remover with bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids, and rust-removal products too. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

## Etch vs crust. Cleaning will not undo an etch.

White crust is mineral sitting on sealed stone. A dull patch after acid is a hole in the finish.

OUTERclé: mineral buildup sits on top and can often be removed; etching is a chemical reaction with the stone, a dull or rough area that cleaning products cannot restore. Tucson: if the spots have etched rather than sitting on top, cleaning won’t resolve them; restoring that finish requires professional honing and resealing. Olson: an etch is missing stone, not a stain.

Do the wet/feel test before you pick a tool.

1. Wet the mark with a drop of distilled water.
2. Feel it with a fingernail.
3. Raised, chalky, catches a nail: crust on top. You can try to lift it.
4. Smooth and dull, same wet and dry: the finish is gone. That is an etch. Stop. Stone pro.

Do not keep scrubbing a smooth dull patch. A cloth will not restore the gloss. More acid will make the hole bigger.
