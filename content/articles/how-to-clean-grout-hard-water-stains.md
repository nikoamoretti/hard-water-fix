## What that crust is

USGS: hardness is calcium plus magnesium. Heat or evaporation drops them out as calcium carbonate. Cementitious grout is porous. TCNA: mostly cement and sand. Cement is alkaline. Acids dissolve it the way vinegar reacts with baking soda. A short acid contact can lift the crust. Repeated vinegar or acid eats grout and any sealer. Grout makers do not recommend acid as routine cleaning. Sometimes you have to regrout. CTEF / Stone World: Portland cement grout is pH 11 to 13. Repeated acid lowers that pH and makes the joint more porous. TEC: pH-neutral every day. Acid only for infrequent mineral deposits, then rinse. [Vinegar](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains) and [citric acid](/articles/citric-acid-vs-vinegar-for-limescale) dissolve the same scale. They are pantry acids, not the grout hero.

Bleach is the wrong chemistry. CDC: household bleach (sodium hypochlorite) disinfects germs. It does not dissolve calcium carbonate. TCNA: people spray bleach because it clears mildew or "pink stuff" on contact. That is not this crust. Do not mix bleach with acid. Washington State DOH: that mix gives off chlorine gas. CLR: do not mix with bleach or other household cleaners.

## Identify the joint first

Ceramic or porcelain tile only for the acid step. Natural stone (marble, limestone, travertine, some slate) and stone grout: no acid. Natural Stone Institute: calcareous stone is calcium carbonate. Bathroom and grout acids etch it. CLR: stay off natural stone, including marble, granite, and synthetic marble.

Cementitious grout is the porous, calcium-based joint this page is for. Epoxy is different. TCNA: epoxy is virtually as stain-proof as the tile, and sealer is for cementitious grout, not epoxy. TEC: pH-neutral routine on epoxy.

Colored grout: stay off. CLR How-To FAQs: no Calcium, Lime & Rust on colored grout (any color other than white). Brilliant Bath, a different bottle, says acid lightens colored grout. That spray is the tub article's product.

Household [CLR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) does **not** list grout on the product-page use list. Live "for use on": chrome, fiberglass, porcelain, stainless steel. Treat grout as unlabeled. Two-minute cap. Ceramic and porcelain tile only.

## How to lift the crust

Follow **your** label. CLR: equal parts warm water, **2 minutes**, cold rinse. TEC: white nylon on the joint. TCNA: nylon or plastic, not a scratch tool.

1. Ventilate. Gloves. Spot-test. Stay-off: natural stone, colored grout, sealed surfaces, brass, copper, aluminum, wood. Older tile can etch. No bleach mix (Washington State DOH: chlorine gas).
2. Dry-brush first. A [nylon grout brush](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08M9PYJVV?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) in the joint, not a wire wheel. Lift crust. Do not grind the cement.
3. Mix [CLR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) 50/50 with warm water. Apply to mineral on **ceramic or porcelain tile**. Work a little into the crust on a white cementitious joint. **No longer than 2 minutes.** Do not soak grout.
4. Rinse with **cold water**. Repeat a fresh 2-minute pass if crust remains. Do not extend the clock. Acids are lactic and gluconic. Oxalic powder split: [Bar Keepers Friend vs CLR](/articles/bar-keepers-friend-vs-clr).
5. Dry fully. Then a [penetrating grout sealer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DG9RCR6Y?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). Unsealed cement wicks minerals back. TCNA: penetrating class soaks in; topical coats the surface. Not a "best" SKU. Follow the bottle.

Aqua Mix Sealer's Choice Gold (Custom Building Products TDS-158) is one published example of the class: dry, clean surface; penetrate up to 5 minutes; wipe dry; 30 minutes between coats; water-drop test at 2 hours; traffic in 2 hours; keep dry 12 hours; full cure 24 hours. New installs: dry 48 hours. After this wet clean, dry fully first. We do not invent a yearly recoat. If a later water drop soaks in, another coat. After sealing, skip ammonia, acids, and bleach as routine. Neutral cleaner only.

## What not to do

- Do not bleach the white crust. CDC: bleach is for germs.
- Do not soak grout in vinegar or citric. TCNA: cement dissolves in acid. See [vinegar vs CLR](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains) and [citric vs vinegar](/articles/citric-acid-vs-vinegar-for-limescale).
- Do not use a wire brush. Nylon only.
- Do not put [CLR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) on stone, colored grout, or sealed joints.
- Do not mix CLR with bleach (Washington State DOH: chlorine gas).
- Do not skip sealer after a successful mineral clean. Dry fully first.

## If the crust comes back

The minerals are still in the water, and the joints may still be open. USGS: calcium and magnesium. A third acid pass does not change the supply. [How to test water hardness at home](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home) is the Ca+Mg check. This page does not size a softener. Reseal when a water drop soaks in, per the sealer label.

## FAQ

**Is the white stuff mildew?**
No. Raised white or tan crust is calcium carbonate from hardness (USGS: Ca+Mg). Mildew is dark or organic. Bleach can clear mildew. It does not dissolve the mineral.

**The household CLR page does not list grout. Can I still use it?**
Porcelain is listed. Grout is not on the product-page use list. Keep it as the tile-adjacent descaler: 50/50, **2 minutes**, cold rinse, ceramic/porcelain only. Nylon brush in the joint. Do not soak grout. Colored grout: stay off (CLR FAQ).

**Can I use vinegar instead?**
Vinegar is acetic acid. It dissolves calcium carbonate (USGS). It also dissolves cement (TCNA). Short, rare contact if you must. Not a weekly grout wash. CLR's labeled clock is two minutes.

**What about epoxy grout?**
Different material. TCNA: virtually as stain-proof as the tile. Sealer is for cementitious grout. TEC: pH-neutral routine. Do not treat it as porous cement.

**When do I reseal?**
After the joints are fully dry. Follow your [penetrating grout sealer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DG9RCR6Y?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) label. Aqua Mix's published check is a water drop: if it soaks in, another coat. We do not invent a yearly interval.
