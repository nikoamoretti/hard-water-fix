## Name the part. Then stop stealing other steamer URLs.

Countertop food steamers boil a measured fill under stacked baskets. Scale lives on the heating plate and in that reservoir. Broccoli and rice ride above the steam. They are not the descaling target.

- Countertop vegetable / food steamer with a base reservoir and removable baskets: this page.

## Baskets out. Vinegar at maker dilution. Then one plain-water steam.

Food out. Baskets out. You are cleaning the plate and the reservoir, not steaming broccoli in salad dressing.

1. Unplug if the booklet says unplug first. Cool base. No hot splash.
2. Pull the baskets and the drip tray. Soft cloth on loose chalk. No steel wool on a coated plate.
3. Mix or measure distilled white vinegar exactly as **your** sheet prints — Oster-style measured reservoir shot to HI, Cuisinart-style **1:1**, Braun-style cover the element with 5% vinegar. Pantry bottle. Grocery aisle. No Amazon vinegar link on this page.
4. Pour only into the reservoir / onto the element the way the booklet directs. No food. No baskets in the acid pass unless **that** sheet says otherwise (most want them out).
5. Run the timed steam or the sit the maker names — Oster help’s ~20 minutes, Cuisinart’s ~60-minute steam, Braun FAQ’s ~15-minute cover. Stay in the room. Watch for boil-over.
6. Off. Unplug if required. Empty the spent liquid into the sink. Soft wipe the plate, the corners, and the fill-line marks. Scale hides at the rim.
7. **One plain-water steam after.** Fill with plain water (prefer distilled for this rinse). Run the empty steam the sheet wants — Cuisinart prints about **30 minutes** of water after the vinegar hour; other sheets want at least one full rinse steam. Smell the chamber. Water only. Not pickle.

Skip that rinse and the next batch of vegetables tastes like vinaigrette. If you still smell vinegar, run a second plain-water empty steam before food goes back.

## Hard-water signs on a food-steamer plate

Know the film before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze after the base cools: early mineral film. Distilled fill and a wipe may stretch the gap between full descales.
- Raised chalk a fingernail catches on the heating plate: carbonate scale. On-label vinegar pass with baskets out.
- Weak steam, longer heat-up, or a timer that cuts early (Oster-style scale shutoff): scale may be insulating the element. Descale on the booklet path. Then one plain-water steam.
- Brown or rusty tint in the reservoir: that can be iron or heater discoloration, not only calcium. Stay on vinegar at maker dilution first. Do not jump to bathroom lime gel.

This is still the food reservoir. Do not turn it into a [garment steamer](/articles/how-to-descale-a-garment-steamer) tank job or a kettle boil.

## Distilled fill going forward — pantry only

Prevention is the next fill. Distilled water in the reservoir slows the chalk. It is **not** cooking water for the broccoli — you still steam food with whatever fill the booklet allows for daily use; distilled is the mineral-free choice when the sheet permits it and the tap tests hard.

Buy a grocery gallon from the pantry aisle. **No Amazon distilled URL on this page.** Do not paste `B07VD4KN28` or purified `B07VC5RZHM`. Text only: distilled from the store. Same jug for the post-vinegar rinse steam and for daily fills when your booklet allows distilled or demineralized water.

Habits that keep the plate clear:

- Empty leftover reservoir water after the last cook of the day. Standing tap dries into fresh scale overnight.
- Wipe the cool plate. Soft cloth. No metal scraper on a coated heater.
- Descale when steam slows, the timer cuts early, or white crust shows — on the calendar your booklet prints if it has one.
- After every vinegar pass, finish with that plain-water steam before the next meal.

If you want the number behind “hard,” [test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home). Hard and very hard taps return crust faster. Soft municipal water stretches the gap between descales.
