## What that stain is (and what it is not)

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heat those and you get white or tan calcium carbonate — the ring, not this page. Iron is a different metal. USGS: dissolved iron in groundwater can stain toilets a brownish red. EPA’s secondary (non-health) guideline is 0.3 mg/L iron; above that, rusty color and reddish or orange staining. Minnesota Department of Health: the iron can come from soil and rock, **or** from corrosion of iron or steel pipes. Water Quality Association: reddish and yellow-tan is often iron; staining can start at 0.3 ppm even when the tap looks clear, because ferrous iron oxidizes in air.

Two common sources:

1. **Iron in the supply** — well or municipal. Clear water that turns orange after it sits is ferrous (“clear-water”) iron. Water already rusty at the tap is ferric (“red-water”) iron (Minnesota DOH).
2. **A rusting part** — tank bolts, a steel supply, a flapper chain.

## 1. Iron Out on the orange stain

[Iron Out](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00JOLNSFA?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) powder is a **reducer**, not HCl bowl gel and not oxalic powder. Summit’s SDS lists sodium hydrosulfite (also called sodium dithionite) as the rust-reducing ingredient. Toilets are a labeled use. Porcelain is on the retailer label reprint. It is **not** a bleach gel.

Follow **your** label. Summit’s published bowl method:

1. Open a window or run the fan. Rubber gloves. Summit and the label reprint: vapors after mixing with water; skip it if you have respiratory problems or sulfite sensitivity. Do not mix with other chemicals.
2. Remove other cleaners or deodorant from the bowl or tank. Flush. If it still smells like bleach, rinse with plain water until it doesn’t. Washington State DOH lists rust removers among the acids that make chlorine gas with bleach.
3. Add **½ cup** of [Iron Out](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00JOLNSFA?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) to the water in the bowl.
4. Scrub the stained area with a brush.
5. Let it stand **until the stains are gone**. Flush.

That is the label clock. For stubborn stains, Summit says increase dosage and/or contact time — still on the label, not an invented overnight bowl bath. Tank stains: raise the water above the stain line if you can, add ½ cup, let stand overnight or until gone. Stains **above** the waterline are a cling job; Summit points those at Iron Out spray. Do not mix the powder into a closed spray bottle — it off-gasses in water. Stay off wood, paint, exposed corroding metal, and older chipped porcelain cast-iron. Pre-test.

You do **not** have to drop the bowl for this powder. Summit’s method is ½ cup **in** the standing water. Dropping the water still helps a wet paste sit on glaze above the waterline; shutoff steps live on the [white-ring page](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-toilet-bowl).

## If it comes back: water or plumbing, not another gel

A stain that returns is still iron in the water, or a part that is still rusting. An iron filter on well water, or new tank bolts / flapper / supply if those are rusting, is the fix — this page does not size a whole-house system. [How to test water hardness at home](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home) is the Ca+Mg test, not an iron lab.

**Bleach is not a rust remover.** Culligan: chlorine bleach can **set** rust stains rather than lift them; the reaction with iron can darken the stain. CLR’s rusty-toilet FAQ: bleach does not dissolve rust well and can make the stain harder to remove. WQA: the visible stain is oxidized iron. Bleach is an oxidizer; Iron Out is a reducer. Never mix Iron Out, BKF, CLR, or any acid with bleach. Washington State DOH: bleach plus an acid gives off chlorine gas; rust removers and toilet-bowl cleaners are on that acid list. If the bowl still smells like bleach, rinse first.

## What not to do

- Do not treat orange/brown as the white/tan calcium ring, or make pumice or HCl gel the rust hero.
- Do not use bleach as a rust remover. Culligan: it can set and darken iron stains.
- Do not mix [Iron Out](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00JOLNSFA?tag=hardwaterfi04-20), [Bar Keepers Friend](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07JGH35XZ?tag=hardwaterfi04-20), CLR, or any acid with bleach.
- Do not let BKF powder dry on the glaze. One minute, then rinse.
- Do not leave CLR past two minutes, or enclose mixed Iron Out powder in a spray bottle.
- Do not buy a third bowl gel because the orange came back. That is water or a rusty part.

## FAQ

**Is this the same as the hard-water toilet ring?**
No. White/tan waterline = calcium. Orange/brown = iron. The ring page is [how to remove hard water stains from a toilet bowl](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-toilet-bowl).

**Will bleach take the rust off?**
Do not count on it. Culligan: bleach can set rust stains and darken them. CLR’s toilet FAQ: it can make the stain harder to remove. Rinse bleach out before Iron Out or acid.

**Do I have to drop the water?**
Not for Summit’s Iron Out bowl method (½ cup in the standing water). Yes if you need a wet BKF paste or two-minute CLR contact. Shutoff steps are on the white-ring page.

**Can I use pumice on rust?**
Pumice is the mineral-ring tool on intact glaze, not this page’s rust hero.

**Why does it keep coming back?**
Iron is still in the water, or a part is still rusting. Cleaners remove the stain. They do not remove the iron.
