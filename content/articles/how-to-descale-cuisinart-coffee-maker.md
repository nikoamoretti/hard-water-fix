The CLEAN light is scale on the heater path, not a dirty carafe. Cuisinart’s DCC-series booklets print it as calcium buildup interfering with function and flavor. The published mix on those sheets is **1 part white vinegar : 2 parts water** (⅓ vinegar, ⅔ water, reservoir to capacity). A water-only brew will not descale and will not turn the light off.

That vinegar path is the opposite of live [Keurig-without-vinegar](/articles/how-to-descale-keurig-without-vinegar) and [Breville espresso](/articles/how-to-descale-breville-espresso-machine): those brands want a labeled bottle or powder. This carafe maker publishes vinegar. Do not copy their rinse counts or button combos onto a Cuisinart CLEAN cycle.

## What the light is

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heat them and you get calcium carbonate — scale. USGS names the home coffee maker and vinegar as the acid that charges those minerals so they rinse away.

Cuisinart DCC-3200 (14-cup PerfecTemp): the CLEAN LED is the decalcify reminder. Clean it whenever that indicator lights up. DCC-1200 Brew Central (Classic 12-cup): the Self Clean light *flashes* when the coffeemaker is turned on to say calcium is already affecting the pot. It is not a stain on the glass. Wash the carafe separately with soapy water.

Hard-water houses see it sooner. [Test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home) if you want the number. This page does not size a softener.

This how-to is US drip — DCC CLEAN-button carafes. Cuisinart Coffee Center single-serve uses a different fill (13 oz vinegar / 26 oz water on the SS sheets). Skip espresso machines.

## Mix and prep from Cuisinart

Grocery distilled white vinegar. Cuisinart publishes 1:2 vinegar for these carafes, not a branded bottle. We did not invent a Cuisinart Descale Solution SKU.

Dezcal is coffee-industry powder. Cuisinart’s US how-to: they don’t recommend commercial descalers here because these makers have a built-in Clean function. Follow the booklet in the box.

**Ratio — do not average.** US DCC-3200, DCC-1200, DCC-2750, DCC-500, and DGB-900BC sheets we fetched all print **⅓ white vinegar and ⅔ water**, reservoir to capacity. That is 1:2. Cuisinart’s US how-to page also prints **3 parts water and 1 part vinegar** in the Clean-button intro (weaker mix), then **2 parts water and 1 part vinegar** in the numbered vinegar list (same 1:2). Cuisinart.ca’s drip FAQ says fill the reservoir with white vinegar, then press Clean. If those lines disagree, use the PDF that matches *your* model.

Prep from those sheets:

1. Empty grounds. Pull the paper or gold-tone filter.
2. DCC-3200 and DCC-2750: **remove the charcoal water filter** from the reservoir before acid. DCC-1200’s Self Cleaning block we fetched does not reprint that sentence; DCC-3200 does. Official US how-to: pull the charcoal filter too.
3. Empty carafe on the plate. The cycle has to go somewhere.
4. Fill the reservoir **to capacity** with the mix *your* sheet prints.

Do not copy the [Ninja](/articles/how-to-descale-ninja-coffee-maker) 16 oz Travel Mug fill. That ounce line is a different machine.

## CLEAN-button cycle (DCC-3200, from the US booklet)

No invented minutes. DCC-3200 does not print a clock. Do not copy a Ninja 75-minute countdown or a Keurig 12-rinse.

1. Reservoir to capacity with **⅓ white vinegar and ⅔ water**.
2. **Press and hold CLEAN** until the CLEAN LED **flashes**. The coffeemaker is in Clean mode. Release.
3. Let the cycle finish. **Five beeps**, then it shuts off.
4. If CLEAN **illuminates once more**, repeat with a **fresh** vinegar-and-water mix. Note: one cycle is usually sufficient.
5. When CLEAN **stays off**, run **one** cycle with a **full reservoir of fresh, cold water**.
6. Return the charcoal filter to the holder.

Official US how-to matches the hold-until-flash start, the five beeps, the repeat-if-the-light-returns line, and one full water rinse. Same page’s Clean-button intro also says fill the *carafe* with 3 parts water and 1 part vinegar, then dump that into the reservoir. Manuals fill the reservoir to capacity at 1:2. Both are Cuisinart. Use the booklet.

## Why the light stays on

- **Water-only brew.** The CLEAN mix is vinegar and water. A regular brew of tap water is the *rinse after* the light is already off (DCC-3200 step 5), not the descale.
- **Didn’t hold Clean (DCC-3200).** The US booklet wants hold until the LED **flashes**. A tap is not Clean mode on that sheet.
- **Didn’t turn On after Clean (DCC-1200 / 2750 / 500).** Those sheets want Clean *then* On (or Brew/Off) so the light shines steadily.
- **Aborted.** DCC-1200: On/Off exits Clean mode and turns the unit off. Start the vinegar cycle again.
- **Skipped the rinse.** DCC-3200: one full reservoir of fresh cold water *after* CLEAN stays off. That is one cycle, not twelve, not a DualBrew flush pair.
- **Heavy scale.** DCC-3200: if CLEAN illuminates once more, repeat with a fresh mix. One cycle is usually enough; the sheet allows a second.

## What not to do

- Do not pour CLR, Iron Out, or bleach into the reservoir. Those bottles are not in the drip CLEAN block.
- Do not copy a [Keurig](/articles/how-to-descale-keurig-without-vinegar) 12-count rinse or a [Ninja](/articles/how-to-descale-ninja-coffee-maker) DualBrew clock onto this carafe.
- Do not copy Breville’s no-vinegar boiler rule onto this maker. Cuisinart publishes vinegar here.
- Do not treat [Instant Pot vinegar](/articles/how-to-descale-instant-pot) (inner pot only) as a carafe recipe.
- Do not invent a Cuisinart descaler SKU. Official US how-to: they don’t recommend commercial descalers for these Clean-button machines.
- Do not run CLEAN with the charcoal filter still in on DCC-3200 / DCC-2750. Those PDFs say pull it first.
- Do not dry the reservoir with a cloth. DCC-3200: lint may remain.

## If it comes back

Hard water keeps depositing calcium carbonate. [Test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home). Recurring CLEAN is hardness, not a failed carafe wash. Distilled: the drip manuals we fetched do not ban it and do not require it. They ship a charcoal filter for tap water (replace every 60 days or 60 uses on DCC-3200). Official US how-to: filtered water to reduce buildup. That is not a softener.

## FAQ

**What vinegar ratio?**
US DCC-3200 / DCC-1200 / DCC-2750 / DCC-500 / DGB-900 print **⅓ white vinegar : ⅔ water**. Official US how-to prints 3:1 water:vinegar in one paragraph and 2:1 water:vinegar in the next. Cuisinart.ca FAQ says fill with white vinegar. Do not average. Open *your* PDF.

**Can I use Dezcal instead?**
Cuisinart publishes pantry vinegar for these carafes. Official US how-to: they don’t recommend commercial descalers because the Clean function is the path. Dezcal is industry powder, not the path on this page. No grocery citric here.

**Can I copy the Keurig rinse count?**
No. DCC-3200 rinse is **one** full reservoir of fresh cold water after CLEAN stays off. Keurig’s bottle path is a different machine.

**Thermal carafe — different recipe?**
DCC-2750 thermal still prints ⅓ vinegar / ⅔ water. The difference is the start: Clean, then On/Off, not DCC-3200’s hold-until-flash. DGB-900BC thermal grind-and-brew: same mix, Grind Off + On, then a fresh-water repeat. Glass vs thermal is not a different acid ratio on the sheets we fetched.

**Does distilled water stop the light?**
Drip manuals we fetched do not say yes or no. They talk charcoal-filtered tap water. Official how-to: filtered water reduces mineral buildup. A water-only brew still does not descale.

**No CLEAN button?**
Official US how-to: run a normal brew with the same vinegar ratio. DGB-900 uses Grind Off + On for decalc. Check that model’s PDF. Do not hold a button the sheet never names.
