The white film on a shower door is usually not “dirty glass.” It is dried calcium and magnesium from hard water, often mixed with soap scum. Some of it comes off in 20 minutes. Some of it is already etched into the pane, and no cleaner will bring that back.

Start with a 10-second test so you do not grind acid into glass that is already damaged, and so you do not waste a bottle on a problem vinegar would have handled.

## The dry-glass finger test

Do this on a dry, uncleaned patch, in daylight, before you spray anything.

1. Run a fingertip across the haze.
2. Then spritz that same patch with water and look again.

| What you feel / see | What it is | What actually works |
|---|---|---|
| Gritty, chalky, slightly raised | Mineral scale (limescale) | Vinegar for light film. [CLR Calcium, Lime & Rust Remover] for crust you can feel. |
| Waxy, slick, smears | Soap scum (soap + hardness minerals) | Dish soap + vinegar, or Bar Keepers Friend Soft Cleanser. |
| Smooth but still hazy, even when wet | Etching | Stop. Cleaners will not fix it. |

The wet check is the honest one. Mineral deposits usually disappear while the glass is wet (water fills the rough layer) and come back as it dries. Etched glass stays cloudy wet or dry, because the surface itself is pitted.

USGS defines hardness as dissolved calcium and magnesium. In hard water, those minerals react with soap and leave scum; when the droplets dry on glass, they leave scale. USGS classifies water above 121 mg/L as calcium carbonate as hard, and above 180 mg/L as very hard. You do not need a lab number to clean a door. If spots reappear after every shower, you have it.

Soap scum and scale often stack. If the glass feels greasy *and* gritty, take the soap film off first, then hit the mineral crust. Otherwise the scum shields the scale from the acid.

## What you need

- Distilled white vinegar, water, a teaspoon of dish soap, a spray bottle (light film)
- Non-scratch sponge or microfiber, plus a toothbrush for the tracks
- Rubber gloves
- Bar Keepers Friend Soft Cleanser (moderate film + soap scum)
- [CLR Calcium, Lime & Rust Remover] (heavy crust)
- [A shower squeegee] (so you are not doing this every weekend)

Skip razor blades. Shower doors are almost always tempered glass. Tempered panes often have microscopic fabrication debris baked into the surface. A blade can drag those particles and scratch the glass permanently. Use chemistry, not a scraper.

## Method 1: Vinegar for light spots (start here)

Good Housekeeping’s Home Care & Cleaning Lab uses a simple mix for glass shower doors: equal parts distilled white vinegar and water, plus 1 teaspoon of dish soap, in a spray bottle.

1. Spray the door until it is wet.
2. Wait 15 minutes. Do not let it dry on the glass.
3. Wipe with microfiber. For leftover streaks, a non-scratch sponge is enough.
4. Rinse and buff dry.

Vinegar is about 5% acetic acid. It is the right first move for faint spots and weekly film. It is the wrong move for a crust you can feel, and it is never the move on marble, limestone, travertine, or other natural-stone surrounds. Acid dissolves calcium. Those stones *are* calcium.

If 15 minutes of vinegar barely moves the haze, step up. Do not “let it soak overnight” on the door. Longer contact is how people etch finishes and eat grout.

## Method 2: Bar Keepers Friend for film that vinegar smears around

Bar Keepers Friend Soft Cleanser is the middle rung. The liquid clings on vertical glass. The oxalic acid (plus a mild abrasive) is built for soap scum and mineral film, not for a two-hour soak.

From Bar Keepers Friend’s own shower-door instructions:

1. Spot-test a corner of the glass and a handle.
2. Apply Soft Cleanser in a horizontal line so it coats evenly. You do not have to pre-wet the door for the liquid.
3. Let the oxalic acid work for **up to one minute**. Their Soft Cleanser label says rinse within one minute.
4. Gentle non-scratch scrub. Rinse until the glass feels squeaky, not chalky. Leftover product dries as a white film.
5. Wipe dry.

Stay off natural-stone tile (marble, granite, travertine). BKF says the same thing CLR does on that point.

If the door is still chalky after two short passes, the leftover is limescale thick enough for a dedicated descaler.

## If the glass is still cloudy

CLR’s own FAQ is blunt: if the product does not move the cloudiness at all, the glass may be etched. They describe that as tiny cuts in the glass from mineral deposits, with a jeweler’s polish as the only home-adjacent option. A restoration shop can sometimes polish light etching with cerium oxide. Replacement is the honest answer for a pane that stays hazy while wet.

Do not keep escalating acid hoping the ghost will lift. That is how you dull a coating or etch the pane further.

## Keep it off: 30 seconds after the shower

Once the door is actually clear, the cheapest fix is not another bottle. It is not letting mineral water dry on the glass.

Carolyn Forté at the Good Housekeeping Institute is on the record: squeegee after the last shower of the day. ## What not to do

- **Do not razor the glass.** Tempered shower doors scratch. Use a non-scratch sponge.
- **Do not put vinegar, BKF, or CLR on marble, limestone, travertine, or granite surrounds.** CLR says the acids will strip the sealer and damage the stone. Vinegar does the same job more slowly.
- **Do not leave CLR on longer than 2 minutes, or BKF Soft Cleanser longer than 1 minute.** Repeat short cycles instead.
- **Do not mix any of these with bleach.**
- **Do not skip the rinse and the dry.** Residue plus hard water is a new stain.
- **Do not treat etching like a stain.** If the wet test fails, stop buying removers.

## FAQ

**Can CLR damage glass shower doors?**
Used as directed (50/50 with warm water, 2 minutes max, full rinse), CLR lists glass as a suitable surface. Damage reports usually come from undiluted soaks, skipping the rinse, or using it on coated / antique glass. Spot-test.

**Will vinegar permanently remove hard water stains?**
It removes light mineral film. Thick crust needs a stronger acid. Neither vinegar nor CLR can reverse etching.

**How often should I deep-clean the door?**
Squeegee daily. Vinegar weekly. CLR only when you can feel scale again. Daily CLR is how you pick a fight with grout and coatings.

**What about the metal tracks and handles?**
Clean them, but match the finish. CLR is not for brass, copper, aluminum, or oil-rubbed bronze. BKF is the safer first try on most alloys, still with a spot test, and not on oil-rubbed bronze.
