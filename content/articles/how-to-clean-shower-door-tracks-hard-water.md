## What you are looking at

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heat them and you get calcium carbonate scale. Soap plus calcium is scum. Both settle in the lowest metal.

- **Bottom channel** — the U-shaped track on a framed bypass door. Water, hair, and scale pool here.
- **Weep holes** — drain slots so water leaves the channel and goes back into the stall. Basco’s install sheets call the part a “curb (w/ weep holes),” face those holes to the interior, and say **do not silicone over them**.
- **Rollers / wheels** — on many bypass sliders the wheels hang from a **top** rail, not the bottom U. Wipe in place unless *your* door manual says how to take the panel out.
- **Threshold** — Kohler: if the bottom track is not level, water caught on it will not gravitate back onto the receptor or drain.
- **Frameless** — often a vinyl bottom sweep, not a deep track. Basco’s frameless hinge units still sit on a curb with weep holes. Clean the channel that exists.

You need a [nylon track brush](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09H733ZSW?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) that fits the channel — not a grout saw, not a metal pick, not a toothbrush-only. Distilled white vinegar for a first pass (grocery bottle). [Food-grade citric acid](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00EYFKKZC?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) only if the scale is heavy **and** the metal is not anodized or painted.

## 1. Vinegar first pass (light scale)

A short sit, not an overnight acid bath. Sterling: do not let cleaners soak on metal. DreamLine: do not use acid on hardware; if it hits the finish, rinse immediately. Alumax, Century Bathworks, and Superior/Coastal warn that vinegar can dull anodized aluminum. Painted or clearly anodized: mechanical + a cloth, then **stop**.

If the channel is unlabeled chrome-looking metal and the crust is a light film:

1. Slide the panels aside. Pull hair and grit out dry.
2. Lay a paper towel in the channel, or pour a thin line of **distilled white vinegar**. USGS: vinegar (an acid) charges mineral so water can carry it away.
3. Follow vinegar’s usual **short soak**, then look. Not overnight on aluminum. Repeat a timed pass. Do not extend the clock.
4. Scrub the **inside** of the U with the [nylon track brush](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09H733ZSW?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). Sterling: skip bristle brushes on the *visible* anodized face — that face gets a cloth.
5. Rinse. If water overflows onto the bathroom floor, the weep holes are clogged. That leak is **drainage**, not dirt.

Rinse stone if you overshoot. Marble and limestone *are* calcium carbonate.

## 2. Citric for heavy scale — not on anodized

[Citric vs vinegar](/articles/citric-acid-vs-vinegar-for-limescale): food-grade citric is a powder you dose, so the contact solution can be stronger than pantry vinegar on heavy scale, with almost no pickle smell. We did not measure “% removed.”

Mix as on that page / Drench: **2 tablespoons of [food-grade citric acid](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00EYFKKZC?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) per litre of hot water**. Drench’s citric clock on chrome is **15–20 minutes** — a shower-head finish clock, not a track OEM clock. Treat that as the upper bound on unlabeled chrome-like metal. Brush, rinse, dry. Repeat a timed pass. Do not overnight-soak aluminum.

| Finish | First pass | Stay off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unlabeled chrome-looking channel | Short vinegar sit, nylon in the U | Color shift, white bloom |
| Heavy crust on that same channel | Citric mix, timed, then brush | Overnight soaks. Repeat timed. |
| Anodized, painted, powder-coat | Mechanical + cloth. Then **stop** | Citric, CLR, HCl bowl gel |
| Unknown | Cloth + nylon. No acid until you name the finish | Guessing |

Alumax: the anodic coating is damaged by strong acids; anything that strips mineral off aluminum can dull that coating. Superior Home Products’ chart: Lime-Away **destroyed** the anodic coating in their soak test. CLR’s FAQ: Calcium, Lime & Rust Remover can take the finish off aluminum and pit it. CLR Brilliant Bath is a **2-minute glass** product — keep it off the **frame, trim, or track**. No CLR bath of the whole track. No HCl toilet gel in the channel.

## 3. Weep holes: poke, do not gouge

Find the slots. Basco: they face the **interior**, in the curb.

Poke them with a nylon bristle from the [nylon track brush](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09H733ZSW?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) or a **wooden toothpick**. Not a screwdriver.

Pour a cup of water into the clean channel. It should run back into the stall, not onto the bathroom floor. If it still dumps on the floor, the holes are blocked or caulked shut. Basco: **do not silicone over the weep holes.** Kohler: water on the track should gravitate back onto the receptor or drain. A puddle on the tile outside the stall is **clogged drainage**, not a dirty track.

## 4. Rollers: wipe in place

Bypass doors that stick are often hair and scale on the **top** rail, where the wheels actually run. Wipe that rail. Wipe the visible wheels with a damp cloth.

Do not invent a “lift and tilt.” DreamLine’s Encore: do **not** remove the bolts or fully disassemble the roller assemblies. If *your* OEM prints a removal sequence, follow that sheet. If it does not, leave the glass hung.

## 5. Dry the channel or it mildews overnight

Towel the U until it is dry. Leave the door **cracked**. Sterling: keep shower doors ajar so air can circulate to reduce mildew. EPA: moisture control; dry wet materials within **24–48 hours**. CDC: scrub what you can see, **dry right away**. A wet U-channel is a mildew farm.

Do not mix vinegar or citric with bleach. Washington State DOH: bleach plus acid gives off chlorine gas. Vinegar is on their acid list.

## Prevention

Squeegee the glass — [how to prevent hard water stains on shower doors](/articles/how-to-prevent-hard-water-stains-on-shower-doors) — **and** wipe the track. Skip the channel wipe and you just moved the minerals into the U. If you do not know how hard the water is, [test it](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home).

## What not to do

- Do not walk the glass-door ladder (vinegar → CLR → razor) on this metal. That URL is the pane.
- Do not soak anodized or coated aluminum in citric, CLR, or HCl gel.
- Do not treat a floor leak as dirt. Clear the weep holes.
- Do not skip drying the channel.
- Do not gouge the slots with a screwdriver.
- Do not pop the door out on a guessed “lift and tilt.”

## FAQ

**The glass is still spotted. Did I do this wrong?**
You cleaned the wrong part. The pane is the [glass-door article](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-glass-shower-doors).

**Vinegar or citric in the track?**
Light film: grocery vinegar, short sit. Heavy crust on unlabeled metal: [food-grade citric acid](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00EYFKKZC?tag=hardwaterfi04-20), timed. Anodized or painted: mechanical, then stop. Comparison: [citric vs vinegar](/articles/citric-acid-vs-vinegar-for-limescale).

**Can I use CLR in the channel?**
No. CLR’s do-not-use list includes aluminum. The 2-minute glass bottle is the wrong job. [Vinegar vs CLR](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains) is not a track soak.

**The door dumps water on the floor after I clean it.**
Weep holes. Drainage, not dirt. Poke, rinse, test with a cup of water. Do not caulk them shut.

**Do I take the rollers off?**
Wipe in place. Open the door manual. DreamLine: do not fully disassemble the roller assemblies.
