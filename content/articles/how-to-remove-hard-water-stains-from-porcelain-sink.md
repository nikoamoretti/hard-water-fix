## Name the sink before you spray

**Porcelain enamel** is a glass-like coating over steel or cast iron. It looks glassy. Water beads. A fingernail does not scratch intact glaze. A chip that shows dark metal is enamel over iron or steel. Cast-iron enamel is this page.

**Stainless** is metal with visible grain. Grain lives on [how to remove hard water stains from a stainless steel sink](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-stainless-steel-sink).

**The tub** is a tub. That job lives on [how to remove hard water stains from a bathtub](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-bathtub).

**Marble** is veined stone. Stop. No vinegar. No household CLR. CLR stay-off: any natural stone, especially marble (even synthetic), and granite. There is no live marble how-to on this site. Neutral cleaner only.

**Fireclay** is a thick clay farmhouse body. No live fireclay slug. If the face is glazed and intact, treat it like this glaze and spot-test.

Worn, dull, or chipped enamel is not intact glaze. CLR: older sinks, tubs, and tiles may etch. Spot-test a hidden patch. If a chip shows bare metal, stop. CLR does not recommend the product on cast iron — the acids can compromise the metal. Acid the glaze, not the iron. If you cannot name it, do not acid it.

## Vinegar first on light film

Faint white haze you can barely feel. Not raised crust. Distilled white vinegar is about 5% acetic acid. The Conversation: vinegar reacts with and dissolves limescale. A sink basin holds a puddle. Use that. Do not soak overnight.

1. Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle.
2. Pool a thin layer on the floor of the basin. For walls, soak a paper towel and park it so the acid stays put.
3. Sit a few minutes. Re-wet if a towel starts to dry.
4. Wipe with a non-scratch sponge. Rinse. Dry the basin.

If two passes barely move the haze and a fingernail still catches white crust, stop stretching the vinegar. That is the gray-jug job.
