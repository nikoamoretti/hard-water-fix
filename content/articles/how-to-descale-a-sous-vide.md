## Food out. 50/50 vinegar bath. Pull the foot. Then a plain-water run.

Bag out of the water. Bag out of the room. You are cleaning the stick, not sous-vide-ing steak in salad dressing.

1. Unplug if the booklet says unplug first. Cool enough to handle. No hot splash on skin.
2. Empty the cook pot or tub. Soft wipe loose chalk on the skirt. No steel wool on coated metal.
3. Mix **equal parts** distilled white vinegar and water in a clean vessel deep enough to cover the skirt and impeller the way **your** sheet shows. Pantry bottle. Grocery aisle. No Amazon vinegar link on this page.
4. Clamp or hang the circulator in that bath. Set the temp the maker prints — Joule help: **140°F / 60°C**. Run for the time that FAQ or booklet names. Stay in the room.
5. Off. Unplug if required. Lift the stick. Empty the spent vinegar into the sink.
6. **Pull the magnetic foot / impeller** if your model has one. Soft brush the cavity behind it. Scale hides there and kills flow long before the skirt looks bad. Rinse the foot. Reseat it fully before the next cook.
7. **One plain-water run after.** Fill with plain water (prefer distilled for this rinse). Run the stick at a mild cook temp until the water and the skirt smell like water only — not pickle, not salad. If you still smell vinegar, dump and run again before food goes back.

Skip that rinse and the next bag tastes like vinaigrette. Food-contact gear: rinse until the smell is gone, then one clean water circulation.

## Hard-water signs on a circulator skirt

Know the film before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze on the skirt after the bath cools: early mineral film. Distilled cook-bath and a wipe may stretch the gap between full descales.
- Raised chalk a fingernail catches on the skirt or impeller: carbonate scale. On-label vinegar 50/50 at maker temp. Pull the foot.
- Weak circulation, louder pump, or temp hunting: scale may be choking the impeller cavity. Descale. Brush behind the magnetic foot. Then a plain-water run.
- Brown or rusty tint on the skirt: that can be iron or heater discoloration, not only calcium. Stay on vinegar at maker dilution first. Do not jump to bathroom lime gel unless the sheet allows a dilute pass.

This is still food-contact gear. Do not turn it into a [kettle](/articles/how-to-descale-an-electric-kettle) boil or a [food steamer](/articles/how-to-descale-a-food-steamer) reservoir job.

## Distilled cook-bath going forward — pantry only

Prevention is the next fill. Distilled water in the cook pot or tub slows the chalk on the skirt. It is the mineral-free bath when the booklet permits it and the tap tests hard — not a sanitizer, not a flavor add-in.

Buy a grocery gallon from the pantry aisle. **No Amazon distilled URL on this page.** Do not paste `B07VD4KN28` or purified `B07VC5RZHM`. Text only: distilled from the store. Same jug for the post-vinegar rinse run and for daily cook baths when your booklet allows distilled or demineralized water.

Habits that keep the skirt clear:

- Empty leftover bath water after the last cook of the day. Standing tap dries into fresh scale overnight.
- Wipe the cool skirt. Soft cloth. No metal scraper on coated metal.
- Pull and rinse the magnetic foot on a schedule when the water tests hard.
- Descale when flow slows, noise rises, or white crust shows — on the calendar your booklet prints if it has one.
- After every vinegar (or dilute CLR) pass, finish with that plain-water run before the next bag.

If you want the number behind “hard,” [test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home). Hard and very hard taps return crust faster. Soft municipal water stretches the gap between descales.
