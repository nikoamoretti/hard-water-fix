## Shut off. Cover the drain. Photograph orientation.

1. Shut off the hot and cold stops under the sink, or the house main. Open the faucet to bleed pressure.
2. Cover the drain with a rag or plug so a dropped screw, clip, or O-ring does not vanish.
3. Pull the handle and trim per the brand sheet. Photograph the stem orientation — hot/cold mark, notch toward the sink, flat — before the cartridge leaves the body.
4. Remove the retaining nut or clip the booklet shows. Pull the cartridge straight. Do not pry sideways against ceramic discs unless the sheet says so.
5. If the faucet body is full of grit, flush the open ports into a bucket **before** you put a clean cartridge back.

Ceramic-disc vs ball vs stem: different cartridges, different rebuild kits. Quote the series on the faucet or the old cartridge stamp. Guessing the wrong Delta or Moen number is how you buy the wrong part twice.

## Pantry vinegar — soak the removed cartridge 30–60 min

Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Pantry. No Amazon vinegar URL. Distilled water for a rinse, if you use it, is pantry text only — no Amazon gallon link on this page.

**Read the sheet for your faucet before any soak.** Brand care (Tap Warehouse–style cartridge descale, Serene Bathrooms cartridge-clean tips, Delta lime/rust/sand inspect notes) points at a **removed** cartridge in a cup — not acid poured into the open faucet body as a default.

1. Pour pantry distilled white vinegar into a cup or jar big enough to cover the cartridge ports and disc faces.
2. Soak the **removed** cartridge **30–60 minutes** for packed crust. Soft toothbrush on ports and screens. No steel wool. No scouring powder on plastic, rubber, or ceramic discs.
3. Rinse thoroughly. Pantry distilled rinse on the metal/plastic body if you want zero redeposit from tap rinse water.
4. Inspect: a cartridge that cleaned up and still rattles free can go back. Scored ceramic discs, cracked plastic, or shredded rubber seats are a **replace**, not another soak.
5. Light **silicone faucet grease** on O-rings and seals the sheet names. Hand-seat the cartridge in the photographed orientation. Nut or clip back. Trim on. Supplies on. Check mix and shutoff.

**Do not overnight-soak rubber seats in acid as a lazy default.** Rubber and long acid sits do not mix. Vinegar on the removed cartridge for 30–60 minutes — then rinse.

## Weak flow after a clean cartridge

If the cartridge ports are clear and the handle mixes correctly but the stream is still weak or forks sideways, the mineral job moved. Check the [faucet aerator](/articles/how-to-clean-a-clogged-faucet-aerator). Do not keep soaking a clean cartridge hoping the spout tip will clear.

## Keep it from packing again

After a clean pass, exercise the handle through hot and cold once a week if your tap is hard so the discs do not sit in one crusted position. Wipe the spout dry after use so less mineral walks back into the body. If the house is very hard, a softener or point-of-use filter is the long game — this page does not sell one.

When you deep-clean the aerator, remember the cartridge exists. Do not wait until the handle will not turn or the mix jumps from cold to scald.
