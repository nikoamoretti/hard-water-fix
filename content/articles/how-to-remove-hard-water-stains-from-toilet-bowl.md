## What that ring is (and what it is not)

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. When those minerals drop out, you get solid calcium carbonate — the same limescale as a shower door or kettle. In a toilet it stacks at the waterline.

Orange or brown below the waterline is often iron, not the white/tan ring. Do not treat it as this page’s job.

A faint film that vinegar fog lifts in one pass is pantry chemistry. A raised crust sitting in water is not. [Citric acid vs vinegar](/articles/citric-acid-vs-vinegar-for-limescale) is the light-film page. A bowl ring is usually too thick and too wet for either to be the hero.

## 1. Drop the water first

TOTO: if the waterline stain persists, remove some water, then reapply acid at full strength — the puddle dilutes it. CLR’s toilet how-to: shut the supply, flush, *then* apply. Martha Stewart: flush to lower the level; if staining sits above the waterline, shut the valve and flush again.

1. Open a window or run the fan. Gloves on. Keep kids and pets out. Hydrochloric acid is not vinegar.
2. Find the shutoff — usually left of the toilet, wall or floor (This Old House; Hunker).
3. Close it. Hunker: a football-shaped handle turns **clockwise until it stops** (several rotations). A lever handle turns **one quarter turn clockwise** until it sits perpendicular to the pipe. This Old House: clockwise until fully closed. Do not crank past the stop.
4. Flush and hold the handle. Sponge or cup out the remaining puddle so the ring is exposed. This Old House: leftover water always remains after a flush; sponge it into a bucket.

If someone already used bleach in this bowl, flush and rinse with plain water before any acid. Washington State DOH: bleach mixed with acid gives off chlorine gas. Toilet bowl cleaners are on their acid list.

## 3. Wet pumice only, intact glazed porcelain

What the gel leaves on ordinary glazed china is a job for a [toilet pumice stick](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000BPRDCW?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) — a Pumie-style scouring stick, not a pedicure stone.

Pumie (Summit Brands): **must be used with water**. Wet the stick first. Rub the edge back and forth. The paste that builds up is the cleaner. Rinse; store dry. Not for glass, fiberglass, plastics, or unbaked enamel. Test a small area — colored porcelain can be fragile. American Standard: a **wet** pumice stone when necessary. Kohler: soft abrasives when needed; strong abrasives scratch the glaze.

1. Keep the stick **and** the porcelain wet the whole time. Dry pumice scratches.
2. Light, even strokes on the remaining ring. Let the paste work. Do not grind.
3. No pumice on cracked glaze, unglazed ceramic, plastic tanks, plastic seats, or a super-smooth factory coating that bans grit.

## 4. Rinse, restore water, flush

Rinse the bowl. Open the shutoff (counterclockwise / lever back in line with the pipe). Let the tank fill. Flush. If a ridge is still there, one more **timed** gel pass — same clock — then wet pumice again. No invented “% removed.” No overnight acid bath.

## Bleach, CLR, and the pantry

**Bleach does not dissolve calcium carbonate.** TOTO: hard-water marks will not dissolve using bleach or detergents; they take a slightly acidic cleaner. USGS: vinegar (an acid) charges mineral particles so water can carry them away.

Never mix acid with bleach. If the bowl still smells like bleach, rinse until it doesn’t, then start.

CLR is **not** the bowl soak on this page. CLR’s own label is still **2 minutes**, then a cold rinse. Use it only if the stain is mixed scale **and** rust **above** the waterline (tank exterior, a dry under-rim patch). ## What not to do

- Do not skip dropping the water.
- Do not mix HCl gel (or vinegar, or CLR) with bleach.
- Do not dry-pumice, or pumice cracked glaze, plastic, or unglazed ceramic.
- Do not soak chrome, enamel, or the floor with bowl gel (The Works EPA label).
- Do not treat orange/brown iron as this ring.

## FAQ

**Why didn’t bleach take the ring off?**
It is not an acid that dissolves calcium carbonate. TOTO: hard-water marks will not dissolve using bleach.

**Do I have to shut the valve?**
If the ring is at or above the waterline, yes. Flushing alone leaves a puddle. Shut off (clockwise on a typical multi-turn valve), flush, sponge the rest so the gel sits on the stain.

**Will pumice scratch the bowl?**
Dry, or on plastic / cracked / unglazed / CEFIONTECT-style coatings: yes. Wet, on intact glazed porcelain: Pumie and American Standard both allow it. Stop if the glaze looks dull.

**Can I just use vinegar overnight?**
Light film, maybe. A thick wet ring is the gel’s job, on the label clock. Pantry acids are [citric vs vinegar](/articles/citric-acid-vs-vinegar-for-limescale), not this soak.

**What if the stain is orange?**
Often iron, not this mineral ring. Different chemistry. Do not merge the two.
