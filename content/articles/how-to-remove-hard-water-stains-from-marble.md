## Crust on top is not an etch

Hard-water marks on marble are minerals on the face. Countertop Advisor: when hard water evaporates, calcium and magnesium stay behind as a cloudy white ring. They sit on top. They have not eaten the polish yet.

An etch is different. Acid hits calcium carbonate and dissolves a thin layer of the stone. The dull spot after vinegar or CLR is a hole in the polish. NSI: etch marks are acids left on the surface of the stone. Contact a stone dealer or a professional stone restorer for refinishing or repolishing.

Do the wet/feel test before you pick a tool.

1. Wet the ring with a drop of distilled water.
2. Feel it with a fingernail.
3. Raised, chalky, catches a nail: crust on top. You can try to lift it.
4. Smooth and dull, same wet and dry: the polish is gone. That is an etch. Stop. Stone pro.

Do not keep scrubbing a smooth dull patch. A cloth will not restore the gloss. More acid will make the hole bigger.

## pH-neutral wipe first

Faint haze you can barely feel. Not raised crust.

NSI: clean stone with a neutral cleaner, stone soap, or a mild liquid dishwashing detergent and warm water. Then rinse. Then dry.

1. Damp a microfiber with a pH-neutral stone cleaner, or with [distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) if the film is light.
2. Wipe the faucet ring only.
3. Rinse with distilled. Tap rinse puts the same calcium and magnesium back on the polish.
4. Dry immediately with a second cloth.

## Baking-soda poultice on raised crust

Raised white crust a fingernail catches. Baking soda and water make a paste. That paste is mechanical. It is not acid.

1. Mix baking soda with a little water to a thick paste. Peanut-butter thick.
2. Spread it on the crust only.
3. Sit 5 to 10 minutes for a thin ring. Do not let it dry hard.
4. Wipe with a damp cloth. Do not scrub dry powder into the polish. NSI: scouring powders or creams may scratch certain stones.
5. Rinse with [distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). Dry now.

Do not mix baking soda with vinegar to “boost” it. Acid plus bicarbonate is salt, water, and CO2. The fizz is gas, not extra cleaning. The poultice is not that fizz. That mix myth already lives on [baking soda vs vinegar](/articles/baking-soda-vs-vinegar-hard-water-stains).

## Plastic scraper on leftover crust

Paste lifted most of it. A thin mineral skin is still raised.

Use a plastic scraper. Not a razor. Not steel wool. Countertop Advisor: skip steel wool and Magic Erasers on marble. They dull the polish.

1. Spot-test a hidden edge of the same finish.
2. Hold the scraper almost flat. Work the crust, not the stone.
3. Lift only what stands up.
4. Rinse with [distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). Dry immediately.

Stop the second you see new haze. New haze that stays smooth when wet is damage, not leftover scale.

## Honed vs polished

Polished marble is a mirror. Honed marble is matte on purpose.

Friction that lifts crust on a polish can leave shiny spots on honed. Countertop Advisor: do not rub honed marble hard to chase the last shadow. You burnish a patch that will not match.

On honed: pH-neutral wipe, poultice, plastic on raised crust only. Light pressure. Stop early. A shiny island in a honed field is finish wear. Stone pro.

On polished: still no acid. Still no powder cleanser. Still dry immediately.
