## What hard water leaves in a washer

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heat those minerals and they drop out as calcium carbonate scale. Soap plus calcium is scum. Hard water also takes more detergent on actual loads. That is residue, not a detergent page.

Hard-water scale is not the same as front-loader mildew on the bellows. Mold smell is a wipe-and-dry gasket job, not this mineral cycle.

## Why vinegar and citric are the wrong default

Bosch US: “Avoid using citric acid.” Do not descale with household acid or vinegar: “These can attack the rubber parts of the machine.” Household-products page: they “may degrade the rubber parts of your machine over time.” Bosch names its own descaler. Mixed lines. Not all Bosch. Check **your** book.

Whirlpool US: vinegar’s acidity “can cause damage to washing machine components, particularly rubber seals and hoses.” Adding vinegar “directly to the washing machine drum can potentially lead to costly repairs or worse, permanent damage.”

Maytag US: “No, you should not pour vinegar in your washing machine drum.” Acidity “can damage rubber seals, hoses and other components.” Select Maytag washers have a Clean Washer with affresh cycle. Not all Maytag.

Affresh’s sanitize article still lists vinegar as one sanitizer option. Whirlpool, Maytag, and Bosch warn the drum path. This page follows the stricter seal warning plus the Affresh box. Citric vs vinegar belongs on kettles and coffee, not this gasket: [citric acid vs vinegar for limescale](/articles/citric-acid-vs-vinegar-for-limescale).

Do not copy the UK 90 °C citric-drum recipe onto a US front-loader. Whitegoods Help (UK) is the trap: 50–100 g food-grade citric in an empty 90 or 95 °C cottons cycle. Their page, not these steps.

## How to run the empty cycle

Follow the box plus **your** manual. Affresh and the OEM clock can differ slightly. Do not invent a blend.

[Affresh washing machine cleaner](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00C91Q86I?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) is the queued SKU because it is the US warranty-safe empty-cycle path, not a bake-off. Affresh product page: all makes and models, including front-load, top-load, and HE. Affresh ingredients page and the current washer SDS: sodium percarbonate (oxygen-based bleach, no chlorine) and sodium carbonate (washing soda), plus PEG, glucono-delta-lactone, and sodium acetate. Not citric. Citric is on Affresh’s dishwasher and coffee lists. Those are other appliances.

1. Empty the machine. Affresh and Whirlpool: no clothes in the basket. Maytag: top-load and front-load “will most likely require different steps.” Check **your** Use and Care Guide.

2. Place **one** tablet of [Affresh washing machine cleaner](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00C91Q86I?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) in the empty drum, not the dispenser. Affresh: “place affresh tablet inside your empty washing machine (not the dispenser).” Whirlpool and Maytag: same, washer basket only.

3. Pick the cycle **your** machine names. Affresh and Maytag: Clean Washer if it has one, or Normal or Heavy and hot. Whirlpool: Clean Washer; if none, highest water and hottest temperature. Some Bosch units name Drum Clean. That is their cycle, not a citric scoop. If the box and the book disagree, follow both: one tablet, their cycle, their temperature.

4. Start the washer. Let the cycle finish. Whirlpool: do not open the door or lid during Clean Washer. If a tablet is left: check the expiration date, run hot water at the nearest sink first so the line is actually hot, and do not interrupt the cycle.

5. Wipe leftover tablet residue with a clean microfiber cloth. Affresh: the tablets are time-release; residue in the bottom of the tub is normal.

6. Repeat only if Affresh says to. Affresh: if you have never cleaned the washer, it has been more than 6 months, or there is excessive residue or odor. Up to 3 consecutive cleaning cycles in extreme cases. Affresh frequency: monthly or every 30 wash cycles. Whirlpool: once a month, when the clean-washer light is on, and/or if odor is present.

## After the cycle

Wipe the gasket. Leave the door or lid ajar if the brand says so.

Affresh, Whirlpool, Maytag, and Bosch US all want the gasket wiped and the door or lid left open so the drum can dry. Affresh: wipe the gasket after laundry; wipe dispensers weekly. Maytag: lift the edge of a front-load seal. Bosch US: leave the detergent drawer open too.

Mold smell after that is still a wipe-and-dry gasket job, not a second mineral cycle.

## FAQ

**Can I use vinegar to clean the washer?**
Not as the drum path. Whirlpool and Maytag: do not add vinegar to the drum; acidity can weaken rubber seals and hoses. Affresh’s sanitize article still lists vinegar as a sanitizer option. Follow the stricter seal warning. Vinegar on some removable dispensers and the exterior only.

**Can I use citric like the UK blogs?**
No. Bosch US: household citric or vinegar can attack rubber. Whitegoods Help’s 90 °C / 50–100 g citric cycle is the SERP this title skips. Citric for kettles: [citric acid vs vinegar for limescale](/articles/citric-acid-vs-vinegar-for-limescale).

**Top-load or front-load? HE?**
Affresh: all machines, including HE, front-load, and top-load. Maytag: the two layouts most likely need different steps. Some machines name Clean Washer, Tub Clean, or Drum Clean. Check **your** manual. Not “all Whirlpool.” Not “all Bosch.”

**Is this laundry detergent?**
No. One tablet, empty cycle, then wipe. USGS: hard water needs more detergent on actual loads. That is a dose on the detergent box, not this page.

**How often?**
Affresh: monthly or every 30 wash cycles. Up to three consecutive cycles only if Affresh names it (never cleaned, more than 6 months, or heavy residue or odor). Whirlpool: monthly, when the light is on, or if it smells.

**Is this the same as dishwasher cleaner?**
No. Affresh washer tablets are sodium percarbonate and sodium carbonate. Affresh dishwasher tablets are a different formula (that list includes citric). Finish dishwasher cleaner is another job.
