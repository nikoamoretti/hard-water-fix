White spots on a plate are usually dried calcium and magnesium. A cloudy wine glass might be the same film — or the glass itself may already be etched. Vinegar tells you which in about a minute. If the acid lifts the haze, you can soak the rest of the load and stop it coming back. If vinegar does nothing, stop buying removers. Etching is permanent.

## The vinegar wipe (do this first)

Whirlpool’s cloudy-glass test: dip a cloth in white vinegar, rub a small patch in a circle. Maytag says the same thing. CLR’s drinking-glass FAQ is blunter: if the descaler does not move the cloud at all, the glass is etched.

| What happens | What it is | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Patch turns clear | Mineral film (hardness spots) | Soak the load in warm vinegar, then change how you wash |
| Nothing changes, even wet | Etching (or silica film) | Do not soak longer. Cut detergent, heat, and pre-rinsing. Replace the glass if you care how it looks. |
| Rainbow / oil-slick shimmer | Often etching | Same as above. More acid will not polish it. |

Spots you can scratch with a fingernail are almost always film. A smooth, even frost that survives vinegar is damage. Dishwasher etching is not “more hard water.” Whirlpool: detergent plus **soft** water, high heat, and a light soil load erodes the glass. Hard-water film sits on top. Soft-water etch is the glass.

## How to remove the spots you can still save

Distilled white vinegar, warm, 20–30 minutes. Whirlpool’s own soak is 15 minutes; we give it the extra time a kitchen load usually needs. Not boiling. Not overnight.

1. Sink or dishpan. Glasses on their side so the film is under the liquid, not beading on the rim.
2. Cover with warm distilled white vinegar. Straight vinegar for crusty spots; 50/50 with hot water for a light film.
3. 20–30 minutes. Do not add bleach, baking soda, or dishwasher detergent to the soak. Acid plus bleach is chlorine gas (Washington State DOH). Baking soda just cancels the acid.
4. Soft sponge or microfiber. No scouring powder on glass.
5. Rinse in hot water. **Dry by hand.** Air-drying is how the spots return before you put the plate away.

Stainless and ceramic take the same soak. Skip silver, aluminum, painted mugs, and unsealed natural-stone serving pieces.

If one soak lifts most of it and leaves a ghost, repeat once. If the ghost is unchanged after two soaks, you are in etching territory. Say so and stop.

Citric acid powder will do this job with less smell: a couple of tablespoons in a sink of hot water, same 20–30 minutes, same hand dry. It is not required if vinegar is already working.

## If vinegar did nothing

Stop. There is no honest “glass restore” bottle for etched dishwasher glass. Maytag: lower rinse-aid if the water is already soft, use less detergent, shorter and cooler cycles, and stop pre-rinsing so the detergent has something to work on besides the silica in the glass. That prevents the next set from etching. It will not un-frost the set you already have.

A rainbow haze plus a failed vinegar wipe is the same answer. Do not put CLR on dinner plates. Vinegar first, then accept the etch.

## What not to do

- Do not treat etching like a stain.
- Do not mix vinegar, rinse aid, or booster with bleach.
- Do not air-dry rescued dishes in hard water.
- Do not fill the detergent cup “because the water is hard” if the vinegar test already failed — that is how you etch the next load.
- Do not run a Magic Eraser on wine glasses.

## FAQ

**Will vinegar ruin my dishwasher?**
A bowl of vinegar on the top rack of an empty hot cycle is a common scale rinse. It is not a substitute for the monthly machine cleaner, and it should never share the box with bleach. For the dishes themselves, soak in the sink.

**Rinse aid or booster?**
Rinse aid) for spots that form as water dries. Booster) for film that forms during the wash in hard water. Hard water often wants both.

**Why are only the glasses cloudy?**
Glass shows mineral film and etching first. Plates hide it in the glaze. Test a glass. Believe that result for the whole load.

**Is this the same as cloudy-glasses etching?**
Same diagnostic, narrower query. The dedicated etching piece is next in the queue. The test does not change.
