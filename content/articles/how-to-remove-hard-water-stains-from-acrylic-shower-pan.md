## Vinegar on a cloth. 15–20 minutes. Soft wipe.

Pantry distilled white vinegar. Soft cotton or microfiber cloth. No Amazon vinegar URL. No “improve it” with lemon, baking-soda grit, or a second acid.

1. Dry the pan first. Wipe standing water so the vinegar sits on film, not diluted puddles.
2. Soak a soft cloth in undiluted distilled white vinegar (5% pantry bottle). Lay the cloth flat on the stained zones — footprints, splash rings near the drain, the corners where water pools.
3. Sit **15–20 minutes**. Stay in the room. Do not let the cloth dry out; re-wet if the edge crisps.
4. Lift the cloth. Soft wipe in straight lines. No circles with pressure. No green Scotch-Brite. No pumice. No Bar Keepers Friend powder on acrylic.
5. Rinse with warm water. Soft dry with a clean towel so new mineral film does not flash-dry on the floor.
6. Check the **weep channels** at the curb / threshold and around any glass track that drains into the pan. Soft toothbrush or a folded cloth corner only. Keep them open. Do not pack grit, wax, or sealant into the weep holes.

One pass removes fresh white haze. Raised chalk that a fingernail catches may need a second 15–20 minute cloth. If the stain is under the gel coat or the gloss is already abraded, stop. More acid will not polish plastic back to new.

Never mix leftover vinegar with bleach. Poison Control: bleach plus an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Leave the area. Fresh air. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

## Hard-water signs on an acrylic shower pan

Know the film before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze after the pan dries: early mineral film. Vinegar cloth and a squeegee habit after each shower stretch the gap between deep cleans.
- Raised chalk a fingernail catches near the drain or curb: carbonate scale. On-cloth vinegar pass. Soft wipe only.
- Rough “sandpaper” feel underfoot that does not wipe off: can be etched or abraded gel coat from past green pads or powder cleansers — not only scale. Cleaning will not restore worn gloss.
- Brown or rusty tint at the drain flange: can be iron or metal under a failing seal, not only calcium. Stay on vinegar cloth first. Do not jump to bathroom lime gel on acrylic.
- Persistent wet stripe along the curb after the pan looks dry: check weep channels. A clogged weep hole holds water that scales faster than the open floor.

## Weep channels stay open

The curb / threshold and many glass tracks drain through small weep holes into the pan. Soft-clear them with a folded cloth corner or a soft toothbrush after the vinegar pass. Do not force a pick that scratches acrylic. Do not fill them with caulk “to look cleaner.” Blocked weeps leave a permanent wet band that grows fresh hard-water film and can feed leaks behind the curb.

If water sits against the glass track after every shower, clear the weeps before you blame the vinegar cloth for “not working.” Scale on a wet stripe returns until the water can leave.
