## Vinegar at the exterior base and (lid off) the mounting sleeve. Short contact. Rinse. Dry.

Pantry distilled white vinegar. Cloth or **nylon brush** at the **exterior pivot** and, lid off, the **mounting sleeve**. **Short contact** — a few minutes, not an overnight tank dump. Rinse. Dry. No Amazon vinegar URL. No distilled Amazon URL (NONE_QUALIFY — never `B07VD4KN28` or `B07VC5RZHM`). No Lime-A-Way. No abrasives on chrome.

1. Confirm the part is the **flush handle**, not the bowl ring, not the rim jets, not the siphon jet. Wipe grit off the exterior base dry.
2. Dampen a soft cloth or **nylon brush** with pantry distilled white vinegar (5% bottle). Coat the **exterior base** — the ring where the handle meets the tank, the underside of the paddle, and the visible pivot. Short damp contact. Stay in the room. Re-wet if it dries. Do **not** wrap the handle in a soaked towel and walk away.
3. **Lid off.** Set the tank lid on a towel. Wipe the **mounting sleeve** on the inside of the tank hole — the plastic or metal collar behind the handle. Vinegar on the cloth or nylon brush. Short contact on the sleeve you can see. Do **not** pour vinegar into the tank water and call it this clean. That is a tank soak (#36), not a handle wipe.
4. Nylon brush the crust a fingernail catches at the base and around the sleeve. Small passes. No steel wool. No green pad on chrome. Do not grind the porcelain hole.
5. **Rinse.** Fresh damp cloth and clean water until the handle and sleeve do not smell like pickle. Keep acid off adjacent stone or plated supply trim you are not cleaning.
6. **Dry.** Soft towel the exterior pivot, the paddle, and the sleeve. A wet base flash-dries a fresh chalk ring overnight and sticks again in the morning.
7. Optional pantry **distilled water** final wipe so tap spots do not flash-dry back onto the chrome — grocery jug only. No Amazon jug link. NONE_QUALIFY for distilled.

One short vinegar pass at the base and sleeve clears most fresh film. Raised crust that survives two short vinegar passes on **chrome** can take the short CLR step below. Vinegar will not rebuild a bent lever, a cracked paddle, or a rusted-through cheap plating. That is the replace decision.

If you need to snug or back off the mounting nut to reach the sleeve, stop and remember the thread: **most mounting nuts are reverse-threaded**. Korky: turn **clockwise to loosen**, **counterclockwise to tighten**. Forcing “lefty-loosey” the usual way can tighten the nut into thin porcelain. Snug, not cranked. Chain and flapper stay where they are unless you are replacing the lever.
