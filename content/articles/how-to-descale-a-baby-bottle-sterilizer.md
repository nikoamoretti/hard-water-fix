## Name it first. Heating plate and reservoir — not bottles, not a kettle.

Steam sterilizers boil a measured fill on a metal plate or in a small reservoir, then flood the chamber with steam. Scale lives on that plate and in that cup. The bottles ride above the steam. They are not the descaling target.

Identify the machine before you pour acid.

- Countertop steam sterilizer with a heating plate or small water reservoir, baskets for bottles and parts: this page.
- Electric kettle with a hidden element or a visible coil: different appliance. Different fill. Not this page.
- CPAP humidifier chamber: different medical device. Different care sheet. Not this page.
- Microwave steam bag or boil-in-a-pot method: no heating plate to descale. Skip this page.
- Dishwasher sanitize cycle: that is a dishwasher. Not a steam-plate reservoir.

If the booklet bans vinegar, follow the booklet. Some makers name only their own descaler. Quote that line and use what they allow. Do not invent a stronger acid because the crust looks thick.

## Why the plate crusts. Why vinegar at maker dilution.

Steam leaves minerals behind. Every cycle that used hard tap water concentrates calcium and magnesium on the hot surface. The white ring and the chalky film are carbonate scale. Thin haze first. Then a fingernail-catching crust. Then longer heat times and error lights on some units.

Vinegar is dilute acetic acid. At the dilution your maker prints, it softens that film so a wipe or an empty cycle can lift it. Stronger-than-booklet acid is not faster care. It is a warranty and coating risk.

Two booklet examples — always follow YOUR sheet, not these numbers if they disagree:

- Philips Avent-style guidance often lists about **12 ml white vinegar + 120 ml water**, then a short empty run (commonly about five minutes) with no bottles in the chamber.
- Momcozy-style hard-water guidance often lists a **1:1 vinegar-to-water** empty clean about every four weeks when the tap is hard.

Those are booklet examples only. Open the card that came with your unit. Match that dilution, that dwell, and that empty-cycle rule. Pantry white vinegar. No Amazon link for the vinegar. Do not “improve” the mix with lemon juice, baking-soda fizz, or a second acid.

Never put bleach in the reservoir. Never mix leftover vinegar with bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

## Empty the chamber. Vinegar at the maker dilution. Short run.

Bottles out. Parts out. Baskets out if the sheet says so. You are cleaning the plate and the reservoir, not sterilizing gear in acid.

1. Unplug if the booklet says unplug first. Cool plate. No hot splash.
2. Wipe loose chalk with a soft cloth. Do not gouge the coating with steel wool or a metal scraper.
3. Mix white vinegar and water exactly as YOUR sheet prints. Avent-style example: 12 ml vinegar + 120 ml water. Momcozy-style example: 1:1. Measure. Do not free-pour “enough.”
4. Pour into the reservoir or onto the plate only as the booklet directs. No bottles. No nipples. No pacifiers in the chamber for this run.
5. Run the empty descaling or clean cycle the maker names — often a short steam or a timed five-minute style run. Stay in the room. Watch for overflow.
6. When it ends, discard the spent liquid. Wipe the plate and reservoir walls. Soft cloth. Lift what softened.
7. Check the corners of the plate and the fill-line marks. Scale hides there after a light pass.

If the booklet bans vinegar and names only a house descaler, stop here and use that product as written. Do not substitute CLR, a Keurig descaling bottle, Breville powder, a citric money SKU, or a humidifier demineralization cartridge. Those are other machines and other chemistries.

Spot-check after the wipe. If white film remains but the plate coating looks intact, one more on-label empty vinegar run is usually the next step — not a switch to bathroom limescale gel.

## One plain-water cycle after. Then dry.

Acid smell and acid film must leave before the next sterilizing load. Skip this rinse and the next steam cycle can carry vinegar odor onto bottles and parts.

1. Fill with the plain water volume the sheet uses for a normal cycle — preferably [distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) for this rinse pass.
2. Run one empty steam cycle. No bottles. No parts.
3. Discard that water. Wipe the plate dry. Wipe the reservoir dry. Lid ajar until cool and dry if the booklet allows.
4. Smell the chamber. Water only. Not salad dressing.

If you still smell vinegar, run a second plain-water empty cycle. Do not load bottles until the chamber smells like water. Dry matters. Lingering wet film on a warm plate becomes fresh spots by evening when the tap is hard.

## Distilled fill going forward. Rinse the plate.

Prevention is the boring win. Tap hardness put the crust there. Distilled fill slows the return.

Rinse bottles and parts per your washer sheet — this page is the sterilizer plate, not a bottle-washing guide.

Habits that keep the plate clear:

- Use distilled or the water grade your maker names for every sterilizing fill when the tap tests hard.
- Empty leftover water after the last cycle of the day. Standing water dries into fresh scale overnight.
- Wipe the plate when cool. Soft cloth. No abrasive pad on a coated heater.
- Descale on the calendar your booklet prints — Momcozy-style hard-water notes often land near every four weeks. Soft water can wait longer. Hard water cannot.
- After any descale, finish with that plain-water empty cycle before the next baby load.

If you want the number behind “hard,” [test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home). The test decides how often you open the vinegar bottle — not whether you skip the plain-water rinse. Hard and very hard taps return crust faster than soft municipal water.

## Hard-water signs on a sterilizer plate

Know the film before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze after the chamber cools: early mineral film. Distilled fill and a wipe may be enough between full descale days.
- Raised chalk a fingernail catches on the heating plate: carbonate scale. On-label vinegar empty run.
- Longer cycle times, weak steam, or a heater warning after months of tap fills: scale may be insulating the plate. Descale on the booklet schedule. Then one plain-water cycle.
- Brown or rusty tint in the reservoir: that can be iron or heater discoloration, not only calcium. Stay on vinegar at maker dilution first. Do not jump to CLR.

This is still the sterilizer plate. Bottle cloudiness from dishwasher hard water is a different job. Keep the bottles out of the acid run.

## Booklet first. Then pantry vinegar.

Makers disagree on vinegar. Some print a dilution. Some ban household acids and sell a packet. Some only say “descale regularly” without a recipe. Hierarchy is simple:

1. Your printed sheet wins.
2. If the sheet allows white vinegar, use the printed ratio. Avent-style 12 ml + 120 ml and Momcozy-style 1:1 are examples for hard-water households — not universal law.
3. If the sheet bans vinegar, do not argue with the internet. Use the named descaler or the named method only.
4. After any acid or descaler pass, one plain-water empty cycle. Prefer distilled for that rinse and for daily fills when the tap is hard.

Write the date you descaled on a tape strip under the unit if you forget calendars. Four weeks is a common hard-water reminder in Momcozy-style notes. Soft water stretches. Very hard water shortens. The hardness cutoffs in the lede are the reference for what “hard” means — not a softener sales pitch.

## When the crust will not lift

Heavy scale that survives one on-label vinegar empty run usually needs a second pass at the same dilution — not a stronger acid. Repeat only as the booklet allows. Soak times longer than the sheet prints are not a free upgrade. They are a coating gamble.

If the plate coating is peeling, the heater error light stays on, or the unit will not heat after cleaning, stop and contact the maker. Do not chip at the element with a knife. Do not drill into the reservoir seam.

Warped plastic, burned smell, or scorched marks on the plate are damage, not mineral film. Descaling will not fix a failed heater. Replace the unit if the maker says so. Keep the old booklet until the replacement arrives so you do not guess the new dilution from memory.
