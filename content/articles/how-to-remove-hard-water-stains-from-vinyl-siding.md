Do this in daylight, on a dry uncleaned streak.

## Wet the siding first. Vinegar on white streaks.

Faint white film. Not orange. Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. About 5% acetic acid. Zolton Cohen (MLive, former ASHI inspector): the spots are hard-water minerals left after the water dries; they are mostly alkaline, so an acid is the chemical opposite. Distilled white vinegar is acidic enough to break them down and mild enough not to hurt the siding — still test a scrap or an out-of-the-way course in case it affects the color.

Work in shade if you can. Heat plus leftover acid is how people mark vinyl.

1. Wet the siding with a garden hose first. Top-down. Gentle. Do not shoot water up under the laps.
2. Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Spray the *white* streaks. Or wet a paper towel and park it on a stubborn patch.
3. Keep it wet a few minutes (Cohen). Spray again. Soft-bristle brush or a nonabrasive pad. No stiff brush. Vinyl Siding Institute: stiff bristles can change the gloss and leave a splotch.
4. Rinse — gently — from the top down, as rain would descend (Cohen). Never train the hose upward into the bottom or vertical laps. Vinyl is designed to keep out water that falls from the top down. Water blasted up can leak behind.
5. If the deposits are not soft enough, Cohen: let the vinegar soak a while longer. Particularly stubborn spots: tape paper towels, soak them with vinegar, press so they cling, leave several hours, then rinse top-down.

That is the siding path. Distilled water is not a hose rinse. You are not filling a wall with distilled. The garden hose is the rinse. Do not buy a distilled jug as a required product for this job.

Two failed wet vinegar passes and a fingernail still catches white crust: that is leftover calcium, not overnight vinegar on the whole elevation.

## Do not pressure-wash the laps. Wash the whole wall.

CertainTeed: they do not recommend power washing vinyl or polymer siding — it can cause moisture intrusion, damage, and/or discoloration. A garden hose and a soft-bristle car-wash brush is the care path (Vinyl Siding Institute). Pressure washers can drive water behind the laps.

Vinyl sheds rain downward. Cohen: if you train the hose so it shoots water upward into the bottom or vertical laps, it might cause leaks. Always rinse gently from the top down.

A clean rectangle next to a dirty wall means wash the whole wall. Spot-cleaning mineral on faded vinyl leaves a bright patch. VSI: when washing the entire house with detergent, start at the bottom and work up to prevent streaking. That is the soap wash. The acid *rinse* is still top-down so you are not forcing water into the joints.

Stay off window glass, painted trim, and the plant bed. Cover shrubs if runoff will hit them.

## If the streaks come back

Sprinklers that hit the wall will keep writing the same arcs. Aim the heads off the siding. Well water with iron will keep writing orange until the supply changes. [Test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home). This page does not size a softener.

## FAQ

**Will vinegar take sprinkler spots off vinyl siding?**
White calcium film, often. Cohen: distilled white vinegar, a few minutes, soft brush, rinse top-down. Two failed wet passes means leftover crust, not overnight vinegar on the whole elevation.

**Can I use CLR on vinyl siding?**
Only leftover white calcium, and only if the vinyl maker and the CLR jug both allow it. Vinyl is plastic. CLR lists plastics on the stay-off. Two minutes. Do not let it dry. Rinse immediately. Off landscaping. Off window glass.

**The streak is orange. Vinegar or CLR?**
Neither as the plan. That is iron. [Iron Out](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07N4KGV2Q?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) 2-pack only. Never mixed with CLR or vinegar. Never bleach.

**Can I mix Iron Out with vinegar to get calcium and rust at once?**
No. Iron Out SDS: contact with acids liberates toxic gas. Sequential jobs if you have both colors — rinse hard between them.

**Why not a pressure washer?**
CertainTeed: power washing can drive moisture behind vinyl. Cohen: never shoot water upward into the laps. Garden hose, top-down, gentle.

**Why not distilled water for the rinse?**
The wall is outdoors. The hose is the rinse. Distilled is a glass last-pass, not a siding product. Do not buy a distilled jug for this page.

**Is this the window method?**
No. House windows live on that other URL. Frames, glass, and etch are a different job.
