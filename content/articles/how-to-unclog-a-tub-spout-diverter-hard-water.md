## Two spout types. Shut off. Pull the spout.

Moen Solutions: look at the small hole on the underside, near the wall.

- **Set-screw slip-fit (CC).** You see a screw. Moen: 5/32" hex wrench. Loosen until the spout slides off the 1/2 in copper. Home Depot: score the caulk, then pull it straight off with both hands.
- **Threaded IPS.** No screw. Moen: unthread counterclockwise. Home Depot: two hands first. Cloth on the jaws if you need pliers. Bare metal scars chrome.

Dalmatian Plumbing: most tub spouts either screw off counterclockwise or release on a set screw underneath.

1. Shut off the water. Rick's Plumbing: tub and shower shutoffs, usually behind an access panel.
2. Score the caulk at the wall. Home Depot names that step on both types.
3. Slip-fit: loosen the hex screw. Slide the spout off. Do not twist the copper stub-out in the wall.
4. IPS: unscrew counterclockwise. Rag on the spout if pliers. Home Depot: rag or tape on the jaws.
5. Photograph the stub-out. Smooth 1/2 in copper is slip-fit. Male threads are IPS. The Moen 3801 later on this page is slip-fit only.

## Vinegar soak of the removed spout. Then work the knob.

**Removed spout only.** Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Pantry. No Amazon link.

Rick's Plumbing: submerge the pulled spout in equal parts white vinegar and water for **30 to 60 minutes**. Soft brush if crust remains. Then test the gate by hand. It should move without grinding.

Dalmatian Plumbing splits mineral on the gate vs worn rubber. Calcium, magnesium, and iron make a white or orange crust that binds the moving parts. Their vinegar clock on a removed spout is 2–4 hours. WOWOW Faucets soaks a bag on the still-mounted spout for 4–12 hours. Some plumber pages go hours. **This page is 30–60 minutes on the removed spout, then work the knob.** Do not hang a vinegar bag on the spout as the [shower-head vinegar](/articles/how-to-clean-a-shower-head-with-vinegar) how-to. That is a different fixture.

USGS: vinegar is an acid; the acidity helps dissolve mineral so water can carry it away.

1. Bowl in the tub. Spout fully under the liquid.
2. Distilled white vinegar from the pantry. Rick's mix is 50/50 with water. Chrome can take the pantry bottle straight. Specialty finishes stay 50/50.
3. **30–60 minutes.** Not overnight on the rubber gate.
4. Soft toothbrush on the gate and the track. No wire wheel. No scouring powder.
5. **Work the lift-rod by hand.** Up and down. Rick's: it should move smoothly. WOWOW: work the knob after the soak to clear loosened scale. Do not snap a plastic gate.
6. Rinse until it smells like water.

Never mix vinegar and bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids, and rust-removal products too. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

## Gate cracked or collapsed: replace the spout

Dalmatian: the gate is usually rubber or plastic. Over time it goes brittle, warped, or coated. Once it cannot seal, water keeps coming from the spout with the knob up. Most tub-spout diverters are not meant for an internal rebuild. Replace the spout.

Rick's: if water still trickles after a thorough clean, the rubber seal or gate is worn. Cleaning will not rebuild it.

WOWOW: cracked knob, gate that will not hold pressure, water from both outlets, vinegar that did not free it — buy a new spout. The gate is molded into most modern spouts.

## Reinstall. Test. Water from a clean gate is a shower valve.

Slide or thread the cleaned spout back on. Tighten the set screw if it is slip-fit. Restore water. Pull the lift-rod. Water should go to the shower.

A small bypass trickle is common on some pull-up designs. A steady stream with a **clean, intact gate** is not this page. Dalmatian: a failed pressure-balance or thermostatic valve behind the wall can keep sending water out the spout even after a new spout. ## FAQ

**Is a jammed lift-rod a dead shower valve or a packed gate?**

Look at the gate. Dalmatian: mineral crust binds the moving parts; worn rubber will not seal even when the knob moves. Rick's: stiffness is almost always scale around the gate. Pull the spout. Soak it. If the stream still dumps from the spout with a **clean, intact gate**, that is a shower valve, not this page.

**Slip-fit or IPS — same soak?**

Yes. Pull it first. Vinegar soak. Work the knob. Moen: screw in the underside hole is slip-fit. No screw is IPS. Replacement SKUs are not interchangeable.

**How long is the vinegar soak?**

Removed spout: **30–60 minutes** (Rick's Plumbing). Dalmatian goes 2–4 hours. WOWOW’s bag-on-spout clock is 4–12 hours. This page stays 30–60, then work the knob. Not overnight on the rubber gate.

**Can I bag vinegar on the spout like a shower head?**

No. Pull the spout. Soak the removed spout. The [shower-head vinegar](/articles/how-to-clean-a-shower-head-with-vinegar) bag is a different fixture.

**Can I use CLR on a matte black or brass spout?**

No. CLR lists chrome and bans brass, copper, aluminum, and coated or sealed surfaces. CLR can pit brass. Two-minute chrome-only clock, then a hard rinse. Not overnight on the rubber gate. Not matte black.

**The gate is cracked. More acid?**

No. Dalmatian and WOWOW: a cracked or collapsed gate is a new spout. [Moen 3801](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B001A05LVC?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) is 1/2 in copper set-screw only. Not IPS.

**Can I mix vinegar or CLR with bleach to get scale and germs?**

No. Poison Control: bleach plus an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar and rust-removal products. Leave the area. Fresh air. **1-800-222-1222**.
