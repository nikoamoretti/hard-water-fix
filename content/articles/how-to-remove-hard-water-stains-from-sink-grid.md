## Pull the grid. Pull the rubber feet.

A grid protects the basin floor. It also sits in standing water. Mineral coats every wire. Cleaning it in the bowl just moves the film around.

1. Lift the grid out. Do not scrub it while it is still in the basin.
2. **Pull the rubber feet separately.** They pop off most stainless grids. Mineral hides under the caps. Feet go in their own vinegar bowl. Do not soak rubber in CLR.
3. Wash food off with mild dish soap first. Scum shields mineral.
4. Close the drain or drop a towel. Small feet go there.

Stay off the basin job. The [stainless steel sink](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-stainless-steel-sink) page is grain and Soft Cleanser. This is the rack.

## Vinegar soak of the detached grid

**Detached grid only.** Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Pantry. No Amazon link.

Vinegar is acetic acid. The acidity dissolves mineral so water can carry it away. Pantry acid vs the labeled bottle lives on [vinegar vs CLR](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains). Do not retread it here.

1. Plastic tub, or the empty rinsed sink as a tray. Grid fully under the liquid.
2. Distilled white vinegar. Enough to cover the wires.
3. Soak **30–60 minutes**. Not overnight as the default. Re-wet any wire that sits above the line.
4. Soft toothbrush on the crossings. No steel wool. Particles from steel wool adhere and rust.
5. Soak the rubber feet in the same vinegar, short. Rinse. Do not leave them in CLR.

Never mix vinegar and bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

No citric on this page. No Granite Gold. No Lime-A-Way. Do not pour baking soda into the vinegar and call the fizz a descale.

## Rust on the wire only: Iron Out

White crust is calcium. Orange on the wire is rust. Different job. [CLR vs Iron Out](/articles/clr-vs-iron-out) is the split. Do not mix them.

1. Ventilate. Gloves. Pre-test. Do not mix with other chemicals. Iron Out SDS: contact with acids liberates toxic gas. Household CLR is an acid. Vinegar is an acid. Rinse the grid first until it does not smell like vinegar or CLR.

## Distilled rinse and dry

Tap rinse puts calcium and magnesium back as spots when it evaporates.

1. Final rinse with [Amazon Grocery Distilled Water, 1 gal](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). No minerals in the rinse.
2. Towel-dry every wire. Crossings too.
3. Dry the rubber feet. Snap them back on dry.
4. Set the grid back in a dry basin.

Skip the distilled rinse and the film returns the same afternoon.
