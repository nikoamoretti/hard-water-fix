## What you are dissolving

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heat them and you get solid calcium carbonate — limescale — on heaters, shower jets, and glasses. USGS even names the home coffee maker. The acid charges the mineral so water can carry it away.

**Vinegar** is acetic acid already diluted. Harvard T.H. Chan: the FDA requires vinegar to contain at least **4%** acetic acid; household distilled white is the **5%** bottle people clean with, and the one Harvard names for mineral deposits. Not apple cider. Not cleaning vinegar at 6% unless you say so.

**Citric acid** is a white powder you mix yourself. FDA (21 CFR 184.1033) lists it as a GRAS food ingredient. The agency’s Substances Added to Food inventory also names it as a **sequestrant** — it binds metal ions. That is the chelation. Fellow’s kettle care: citric is a bit stronger than vinegar and does not leave the lingering scent. Drench (UK retailer): strongest of their three household options on heavy buildup, odourless, faster than vinegar. Maker/retailer statements, not a lab score from this site.

Buy food-grade powder: a 1–2 lb bag of [food-grade citric acid](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00EYFKKZC?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). Not Dezcal packets, not a Lemi Shine booster, not bath-bomb mix.

Both are acids. Same stone and finish risk. Acid plus bleach makes chlorine gas (Washington State DOH; vinegar is on their acid list). Baking soda in the soak just kills the acid.

## Electric kettle: citric is the default

Full steps: [how to descale an electric kettle](/articles/how-to-descale-an-electric-kettle). Unplug, cool, **never put the base in the sink**.

Good Housekeeping: fill the kettle **halfway**, add **2 tablespoons** of citric acid, boil, sit **about 20 minutes**, dump, rinse, wipe remaining flakes with a soft sponge. Fellow: **500 mL water + 2 spoonfuls** of citric, boil, sit **at least 30 minutes**, rinse thoroughly.

Vinegar still works. Fellow: **half vinegar, half water**, 30 minutes. GH goes weaker: **1 part distilled white vinegar to 3 parts water**, about a third full, 20 minutes, then extra rinses. Citric is why you stop tasting salad in the next two pots of tea.

Never CLR, Bar Keepers Friend, Lime-A-Way, or toilet descaler in a kettle.

## Shower head: vinegar bag first, citric when it stalls

The pantry method is a 50/50 distilled-white-vinegar bag. Delta: that mix helps lift deposits “while not harming the finish” on *their* heads; check periodically. Full bag method: [how to clean a shower head with vinegar](/articles/how-to-clean-a-shower-head-with-vinegar).

Switch to citric when vinegar is slow or too smelly. Drench mix: **2 tablespoons of powder per litre of hot water**, **15–20 minutes on chrome**. Same bag. Same finish logic — citric is still an acid:

| Finish | First clock | Stop if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chrome or plastic | 15–20 min citric (Drench) or timed 50/50 vinegar, then check | Finish dulls, plastic clouds, gaskets swell |
| Brushed / polished nickel | 15–30 min, rinse immediately (Moen: 50/50 vinegar wipe, rinse off at once) | Color shift, dark streaks |
| Matte black, oil-rubbed bronze, other powder coats | 10–15 min only if the brand allows acid at all. Moen: avoid lime-scale removers on these lines. | Fade, bronze showing through |
| Brass, gold, unknown | 10–15 min, look, stop | You cannot name the finish |

Overnight only for a **removed** chrome or plastic head in a bowl. Repeat a timed pass. Do not extend the clock. Keep acid off marble, limestone, travertine, and unsealed grout. This page does not sell a replacement shower head.

## Dishes: film only, etching is done

White spots you can scratch are usually mineral film. A smooth frost that survives acid is etching — permanent. Whirlpool and Maytag both start with a vinegar wipe: dip a cloth, rub a patch. If it clears, soak. If nothing moves, stop buying removers.

Sink soak for **film only**: **20–30 minutes** in distilled white vinegar (Whirlpool’s own soak is 15 minutes; we give crusty loads the extra time), or a couple of tablespoons of citric in hot water for the same window. Hand dry. Skip silver, aluminum, painted mugs, and unsealed stone serving pieces.

The jar of citric is a sink soak, not rinse aid, and not a Lemi Shine dishwasher booster. Those products live on [how to remove hard water spots from dishes](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-spots-from-dishes). Do not put CLR, Bar Keepers Friend, or Lime-A-Way on plates.

## Finish and stone (same as vinegar)

Acid dissolves calcium. Marble, limestone, and travertine *are* calcium carbonate. Unsealed grout takes the same hit. Some nickel and powder-coat faucet finishes dull if you leave the bag on. Aluminum: do not soak overnight. Gaskets: timed contact, not an overnight acid bath. “Food-grade” does not mean “cannot etch stone.” Repeat a timed pass. Do not extend the clock.

## What not to do

## FAQ

**Is citric acid stronger than vinegar?**
On heavy scale, yes — stronger contact than a 5% vinegar soak, with almost no smell (Fellow; Drench). We did not measure “% of scale removed.” Light film still yields to pantry vinegar.

**Will citric ruin my shower head?**
Same risk as vinegar. Chrome and plastic take a timed soak. Matte black, oil-rubbed bronze, and some nickel get the short clock. Color change means stop and rinse.

**Can I descale a Nespresso with bulk citric?**
Not from this page. Super-autos that ban generic acids are not a bulk-citric job. Check the coffee-maker comparison.

**How much powder do I keep on the shelf?**
A 1–2 lb bag of [food-grade citric acid](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00EYFKKZC?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) lasts. Kettle: 1–2 tablespoons a pass. Shower: 2 tablespoons per litre. Dish soak: a couple of tablespoons in a sink of hot water.
