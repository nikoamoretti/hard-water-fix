## Siphon jet vs rim jets vs bowl ring vs tank

The siphon jet is one large hole at the bottom rear of the bowl, visible only after you drop the standing water. When you flush, that hole shoots a rush of water that starts the siphon and pulls the bowl contents down the drain. Calcium in that hole steals the pull even when the rim still swirls.

The rim jets are the tiny under-rim holes that make the swirl. Those are a different path and a different how-to: [how to clean toilet rim jets hard water](/articles/how-to-clean-toilet-rim-jets-hard-water). Overflow tube, then a zip-tie. Do not run that method here and call it a siphon-jet clean.

The white band at the waterline is the bowl ring. That page is [how to remove hard water stains from a toilet bowl](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-toilet-bowl). Gel and pumice stay there. They are not a jet tool.

A tank descale is article #36 territory. This how-to does not dump vinegar or CLR into the tank water. Shutoff and flush only to drop the bowl so you can see the hole.

Orange in the hole is iron, not this mineral. That job is [how to remove rust stains from a toilet](/articles/how-to-remove-rust-stains-from-toilet-hard-water). Do not merge the two.

White or crusty buildup inside the jet throat is calcium. A flashlight aimed into the dropped-bowl hole shows it. Some washdown bowls have no separate siphon jet — if you cannot find a bottom-rear hole after dropping the water, stop; this page is not that bowl. If the rim wash looks fine and the standing water level looks normal but the flush still feels soft, start here before you replace a flapper.

## Drop the bowl. Vinegar into the exposed hole.

The siphon jet does not drink from a full puddle you pour acid into blindly. You need the hole empty and visible so vinegar actually enters it.

Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Pantry. No Amazon vinegar URL. Distilled water is NONE_QUALIFY on this page — pantry name only if you rinse, no Amazon gallon link.

1. Find the shutoff, usually on the wall or floor behind the toilet. Close it (turn clockwise). Do not crank past the stop.
2. Flush once. Hold the handle down if needed so the tank empties and the bowl drops as far as it will. The tank should not refill.
3. If standing water still covers the bottom rear, bail or sponge the rest into a bucket until the large hole at the bottom rear is exposed. That hole is the siphon jet.
4. Pour pantry distilled white vinegar slowly into that hole — enough to fill the jet throat and wet the scale you can see. Do not dump it into the tank. Do not pour it around the rim and call it done; that is the rim-jets page.
5. Timed sit. At least **2 to 3 hours**. Heavier crust: overnight vinegar is fine in the jet and trapway throat. CLR is not. That clock is the next section.
6. Open the shutoff. Let the tank refill. Flush several times — 3 to 4 in a row — so loosened scale leaves the jet and the drain.

Gloves. Open a window or run the fan. Vinegar is an acid; the acidity helps dissolve mineral so water can carry it away. Wash it off your hands when you are done pouring.

If someone already used bleach in this bowl or tank, flush and rinse with plain water before any vinegar. Never mix acid and bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

Do not skip the drop. Vinegar sitting on top of a full bowl never concentrates in the siphon-jet throat the way a pour into the exposed hole does. Bail first. Then pour.

## Paper clip after the soak

After the vinegar sit, look into the hole with a flashlight. White or crusty rings inside the throat are scale.

Gently work a straightened paper clip into the opening. Slow in-and-out. Loosen crust; do not ream the hole larger. Do not force past a hard stop. A clip that snaps off inside the jet is a new problem — go slow, and pull it out the same way it went in.

Nylon zip-tie or wooden toothpick works the same job if that is what you have. This page allows the paper clip for the siphon jet after the soak. The rim-jets page stays on zip-tie or toothpick for the tiny under-rim holes; different holes, different tool call.

Not pumice in the jet. Pumice is the bowl-ring path on [how to remove hard water stains from a toilet bowl](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-toilet-bowl). A stick does not belong jammed deep into the trapway.

Flush again. Watch the pull. A clear siphon jet often restores the strong drop even when the rim swirl already looked fine. The standing water level in the bowl usually does not change; the change is the siphon kick.

## FAQ

**Is a weak flush clogged rim jets or a clogged siphon jet?**

Drop the bowl and look. Rim still swirls but the pull is weak → siphon jet (this page). Uneven or missing rim wash → [rim jets](/articles/how-to-clean-toilet-rim-jets-hard-water). Both can be calcium. They are not the same hole.

**Is the bowl ring the same as a clogged siphon jet?**

No. The ring sits at the waterline. The siphon jet is the large hole at the bottom rear after the water is dropped. Bowl ring: [how to remove hard water stains from a toilet bowl](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-toilet-bowl). This page does not recopy it.
