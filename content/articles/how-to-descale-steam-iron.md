White crust in the steam holes is calcium carbonate from hard tap water heated in the tank. USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heat them and they drop as scale that clogs vents and spatters chalk on the next shirt. Fill with distilled if *your* booklet allows it. Descale the way that sheet prints — often a water-only self-clean, sometimes diluted vinegar, sometimes an explicit vinegar ban. Read the manual before you pour. If you ran acid, flush with plain water or the next shirt smells like a pickle.

## The manuals contradict. Do not average them.

There is no universal vinegar recipe for steam irons. US sheets we fetched disagree on acid and on 100% distilled. If a UK household article and the US booklet disagree, the US booklet wins for that brand.

## How to descale — match the box

Work over a sink. Unplug to fill unless the sheet says otherwise. No invented clocks.

### 1. Rowenta Calc Clean (water only)

1. Unplug. Fill the tank with **water** (not vinegar).
2. Plug in. Hold over a sink.
3. Press **MODE** and the **steam trigger together for 3 seconds**.
4. Let it run **4–6 minutes**. It turns off when done.

If Calc Clean flashes mid-session, that FAQ says you can finish the shirt. Anti-calc **valve** models (Everlast / Expertis): cool **30–45 minutes**, pull the valve by the top, soak the **valve only** 4 hours in lemon juice or citric acid, rinse, replace. Never touch the valve tip. That soak is not a tank fill. Some Rowenta models use a replaceable anti-calc cartridge as a labeled part; we are not linking a US replacement cartridge here. Collector models: rinse the collector under tap once the iron is cold. Rowenta US blog: vinegar on a **cool soleplate** is exterior residue, not a reservoir fill.

### 2. BLACK+DECKER Auto Clean (water, ~monthly)

1. Fill well below MAX with tap water. Plug in. Fabric setting **6**. Heel rest. Heat **2 minutes**. Unplug.
2. Soleplate down over a sink. Hold **Auto Clean** until empty. Rock the iron if needed. Hot water and steam come out after several seconds.
3. Heel rest, plug in, heat **about 2 minutes** to dry.

Steam an old cloth afterward. Vinegar on that sheet is what **not** to wipe the soleplate with.

### 3. Conair self-clean (water, every 10–15 days)

1. Fill to MAX. Steam **OFF**. Thermostat **max**. Wait for the ready light to go off.
2. Unplug. Hold horizontally over the sink. Hold self-clean until boiling water, steam, and impurities come out.
3. Cool. Damp cloth on the soleplate. Empty. Store upright.

If that booklet prefers distilled, fill the tank with [distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) for ordinary ironing and for this flush. Do not add a store descaler on a sheet that voids the warranty for decalcifiers.

### 4. Oliso steam-burst flush (water)

Heat to **linen**, unplug, hold over a sink, press **steam burst** until water drips heavily from the soleplate. Keep it hot so leftover water evaporates. Do not submerge. Slide the steam selector off ↔ max to clear the valve. Tap, or 50/50 if very hard — not 100% distilled on this brand.

### 5. Vinegar only when *your* U&C prints it (Hamilton Beach 11559)

Brand FAQ: no vinegar unless the Use & Care directs otherwise. Model **11559** does:

1. **1/4 cup white vinegar + 1/4 cup cold water**.
2. Steam-iron steps, **HIGH**, steam button about **4 minutes**. **Do not steam clothes** with vinegar in the tank.
3. Cool. Empty. Rinse. **1/2 cup water**. Steam again.

If your Hamilton Beach sheet does not print that block, do not borrow it. After any vinegar cycle, flush with [distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) if the booklet allows distilled — then steam an old cloth until it does not smell like pickle.

## What not to do

- Do not pour vinegar into a Rowenta Calc Clean, a Conair that bans decalcifiers, or a Hamilton Beach whose FAQ says no unless the U&C prints it.
- Do not pour CLR, bleach, or any bottle the iron booklet never names into the tank.
- Do not run a kettle citric mix through the iron. Rowenta’s lemon/citric line is a **removed valve soak**, not a tank fill.
- Do not skip the plain-water flush after vinegar. 11559 prints the rinse on purpose.
- Do not scrape vents with metal (Rowenta / Conair: no steel wool or metallic pads).
- Do not leave water in the tank. Hamilton Beach: leftover water can make rusty-looking steam.

## If the vents clog again

Hard water keeps depositing calcium carbonate. [Test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home). Empty after each session. Where the booklet allows it, daily fill is [distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) or that sheet’s 50/50 mix. Do not copy Breville espresso’s distilled ban onto Conair, or Conair’s “preferred” onto Oliso.

## FAQ

**Can I just use vinegar?**
Only if *your* US Use & Care prints it. Rowenta DW9510D1 bans it on Calc Clean. Conair GI100 bans decalcifiers. Hamilton Beach’s FAQ says no unless the booklet directs otherwise — 11559 does, at 1/4 + 1/4, then a water rinse. Do not average those into one cup.

**Should I fill with distilled only?**
Conair prefers it. Rowenta handhelds want untreated tap (50/50 if very hard); stations ban pure distilled. Oliso: never 100% distilled. Hamilton Beach FAQ lists distilled as a leak risk. Open the booklet.

**Is self-clean the same as descale?**
On these sheets, yes: a hot-water flush (Rowenta 4–6 min; BLACK+DECKER until empty; Conair until impurities exit; Oliso until water drips). Not a vinegar soak unless that U&C adds one. Not a coffee CLEAN cycle.

**What about the anti-calc cartridge?**
Some Rowenta models use a replaceable anti-calc cartridge as a labeled part. Others use a collector you rinse or a valve you soak off the iron. We are not linking a US replacement cartridge. Do not pour vinegar in the tank to stand in for a part the sheet never names.
