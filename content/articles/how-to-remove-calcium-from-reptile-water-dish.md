## Hard-water signs on a reptile water dish

Know the film before you open the vinegar.

- Slick, sour, or gray-brown film that soap lifts: biofilm. Mild unscented soap first. Then judge the chalk.
- Faint white haze at the waterline after hard tap sits and evaporates: early mineral film. Soap first. Vinegar **~1:9**. Soft brush. Rinse until **no vinegar smell**. Pantry distilled refill going forward.
- Raised white chalk a fingernail catches on the rim, floor, or grooves: carbonate scale. Same soap pass. Then a **stronger short soak** (up to **1:1**, **10–15 minutes**). Soft brush. Full rinse. Distilled refill.
- Cloudy “dirty dish” look that soap alone does not clear: often mineral on leftover slime. Honesty: chalk reads as dirty dish until vinegar softens it.
- White dust from calcium supplement powder on the rim with little waterline ring: often **supplement dust**, not tap scale. Wipe and soap. Do not treat powder dust as a reason for bathroom lime remover.
