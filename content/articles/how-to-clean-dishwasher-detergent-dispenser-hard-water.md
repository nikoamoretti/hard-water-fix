## Unplug. Then toothbrush the latch.

1. Unplug the dishwasher, or kill the breaker that feeds it. Do not fish in a live door. Water, vinegar, and door wiring do not mix.
2. Open the door. Empty the tub so you can see the dispenser face.
3. Press the latch release the booklet shows, or gently hold the cup door open if it will move at all. Do not pry with a knife. Plastic latch. Knife cuts plastic.
4. Soft toothbrush or cotton swab. Distilled white vinegar from the pantry. Grocery bottle. No Amazon vinegar URL on this page.
5. Work the latch face, the hinge pins, and the gasket groove around the cup. Circular, light. Keep liquid on the plastic and rubber you can wipe — not sprayed into the door cavity or the wax-motor wiring.
6. Let the vinegar sit on the crust a few minutes. Brush again. Wipe dry with a clean cloth.
7. Flick the latch. It should spring. If it still binds, repeat the brush pass. Do not force the door open with a screwdriver against the plastic catch.

**Wipe the cup dry before the next pod.** Hard water plus a wet cup re-cements the latch in one cycle. Dry cup. Dry latch face. Then load.

## Keep the latch from packing again

Wipe the cup and latch face dry after every load that leaves wet powder or gel in the cup. Hard water plus a damp cup re-cements the latch. Prefer a dry pod seat. If powder cakes the corners, brush the dry cup with a dry toothbrush before the next fill — no liquid needed for loose dust.

After the latch is free, run the monthly empty [Affresh](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00SXC85IQ?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) cycle so mineral in the tub and lines does not walk back onto the dispenser face as fast. Dishwasher Affresh, not washer Affresh.
