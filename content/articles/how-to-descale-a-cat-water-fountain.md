## Soap for slime. Vinegar for scale.

Two jobs. Do not mash them.

Scale is white, chalky, fingernail-gritty. USGS: vinegar (an acid) helps dissolve mineral so water can carry it away. Aitakon: dish soap cannot dissolve crystallized calcium carbonate. White vinegar can. That is the 50/50 soak.

Slime is biofilm. Saliva, hair, a slick film on the waterline. PetSafe’s slimy-Platinum sheet: soak fountain and pump parts in equal parts vinegar and water for up to 20 minutes, then scrub with soap and warm water, including inside the pump where the impeller sits. Rinse until there is no soap or vinegar left. Catit: dishwashing soap and a soft sponge on the bowl. PETKIT: clean the water tank at least once a week and clean thoroughly once a month. Aitakon: mild unscented dish soap on the basin on a weekly-ish clock; plastic needs the short end of that window.

Distilled fill slows the mineral. It does not stop slime. Skip the weekly soap and the bowl still goes slick. Skip the vinegar soak and the impeller still grinds.

Do not bleach the bowl to “kill slime.” PetSafe: never use bleach or other harsh chemicals. Aitakon: bleach odor soaks into plastic; cats smell chlorine you do not. Never chase bleach with vinegar. Chlorine gas. Fresh air. **1-800-222-1222**.

Yes. Every maker sheet that names power says unplug or power off before you take the pump apart. PetSafe. Catit. PETKIT. Uahpet. TheTechInfluencer. Then soak. Never a live pump.

**Can I run vinegar through the assembled fountain?**

No. TheTechInfluencer: never run acids through an active pump. Unplug. Disassemble. Soak the wet parts. Wipe the electricals.

**How long does the impeller soak?**

15–30 minutes in 50/50 white vinegar and water. PetSafe: at least 15 minutes. Aitakon: 20–30, not past 30, or the plastic keeps the odor. Repeat a fresh bath if the crust is still raised. Not overnight.
