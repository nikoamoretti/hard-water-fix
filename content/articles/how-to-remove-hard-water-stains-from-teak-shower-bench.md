1. Lift the bench out of the shower if it is freestanding, or work in place if it is fixed. Shake off standing water. Wipe pooled drips from the seat and the floor under it.
2. Spot-check a small underside or end-grain area first. Teak is oily hardwood; short dilute vinegar is the mineral path. Do not soak the whole bench overnight.

## Hard-water signs on a teak shower bench

Know the stain before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze on the seat after showers dry: early mineral film. Short 1:1 wipe and a dry habit stretch the gap.
- Raised white chalk a fingernail catches on slats or edges: carbonate scale. Soften 1:1 short dwell. Soft brush with the grain. Rinse. Dry.
- Water still pooling in a low spot after every shower: the bench is holding drips. Dry after use or the chalk returns overnight.
- Gray UV fade or deep black in the grain: weathering or mildew, not pure calcium. Short vinegar can help light mildew; sanding is last resort, not the default mineral path.
- Orange on screws or metal feet: rust on metal, not this wood path. Do not soak the whole bench in Iron Out or CLR.
