## Name the stone first. Stop if it is marble.

**Granite** is igneous. Speckled. Quartz, feldspar, mica. Natural Stone Institute: siliceous stone is mostly silicates. It resists kitchen acids better than marble. Acids are still not recommended. Trace minerals and the sealer can still take damage.

**Marble** is calcareous. Veined calcium carbonate. NSI: lemon, vinegar, or other acids may dull or etch calcareous stone. Stop. There is no live marble how-to on this site. Do not invent a URL. Do not copy this granite ladder onto marble.

**Quartz** is engineered resin plus crushed stone. It is not granite. No live quartz how-to here. Do not invent a URL.

Water-drop check on a dry patch. If a tablespoon of water beads, the impregnating sealer is working. NSI: industry “sealers” are impregnators. They repel. They do not make the stone stain-proof. The white ring on a beading, sealed face is mineral on the polish. If the drop darkens the stone in a minute or two, the sealer is tired. Still no vinegar. Still no CLR. Clean the crust first. ## Baking-soda poultice on the ring

Raised white crust a fingernail catches. SHINENOS: baking-soda paste and water, about 30 minutes, for mineral deposits on granite. SlabWise: mix it to a paste, not dry grit. NSI: scouring powders may scratch certain stones.

1. Mix baking soda with a little water to a thick paste. Peanut-butter thick.
2. Spread it on the crust only.
3. Sit 5 to 10 minutes for a thin ring, up to 30 if it is still raised. Do not let it dry hard.
4. Wipe with a damp cloth. Do not scrub dry powder into the polish.
5. Rinse with [distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). Buff dry.

This poultice is mechanical. Do not mix baking soda with vinegar to “boost” it. Acid plus bicarbonate is salt, water, and CO2. The fizz is gas, not extra cleaning. That mix myth already lives on [baking soda vs vinegar](/articles/baking-soda-vs-vinegar-hard-water-stains).

## 0000 wool and water on leftover crust

Paste lifted most of it. A thin mineral skin is still there.

NSI: water spots and rings are a surface accumulation of hard water. Buff with dry 0000 steel wool. SlabWise: 0000 (four-aught) with a little water, gentle circles, on polished granite. That grade is the method. Not a scratch pad. Not an Amazon pack on this page.

1. Spot-test a hidden edge of the same polish.
2. Use 0000 only. Wet the pad so you lift mineral instead of grinding grit.
3. Light circles on the crust. Stop the second you see new haze.
4. Distilled rinse. Dry.

No coarse pads. No powdered cleanser.
