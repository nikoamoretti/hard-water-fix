## Power off. Self-clean. Then extend.

1. Self-clean. Extend. On an electric seat, unplug once the wand is out so it cannot retract while you wrap.
2. Soak a cloth or paper towel in distilled white vinegar. Wrap the spray holes. 10–30 minutes. Re-wet if it dries. Horow: apply only where scale is visible; do not soak wiring or sensors.
3. Unwrap. Soft toothbrush on the holes. Wooden toothpick for a stubborn hole. Bidet.blog: wooden toothpick; metal tools scratch and worsen clogs. TUSHY: no sharp object in the openings.
4. Rinse. ## Inlet strainer. Every few months on electric seats.

Weak spray is sometimes the mesh, not the holes. BidetScout: electric seats have a small mesh filter where the hose meets the seat; sediment hits that screen first. Bio Bidet: clean the strainer every few months — it is the piece that connects the hose to the shutoff. TOTO USA: water-filter drain valve about every six months, or if pressure has weakened. Kohler: clean the filter screen every 6 months or when maximum flow from the spray wand drops. Brondell Swash: check the mesh filter at least once a year; intervals follow use and water quality.

This is a rinse. It is not a pitcher filter. Do not invent a bidet-filter SKU.

1. Shut the supply. Flush to drop pressure. Unplug the electric seat. TOTO: do not pull the drain valve with the shutoff open — water will spurt.
2. Disconnect the hose at the inlet. Pull the cylindrical screen. Tweezers or needle-nose pliers on a Brondell. Long-nosed pliers on Kohler. Bio Bidet: unscrew the hose nut and brush the strainer.
3. Rinse under running water. Soft toothbrush. Reinsert. Reconnect. Water on. Check for leaks.

If the mesh is torn, replace the part your booklet names. BidetScout: 6 to 12 months is a common replace window on electric seats. That is still a screen, not a Brita.

## FAQ

**Is weak bidet spray a dead pump or calcium in the holes?**

Look at the tip. BidetLabs: white buildup, weaker or uneven spray, discoloration at the nozzle. BidetScout: clean the nozzle and the inlet filter first. Horow: sudden weak spray with no crust is often supply or the screen. Kohler: low or no flow — water off, clogged filter screen, or pressure set to the lowest setting.

**Can I just pour vinegar into the tank?**

No, unless *your* booklet writes that cycle. TOTO SW2033R / SW2014: fill the tank with water only. Brondell Swash: no acidic chemicals. This page’s descale is a wrap on the tip.

**Is this the same as cleaning rim jets or the bowl ring?**

No. Rim jets: [how to clean toilet rim jets](/articles/how-to-clean-toilet-rim-jets-hard-water). Bowl ring: [how to remove hard water stains from a toilet bowl](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-toilet-bowl). This page is the wand holes.

**Can I use a metal pin to open a clogged hole?**

No. Bidet.blog: metal scratches and deforms the holes. TUSHY: no sharp object. Wooden toothpick. Soft toothbrush.

**Do self-cleaning nozzles still need a wrap?**

Yes, if you have crust. BidetScout: the self-clean rinses with water; it does not remove mineral deposits. Horow: self-cleaning is helpful, not magic.

**How often is the inlet screen?**

Bio Bidet: every few months. TOTO USA and Kohler: about every six months, or when flow drops. Brondell Swash: at least yearly. Rinse. Do not invent a replacement pitcher-filter SKU.

**Can I mix the vinegar wrap with bleach to get scale and germs?**
