White flakes on a shirt are scale leaving the steamer nozzle, not detergent. USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Hard at 121 mg/L as CaCO3. Very hard above 180. Heat those minerals in a tank and they drop as limescale in the hose and the head. Philips US: if you skip descaling, the steamer can release impurities or stain the garment. Rowenta USA: chalky residue around the nozzle, weaker steam, sputtering droplets instead of mist.

This page is a hanging or handheld clothes steamer. Tank plus hose plus nozzle. Not a [steam iron](/articles/how-to-descale-steam-iron). Not a steam mop. Do not copy the iron vents, Calc Clean clocks, or Oliso distilled ban onto this tank.

## The booklets contradict. Do not average them.

There is no universal vinegar recipe for a garment steamer. Philips US and the Philips STH5030 booklet print a vinegar ban. Philips Australia’s De-Calc / Easy Rinse page says the same: do not put vinegar, descaling agents, or other chemicals into the steamer. Rowenta USA’s handheld guide wants a plain-water flush and the built-in self-clean if your model has one. Some older Rowenta stand sheets and Conair’s GS28 booklet *do* print a vinegar mix. If a lifestyle article and *your* US booklet disagree, the booklet wins.

Homes and Gardens tells people to sit one part distilled white vinegar and two parts water in the tank for 30–60 minutes, then steam and rinse — and, if that fails, a coffee-machine descaler. That is the mill recipe. It is not Philips. It is not Rowenta USA’s handheld path. Do not run it on a sheet that forbids acid.

## Water-only path (Philips De-Calc / Easy Rinse, Rowenta USA handheld)

Work over a sink. Unplug to fill unless that sheet says to heat first. No invented clocks.

### Philips stand steamer — de-calc knob (US)

Philips US, when scale stops steam: switch off, unplug, cool **one hour**. Bring it to a sink. The de-calc knob sits on the lower area of the water tank. Turn it anti-clockwise to unlock. Pull it out. Let the water and scale drain. Push the knob back in. Turn clockwise to lock.

Philips Australia’s Easy Rinse de-calc (EasyTouch GC48x / GC499 / GC50x, EasyTouch Plus, ComfortTouch, ComfortTouch Plus, ComfortTouch Advanced, 3000 Series STE31xx): same cool-down. Water above the **MIN** mark. Hold over a bucket. Knob anticlockwise. Drain. Knob back, clockwise. Monthly, or more if the water is very hard. **Do not** pour vinegar or a descaler into that opening.

### Philips 7000 series — rinse mode (label the region)

US stains page, 7000 series only: hold the steam-setting button and the steam trigger together until **two LED lights blink**. Steam and hot water run **1 minute**. Then the ECO light blinks and the unit heats again.

Australia Easy Rinse-Off Scale (STH7020, STH7030, STH7040, STH7050, STH7060): plug in, wait **30 seconds** to heat, same two-button hold until two LEDs blink, hold over the sink and shake to empty hot water and scale. After **30 seconds**, ECO blinks. Power off. Cool **60 minutes**. Wipe the soleplate with a soft cloth.

Do not average 1 minute with 30 seconds. Use the page that matches the unit.

### Philips Quick Calc Release (AU — StyleTouch Pure GC440/GC442, 8000 Series GC800/GC801/GC810)

Unplug. Cool at least **1 hour**. Remove the cover marked **De-Calc**. Over the sink, pull the rubber stopper. Shake gently to empty water and scale. Stopper and cover back. Monthly. Same vinegar ban.

### Philips engines that say no descale

AU: All-in-One 8600 (AIS8540), All-in-One 8000 (GC628, GC629), ProTouch (GC610, GC612, GC625, GC626, GC627), ProTouch 2-in-1 (GC617, GC618). Scale flakes into a space in the base. No user dump. Ordinary tap, or distilled/demineralized if hard — AU also lists **50% demineralized + tap**. Still no vinegar. US stains page: that stain-fix block does **not** apply to GC625, GC626, GC627, GC610, GC612, GC617, GC618.

### Philips STH5030 (and STH5010 / STH5020) booklet

No De-Calc knob on that sheet. Unplug. Empty residual water. Cool about **60 minutes**. Wipe flakes off the steam plate with a damp cloth. Never a scouring pad, vinegar, or other chemicals on the plate. Hard-water fill is **50/50** tap + distilled or demineralized — not a vinegar soak.

### Rowenta USA handheld — distilled flush, then self-clean if you have it

Rowenta USA: empty the last session. Fill with fresh distilled or demineralized water to the max the tank allows. Heat fully. Run continuous steam over an old towel or into a sink until nearly empty. A second tank if the crust is heavy. Soft damp cloth on the nozzle. Do not scrape or poke the steam holes. Leave the tank open and empty to dry.

If that model has a self-cleaning or anti-calc function, the same blog says use it **monthly**. Follow *that* booklet’s button. It is not a store descaler. It is not a replaceable cartridge SKU — we are not linking one.

Rowenta IS8470D1 and DR8051U1 FAQs: never add descaling chemicals or additives. If steam drops, open **that** unit’s Descaling section. Do not borrow GS4010’s vinegar cup.

## Vinegar path — only if *your* sheet prints it

Label the model. Do not pour this into a Philips tank or a Rowenta whose FAQ bans chemicals. After any vinegar cycle, steam an old cloth until it does not smell like pickle. Never mix vinegar and bleach. Poison Control: bleach plus an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar. Leave the area. Fresh air. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

### Conair GS28 / GS28L (1/3 vinegar + 2/3 water)

Conair Ultimate Fabric Steamer booklet, quoted path:

1. **1/3 white vinegar + 2/3 water** in the tank. Tank back on the base.
2. Plug in. ON. Run until **half** has steamed (**1 liter**).
3. OFF. Unplug. Sit **30 minutes**.
4. Pull the tank. Unscrew the plug at the bottom of the steamer. Drain the internal reservoir over a sink.
5. Repeat with a **fresh** mix until a normal steam rate returns, usually **2–3** times.
6. Run **one cycle of fresh, cold water** before you steam a shirt.

Do not steam clothes with vinegar in the tank. Poor steam on that sheet is sediment — decalcify the reservoir and the tank. Empty leftover water before storage.

### Rowenta GS4010U1 US booklet (1/3 vinegar + 2/3 water, 3 minutes)

Official GS4010 PDF:

1. Unplug. Fill with **1/3 white vinegar and 2/3 water**.
2. Run **3 minutes**. **Do not steam any clothing** with this mixture.
3. Off. Unplug. Cool at least **30 minutes**.
4. Empty over a sink. Rinse with fresh water.
5. Fill with fresh water. Repeat steps 2–4.

At least once a year or every **100 hours**. Sooner if the water is hard. If scale remains, repeat with a new vinegar mix. Pull the hose before you empty — leftover water in the hose can be hot. This is **not** Philips. This is **not** the Rowenta USA handheld blog.

### Rowenta IS9500D1 FAQ (50/50 vinegar, 12-hour sit)

Only this FAQ’s descale block. Cool **one hour**. Pull the steam head and the pole. Remove the tank. **Never fill inside the pole connection.** 0.5 L water in the tank inlet, shake, dump. Then **1 L mix: 0.5 L water + 0.5 L white vinegar only**. Wait **12 hours minimum**. Over a sink, unscrew the **Anti-calc** cap anti-clockwise. Dump. 0.5 L water in, dump. Cap back. Pole and head back. Fill the tank. Run until empty.

That 12-hour sit is printed for IS9500. It is not permission to leave vinegar overnight in a Philips boiler or a Rowenta that bans additives.

## FAQ

**White flakes on the shirt — is that detergent?**

No. That is scale leaving the nozzle. Philips US: skipped descaling releases impurities or stains. First few uses on a new Philips can also spit test particles — brush those off dry.

**Can I just use vinegar?**

Only if *your* booklet prints it. Philips US, Philips AU De-Calc / Easy Rinse, and the STH5030 booklet forbid vinegar. Rowenta USA’s handheld guide wants water. Conair GS28 prints 1/3 + 2/3. Rowenta GS4010 prints 1/3 + 2/3 for 3 minutes. Rowenta IS9500 prints 0.5 L + 0.5 L and a 12-hour sit. Do not average those into Homes and Gardens’ 1:2, 30–60 minute sit.

**Should I fill with distilled only?**

Where the sheet allows it, yes — that is the prevention. Philips often wants tap, then 50/50 if the water is hard. Rowenta handheld FAQs want untreated tap, 50/50 if very hard, and they list bottled/filtered water among the don’ts. Open the booklet.

**Is De-Calc the same as a descaling solution?**

No. On Philips, De-Calc / Easy Rinse is a water-and-scale dump or a rinse mode. The AU line is explicit: do not put vinegar, descaling agents, or other chemicals into the steamer. It is not a Keurig bottle.

**What about the anti-calc cartridge?**

Some steamers have a De-Calc knob, a rubber stopper, or an Anti-calc drain cap. Those are dumps, not a store cartridge we are selling. We are not linking a replacement SKU. Do not pour vinegar in the tank to stand in for a part the sheet never names.

**Can I mix the vinegar descale with bleach to get scale and germs?**

No. Poison Control: bleach plus an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar. Leave the area. Fresh air. **1-800-222-1222**.
