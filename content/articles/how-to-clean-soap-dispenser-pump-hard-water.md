## Clear dried soap first. Then vinegar 50/50 on the removed head.

Pantry distilled white vinegar. Equal parts water — **50/50**. Soft cloth or soft toothbrush on the tip. No Amazon vinegar URL. No CLR. No Lime-A-Way. No citric money SKU as the default.

1. Empty or set aside leftover soap if you can pour it cleanly. Wipe the bottle neck so crust does not fall into the dip tube when you reinstall.
2. Unscrew and **remove** the pump head. Freestanding bottle: lift the whole pump and dip tube out. Built-in deck pump: shut off the under-sink soap supply (or close the bottle valve the booklet names) before you loosen the collar — a plumbed line will keep feeding if you leave it open.
3. Clear dried soap first. Warm water over the tip and dip tube. Soft cloth. Soft toothbrush on the orifice. A wooden toothpick only if the tip is wide enough — do not ream a foam orifice larger. Do **not** force the pump body apart with pliers or a knife if the spring and ball are sealed. Broken plastic is a replacement, not a deeper clean.
4. Mix **50/50** distilled white vinegar (5% pantry bottle) and water in a bowl or jar deep enough to cover the **removed** pump head and nozzle tip (dip tube submerged if chalky).
5. Soak **15–30 minutes**. Stay in the room. Do not overnight-soak painted or specialty finishes as a habit. Soft toothbrush the tip and the collar threads after the soak.
6. Rinse thoroughly with clean water. Then **pump warm water through** the head until the stream runs clear and smells like water, not pickle. Discard that rinse water.
7. If you diluted refill soap with hard tap and the bottle shows white film inside, empty it. Wipe. Optional pantry **distilled water** rinse of the bottle only — grocery jug, pantry text. No Amazon distilled URL (NONE_QUALIFY). Dry the bottle before you refill.
8. Reinstall the pump. Hand-tight. Refill with soap. Prime a few pumps into the sink until product returns.

One soften-and-pump clears fresh chalk plus a light soap skin. A head that still will not draw after two clean passes, a cracked collar, or a dead spring is a **part** — see the replacement CTA below. Vinegar will not resurrect a broken plunger.

## Hard-water signs in a soap dispenser pump

Know the clog before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze on the tip after the last pump dries: early mineral film. Short 50/50 tip wipe and a dry habit stretch the gap.
- Tip that sticks shut with white chalk a fingernail catches: carbonate scale on the orifice. Remove the head. Soften 50/50. Soft brush. Pump warm water through.
- Slow, foamy dribble after you diluted thick soap with hard tap: soap cake plus mineral. Clear dried soap first, then the vinegar soak — do not skip the soap clear.
- Pump that clicks but draws nothing after a long sit: dried product in the valve seats. Warm-water clear first. Then 50/50 if chalk remains.
- Built-in deck dispenser that leaks under the sink after you fought the collar: supply still open or a cracked nut — shut off, stop forcing, check the line. Not more acid.
