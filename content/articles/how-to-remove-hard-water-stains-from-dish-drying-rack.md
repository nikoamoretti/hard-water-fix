## What that film is

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heat those minerals and they drop out as calcium carbonate. Soap plus calcium is scum. Leave wet dishes on the rack and leftover water evaporates. The Ca and Mg stay as white crust on the tines, joints, and drip tray. That is the stain this page is for.

If you want the hardness number, [test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home).

## Name the rack. Then name the stain.

Plastic: vinegar soak. No CLR. Coated or vinyl wire with the coating intact: vinegar soak. Chipped vinyl over bare steel is rust next week. Do not acid that coating. Stainless or chrome: vinegar first. CLR only if crust remains.

White or tan crust is scale. Orange or brown on **bare steel** is rust. Those are different jobs. Black slime is mold. Bleach can be a mold path. Bleach does nothing to calcium. Do not bleach a mineral job.

## Pull the rack. Soap first.

Do not acid-soak the sink to clean the rack. Lift it off the counter or the over-sink arms.

1. Clear dishes. Lift the rack. Empty the drip tray.
2. Pull **rubber feet** and plastic caps if they come off. They do not soak in acid. CLR: always avoid surfaces that cannot be thoroughly rinsed after 2 minutes. Rubber feet get dish soap and water. Set them aside.
3. Wash food and scum off with mild dish soap first. Scum shields mineral.
4. If the sink still smells like bleach, rinse it until it doesn't. Then start. Never mix leftover bleach with vinegar, CLR, or Iron Out.

Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids, and rust-removal products too. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

## Vinegar 1:1 soak. 20–30 minutes.

**Pulled rack only.** Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle. Pantry. No Amazon vinegar.

Pantry acid vs the bottle is [vinegar vs CLR](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains). Light film: vinegar. Raised crust that a fingernail catches on stainless or chrome: CLR after this soak. Do not retread that comparison here.

1. Basin, tub, or a pan long enough to cover the tines.
2. Equal parts distilled white vinegar and water. **1:1.** Rack fully under. Rubber feet stay out.
3. Soak **20–30 minutes**. Not overnight.
4. Soft brush or old toothbrush on the tines, joints, and drip tray. No steel wool. No scouring powder.
5. Dump. Do not fizz this soak with baking soda. That bubble is CO2 leaving, not extra scale power.

Plastic racks stay on this soak. Coated wire with intact vinyl stays on this soak. No citric on this page. No Granite Gold. No Lime-A-Way.
