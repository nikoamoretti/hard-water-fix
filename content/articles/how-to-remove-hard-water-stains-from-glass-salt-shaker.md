## Dry-before-refill honesty: wet salt cakes

Do the dry check before you pour salt back, in daylight. Feel the inner glass, the threads, and the spout holes.

- White crust that felt chalky and faded in the vinegar soak was **mineral film**. Distilled white vinegar **1:1** is still the job. Soft detail brush for the spout, threads, and mesh. Rinse until no vinegar smell.

- Both can live in the same shaker. Vinegar will clear the chalk and leave a damp film that cakes the next fill. Believe the damp. Do not reach for CLR “to get the cake out.”

## Hard-water signs in a glass salt shaker

Know the stain before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze on the inner glass after a hard-tap rinse dries: early mineral film. Vinegar **1:1** soak, soft detail brush for the spout, threads, and mesh, rinse until no smell, **dry fully before refill**.
- Raised white chalk a fingernail catches on the floor, the waterline, the threads, or the spout holes: carbonate scale. Same **1:1** soak. Soft detail brush. Full smell rinse. Dry fully before refill.
- Salt that will not shake, or a hard plug in the holes after a wash: **caked salt**. Honesty: **wet salt cakes.** Empty, rinse, dry fully, then refill. Vinegar is for mineral, not a license to refill damp.
- Cloudy glass that stays cloudy after chalk is gone: etched or scratched surface. Vinegar clears mineral; it does not polish glass clear again.
- Sour pickle smell in the next shake: leftover vinegar film in the threads or spout. Rinse again until there is **no vinegar smell**. Dry fully before refill.
