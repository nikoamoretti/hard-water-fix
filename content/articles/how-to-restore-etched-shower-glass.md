## Confirm it is etch. Then stop the acid.

Finger test on a dry, uncleaned patch, in daylight. Then wet the same patch.

Field test (Koh shower-glass FAQ, not Kohler): slick is soap scum, gritty is mineral, smooth-but-hazy is etching. No cleaner will fix etch. Film usually vanishes while wet. Etch stays cloudy wet or dry — the surface is pitted. If it is still fogged **when wet**, stay here. Do not rerun the vinegar-to-CLR ladder.

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium (hard at 121 mg/L as CaCO3; very hard above 180). Dried droplets leave that film. Acid dissolves the film. It does not patch glass. CLR’s FAQ: if the remover did not help at all, the glass may be etched — “tiny cuts made in the glass by deposits in the water.” The only thing they name that *may* help is a jeweler’s polish. [Vinegar vs CLR](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains) is film, not an un-etch recipe. Stemware: [cloudy dishwasher glasses](/articles/cloudy-dishwasher-glasses-etching-or-hard-water). [Test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home) if you want the number.

Corning Museum of Glass (Astrid van Giffen): water leaches alkali from glass and causes microscopic damage. Glass is fairly resistant to acids. Corrosion runs outside-in and can leave a pitted look. Acids do not refill those pits.

## How-to: one wet test patch, then decide

Small corner first. If it does not clear, stop. Most shower doors are tempered safety glass.

1. Confirm the wet-haze test still reads etch. If the ghost vanished while wet, you still have [film](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-glass-shower-doors).
2. Clean loose dirt. Mask metal, stone, and any factory coating. J. Racenstein: polish removes a micro-thin layer of glass; a coating goes with it.
3. Fit a damp felt or wool pad. 3M names a dual-action or rotary polisher, not a pad in the carton.
4. Shake the [3M Glass Polishing Compound](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B007460F7Q?tag=hardwaterfi04-20). 3M: 2–4 drops per square foot.
5. Keep the slurry **wet**. 60150 is already water-based; 3M does not print a dry-buff recipe. J. Racenstein trade method: about **1,000–1,500 RPM**, pad flat, mist so it never dries. Dry pad is the number-one mistake. Drag or squeak: stop and reload.
6. Stop if the glass gets hot, nothing changes, or the frost still catches a fingernail. J. Racenstein: fingernail-catch is too deep for cerium.
7. Rinse, wipe dry, inspect in daylight. Judge the **dry** patch. Wet slurry hides haze.

3M SDS: avoid breathing dust from sanding or machining; safety glasses. Ventilate. Do not camp the pad.

## When to stop and replace the panel

Replace the pane if the dry test patch is still hazy, the frost is deep or pitted, the door is coated, tinted, or low-E, or the job is a whole tempered stall for a maybe.

Shower doors are safety glazing. Construction Specifier (Michael L. Rupert, PPG): tempered glass has a compression skin and a tension core. Thermal stress is a hotter center versus a cooler edge; that puts the edge in tension. Past edge strength, the pane breaks. J. Racenstein: excessive heat from a dry, aggressive pad on tempered glass can cause spontaneous breakage. Keep it wet. Do not dry-buff a stall door with a high-speed pad. Failed patch: a panel, not more compound.

Same Koh FAQ: no cleaner fixes etch — professional polish on *light* etching, or replace the panel. CLR: jeweler’s polish might help; no chemical remedy. Neither promises factory-new. Neither do we. Call a glazier — you cannot recut tempered glass.

## What not to do

- Do not use vinegar, CLR, or citric acid as an un-etch. Film chemistry. CLR already said so.
- Do not use an aluminum-oxide car polish (including Nu Finish) on shower glass. Wrong abrasive, wrong job.
- Do not treat 3M 60150 as a kit. Liquid only. Pad is a separate hardware-store felt or wool pad.
- Do not dry-buff tempered shower glass with a high-speed pad. Thermal stress is a documented breakage path.
- Do not coat a door that is still frosted, or grab automotive Rain-X for a stall.
- Do not polish factory-coated, tinted, or low-E glass.
- Do not expect factory-new. Light haze *might* improve. Deep frost is a panel.

## FAQ

**Will vinegar or CLR restore etched shower glass?**
No. They remove calcium/magnesium film. USGS: acid dissolves those mineral particles. Corning Museum of Glass: ordinary acids do not attack the silica network. Haze that remains when wet is CLR’s tiny cuts.

**Does cerium oxide refill the pits?**
No. It wears a thin layer of glass. Light haze might look clearer. Deep frost will not.

**Does 3M 60150 come with a pad?**
No. Liquid only. Felt or wool pad from a hardware store. 3M names a dual-action or rotary polisher, not a pad in the carton.

**Can I polish the whole tempered door?**
Only after a test patch actually clears, and only wet, moving constantly. Most heavily frosted tempered stall doors are not worth it. Heat is a thermal-stress risk (Construction Specifier; J. Racenstein). Deep frost: replace the panel.

**When do I use Rain-X Shower Door?**
After the glass is actually clear. Weeks, not years. Squeegee anyway. If the door is still frosted, do not coat it.

**Should I replace instead?**
Yes, if the wet test patch failed, the frost is heavy, or you will not wet-polish tempered glass.
