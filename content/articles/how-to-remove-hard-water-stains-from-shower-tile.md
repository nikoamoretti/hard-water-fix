## Name the tile before you spray

Leftover box or a spare tile is the honest ID. **Glazed ceramic** has a glass-like coating over a clay body. **Porcelain** is denser; many shower tiles are glazed porcelain. Both look glassy. Water beads. A fingernail does not scratch the glaze.

If the face is veined like a countertop, pitted like travertine, dull and absorbent, or sold as marble, limestone, or “natural stone,” stop. That is not this ladder. Natural Stone Institute: products containing lemon, vinegar, or other acids may dull or etch calcareous stone. Calcareous means marble, travertine, limestone, onyx — calcium carbonate. There is no live marble or granite how-to on this site. Do not invent a URL. Neutral stone soap only. Call a stone pro if it is already etched.

CLR stay-off: any natural stone, especially marble (even synthetic), and granite. Porcelain is on the use list. Ceramic is named on CLR’s pet-bowl how-to. This page is the glazed tile face.

Unglazed quarry, saltillo, or anything that soaks a water drop is not this page. CLR may etch older tiles. Spot-test a hidden corner either way.

If you cannot name it, do not acid it.

## Vinegar towels on light film

Faint white haze you can barely feel. Not raised crust. Distilled white vinegar is about 5% acetic acid. The Conversation: vinegar reacts with and dissolves limescale.

A spray bottle sheets off a vertical face and into the grout. Soak paper towels instead. Park them on the tile so the acid dwells where the film is.

1. Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle.
2. Soak paper towels. Press them onto the stained *faces* only. Overlap so they stay wet. Keep runoff off grout as much as you can. Grout is a different job.
3. Sit a few minutes. Re-wet if a towel starts to dry. Do not leave it overnight.
4. Pull the towels. Wipe with a non-scratch sponge. Rinse. Dry.

If two towel passes barely move the haze and a fingernail still catches white crust, stop stretching the vinegar. That is the gray-jug job. Not a longer soak.

## Soap scum is not limescale

Greasy film that smears is soap plus calcium — soap scum, not mineral crust. USGS: in hard water, soap reacts with calcium to form soap scum. Baking-soda paste is the scum pass, not a descaler. Do not mix baking soda with vinegar as a “boost.” That mix myth already lives on [baking soda vs vinegar](/articles/baking-soda-vs-vinegar-hard-water-stains). Take scum off first if the face is both greasy and chalky. Then come back to vinegar or CLR on the mineral.

## What not to do

- Do not spray acid until you have named the tile.
- Do not put vinegar or household CLR on marble, travertine, limestone, granite, or unsealed stone.
- Do not start with CLR on faint film. Vinegar towels first.
- Do not leave household CLR past two minutes. Do not let it dry.
- Do not mix CLR with bleach.
- Do not soak grout, glass, the tub, or fiberglass on this URL.
- Do not mix baking soda with vinegar to “boost” limescale removal.
- Do not treat soap scum as limescale.

## If the haze comes back

Hard water does not leave because you cleaned once. Wipe the faces dry after the shower so droplets do not bake. [Test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home) if you want the number. This page does not size a softener.
