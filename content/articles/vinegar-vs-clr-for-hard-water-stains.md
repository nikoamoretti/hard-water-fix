## The 10-second pick

Dry the surface. Run a finger across the stain.

| What you feel | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Smooth haze, faint spots, weekly film | Distilled white vinegar | About 5% acetic acid. Cheap, slow, fine for light calcium film. |
| Gritty, chalky, raised crust | [CLR] | Lactic and gluconic acids, mixed 50/50 with warm water, **2 minutes max**. Built for scale, not a soak. |
| Smooth haze that stays cloudy when wet | Neither | That is etching. Acid will not polish glass back. |

If it is both greasy and gritty, you also have soap scum. Take the film off with dish soap (or a soap-scum cleaner) before you reach for either acid. Scum shields the mineral layer.

## What each one actually is

**Vinegar** is acetic acid already diluted to roughly 5%. Grocery-store distilled white vinegar is the bottle this article means. Cleaning vinegar is often sold at 6%. Stronger is not automatically better on stone, grout, or coated glass. Good Housekeeping’s lab mix for shower doors is equal parts vinegar and water plus a teaspoon of dish soap, 15 minutes of contact, then wipe, rinse, and dry.

**CLR Calcium, Lime & Rust Remover** is a dedicated descaler. The brand lists lactic acid and gluconic acid as the chelating agents, EPA Safer Choice recognition, and use on glass, chrome, fiberglass, porcelain, and stainless steel. Official method: equal parts CLR and warm water, spot-test, apply, sit **no longer than 2 minutes**, rinse thoroughly with cold water. Repeat short cycles if needed. Full strength is allowed on a stubborn patch, still with the 2-minute cap. Never a long soak.

Lime-A-Way is the supermarket alt, not the pick. The bathroom Lime, Calcium & Rust cleaner is a sulfamic-acid formula. Reckitt’s SmartLabel says apply, wipe, and rinse promptly with cold water, and it claims it can knock back heavy limescale in about 60 seconds. It is also labeled corrosive, and it is **not** for cookware, coffee makers, kettles, humidifiers, or washing machines. Do not grab the toilet-bowl Lime-A-Way by mistake. That bottle is a different (hydrochloric-acid) product and is for bowls only.

## When vinegar is enough

Faint spots on glass, chrome that looks dusty after a shower, a film that vinegar fog lifts in one pass. Mix equal parts distilled white vinegar and water, add a little dish soap so it clings, spray, wait 15 minutes, wipe, rinse, dry.

That is also the weekly habit after the heavy lift. Once [CLR] has taken the crust off a shower door, vinegar plus a squeegee is how you stay off the bottle. The shower-door how-to is the companion piece.

Vinegar loses when the deposit is thick enough to feel, when you have already done two 15-minute passes and the grit is still there, or when you are on a surface the 15-minute wet time will damage (unsealed grout, natural stone, some older porcelain). “Leave it overnight” is how people etch finishes. Repeat a timed pass. Do not extend the clock.

## When to use CLR

If a fingernail catches the stain, skip the pantry. [CLR Calcium, Lime & Rust Remover] is the product this query should buy.

1. Ventilate. Gloves. Mix **equal parts CLR and warm water**.
2. Spot-test a hidden corner. Towel off stone, brass, copper, aluminum, oil-rubbed bronze, and anything painted, coated, or sealed. CLR’s own do-not-use list names natural stone (marble and granite by name), colored grout, and coated or sealed surfaces.
3. Apply with a cloth, sponge, or brush. Timer on. **Two minutes.** Do not let it dry.
4. Rinse with cold water until it is gone.
5. Repeat at 50/50. Full strength only if the second pass still leaves crust, still two minutes, still a full rinse.

CLR on glass is listed as suitable when used that way. Damage stories are almost always a soak, a skipped rinse, or a coated pane.

Use CLR Brilliant Bath only if the problem is soap scum, not scale. Different bottle, different job.

## Lime-A-Way if CLR is sold out

Same job, harsher acid, tighter surface list. Reckitt’s bathroom cleaner: apply to the stain or a damp cloth, wipe, rinse promptly with cold water. Gloves and eye protection are on the label. Same stone, terrazzo, colored-grout, aluminum, and painted-surface bans. Same bleach ban.

If you came here for a coffee maker or a kettle, do not use Lime-A-Way. The label forbids those. Vinegar (if the manual allows it) or the appliance’s own descaler is the path. CLR has a published coffee-maker dilution (1 part CLR to 8 parts water for a 10–12 cup drip machine, then two clear-water cycles). Still check the manual. Some brands void the warranty if you use anything but their bottle.

## Never mix either one with bleach

Washington State Department of Health: do not mix bleach with ammonia, acids, or other cleaners. Mixed with an acid, sodium hypochlorite (household bleach) gives off chlorine gas. Vinegar is an acid. CLR is an acid. Lime-A-Way is an acid. Rust removers and toilet-bowl cleaners are on that same list.

CLR’s label says the same thing in fewer words: do not mix with bleach or other household cleaners. Lime-A-Way’s SmartLabel says it too.

Do not “disinfect after” by spraying bleach onto a surface that still has vinegar or CLR on it. Rinse, dry, then decide if you even need bleach. A shower door usually does not.

## Surfaces that get neither

- Marble, limestone, travertine, granite, terrazzo, cultured marble
- Unsealed or colored grout
- Brass, copper, aluminum, galvanized metal, oil-rubbed bronze
- Painted, coated, or factory-sealed glass and fixtures
- Anything you cannot rinse thoroughly inside two minutes

Acid dissolves calcium. Those stones are calcium. CLR says a spill on marble is a call to a stone pro, not a wipe and a hope.

## FAQ

**Can I just use vinegar instead of CLR?**
Yes, on light film. No, on crust you can feel. Two failed vinegar passes is the signal to buy the bottle, not to soak longer.

**Is CLR just strong vinegar?**
No. Vinegar is acetic acid. CLR’s listed chelators are lactic acid and gluconic acid. Different acids, timed contact, a rinse rule. Treat it like a descaler, not a condiment.

**Which is safer?**
Safer on the surface you have, used as directed. Vinegar is milder and slower. CLR is EPA Safer Choice recognized and still banned on stone. Lime-A-Way’s bathroom formula is labeled corrosive. All three are unsafe mixed with bleach.

**Will either one fix cloudy glass that stays cloudy when wet?**
No. That is etching. Stop.
