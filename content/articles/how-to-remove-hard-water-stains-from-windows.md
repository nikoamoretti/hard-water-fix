## Wet test before you spray

Do this on a dry, uncleaned patch, in daylight.

1. Run a fingertip across the haze.
2. Dampen that same patch with water. Look again.

| What you see / feel | What it is | Next move |
|---|---|---|
| Chalky, slightly raised; fades when wet | Mineral film or crust on the pane | Vinegar first. CLR only if a fingernail still catches crust. |
| Sprinkler arcs, drip lines, lower-pane rings | Dried irrigation or rain on the *outside* | Same ladder. Keep acid off the frame. |
| Room-side spots after cooking or showers | Indoor condensation film | Same vinegar pass. Wipe dry so new droplets do not bake. |
| Smooth frost; same cloudy wet or dry | Etch | Stop. Acid will not un-etch it. |
| Fog between two panes, not on either face | Failed insulated-glass seal | Not a cleaner. Replacement. |

Northern Touch: hard water stains sit on top of the glass; glass etching alters the glass itself. If cloudiness fades or changes when wet, the issue is likely mineral buildup. If it looks the same whether the glass is wet or dry, etching is likely present. Stemware uses the same wet split on [cloudy dishwasher glasses](/articles/cloudy-dishwasher-glasses-etching-or-hard-water).

USGS hardness is Ca+Mg. Dried droplets leave that film. Acid dissolves the film. It does not patch glass. CLR’s FAQ: if the remover did not help at all, the glass may be etched — tiny cuts from deposits in the water. Do not grind more acid into that pane.

## Vinegar first on light film

Faint white haze you can barely feel. Not raised crust. Distilled white vinegar is about 5% acetic acid. The Conversation: vinegar reacts with and dissolves limescale. Northern Touch: vinegar may remove light hard water spots; it usually will not remove heavy mineral deposits.

Keep acid off painted sills, anodized frames, and rubber seals. Andersen’s anodized-finish care: no acid cleaners.

Pop the screen out so liquid does not sit in mesh. Work in shade if the sun is on the pane. Heat plus leftover acid is how people mark glass.

1. Distilled white vinegar. Grocery bottle.
2. Spray the *glass* only. Or wet a paper towel and park it on the spots so runoff does not hit the sash.
3. Keep the pane wet. Do not let it dry. Do not leave it overnight.
4. Wipe with microfiber. Rinse. Buff dry.

If two wet passes barely move the haze and a fingernail still catches white crust, stop stretching the vinegar. That is the gray-jug job. Not a longer soak.

Indoor condensation spots get the same vinegar pass. Outdoor sprinkler rings often need the next step. Same chemistry. Different thickness.

## Distilled rinse, then dry

Tap is the mineral source. A tap rinse can put the same Ca+Mg back on the pane you just cleaned. Distilled has those minerals stripped. Use it for the last rinse. Then dry.

Squeegee or microfiber. Edges and corners first. Outdoor work in shade if you can. You do not fill a window with distilled. You rinse and dry with it.

Glass Doctor’s water-spot pages treat sprinkler and rain rings as mineral left when droplets dry. The fix is get that film off, then do not leave a new wet edge to bake. Distilled plus a wipe is that last step. It is not a softener.

## Still hazy when wet: etch. Stop.

If vinegar and the two-minute CLR passes are done, and the haze still looks the same **when the glass is wet**, that is etch. Not leftover film. Do not reclean. ## If the spots come back

Sprinklers that hit the glass will keep writing the same rings. Aim the heads off the pane. Indoor condensation: wipe the room-side glass dry after a steam event instead of letting droplets bake. Hard water does not leave because you cleaned once. [Test the tap](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home). This page does not size a softener.

## FAQ

**Will vinegar take sprinkler spots off windows?**
Light film, often. Northern Touch: vinegar may remove light hard water spots; heavy deposits usually need more. Two failed wet passes means crust, not overnight vinegar.

**Can I use CLR on the whole window?**
On the *glass* only, and only after vinegar fails on crust. Two minutes. Do not let it dry. Keep it off frames, painted sills, anodized aluminum, and rubber seals.

**Why distilled water?**
Tap is the mineral source. A distilled last rinse does not paint Ca+Mg back on while you dry.

**The window is still cloudy when it is wet. Now what?**
Etch. Acid will not fix it. Use the restore-etched-shower-glass URL above. Do not put Rain-X Shower Door on house windows.

**Is this the shower-door method?**
No. Stall doors live on that other URL. House windows have frames and seals acid must not touch.

**The cloud is between two panes.**
