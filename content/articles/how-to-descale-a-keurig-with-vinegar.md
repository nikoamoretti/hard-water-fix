Vinegar will dissolve the calcium in a Keurig. That is not the argument. The argument is taste, time, and what the sticker on *your* machine actually says.

Current Keurig support and most new manuals tell you to use [Keurig Descaling Solution]. Older Keurig PDFs still on keurig.com list **white vinegar as Method 2**. Both are real. If the brewer is under warranty, or the descale light will not clear, use their bottle. If it is out of warranty and you already have distilled white vinegar, the pantry method works — you will just rinse longer, because acetic acid lingers in a way citric descalers do not.

## What you are actually cleaning

Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium on the heater and in the internal lines. Keurig’s own descale page: scale is non-toxic, and left alone it slows the brew and wrecks the cup. They want descaling every 3 to 6 months, or when the machine says “Time to descale.” The reminder is a volume counter, not a hardness sensor. Hard-water houses should not wait for the light.

Remove the water filter from the reservoir before any acid goes in. No K-Cup in the holder. Ceramic mug, not paper. Power stays on during the sit unless your specific sheet says otherwise.

## When to skip vinegar and buy the bottle

Use [Keurig Descaling Solution] if any of these are true:

- The use-and-care guide in the box names only their solution
- The machine is still under Keurig’s limited warranty and you do not want a fight about “unapproved cleaner”
- You have already vinegar-descaled and the coffee still tastes like a pickle
- The descale light stayed on after a vinegar run (some models expect their cycle)

Their current procedure, from Keurig’s own sheets: empty the reservoir, pour in the **entire bottle**, fill that empty bottle with water and pour that in too (so the working mix is 1:1 solution and water), largest brew size, no pod, keep going until Add Water, then **let it sit 30 minutes powered on**. Dump what is left, rinse the reservoir, fill with fresh water, and run **at least 12** large rinsing brews. Foam in the cup is the solution hitting scale. If the machine coughs air and almost nothing comes out, unplug, rinse the reservoir, plug back in, and keep rinsing.

That 30-minute sit plus 12 rinses is the cycle this article copies for vinegar. It is also why the bottle is worth buying: no vinegar smell, fewer “one more rinse” cups, and it matches the light.

## How to descale a Keurig with vinegar

Distilled white vinegar only. About 5% acetic acid. Not apple cutting, not cleaning vinegar at 6% unless you like guessing.

The 1:1 mix below is the practical match for how Keurig dilutes their own bottle. Older Keurig vinegar sheets used **undiluted** vinegar in the reservoir and, on some models, a **4-hour** sit. If you follow those PDFs to the letter, use undiluted and the longer sit. For a modern reservoir machine, 1:1 plus 30 minutes is enough acid and less abuse of the gaskets.

1. Empty the reservoir. Pull the charcoal filter if there is one. Disable Auto Off if your model has it.
2. Fill the reservoir with **equal parts distilled white vinegar and water**, up to the max line (or with ~48 oz of vinegar if you are on the old undiluted sheet).
3. Largest cup size. No pod. Brew, dump the hot liquid in the sink, repeat until Add Water.
4. Leave the brewer **on** for **30 minutes**. Do not brew. This is the sit that actually attacks scale on the heater.
5. Dump leftover vinegar. Wash the reservoir with dish soap and water so you are not rinsing through a vinegar film.
6. Fill with fresh water. Largest cup, no pod, dump, repeat. **Twelve rinses is the Keurig number for their solution.** Vinegar often needs more. Keep going until a steam-only brew does not smell like salad. Count 12 as the floor, not the ceiling.
7. Put the filter back. Brew a sacrificial cup of coffee if you still doubt the rinse.

Keurig Mini / K10-style tanks are smaller. The old Mini vinegar sheet was 10 oz undiluted, brew, another 10 oz, power off as it starts to dispense, 30-minute sit, then four 10 oz water rinses. Use that if you have a Mini. Do not pour a full 48 oz into a 10 oz tank.

## Urnex Dezcal if you want citric, not Keurig’s label

[Urnex Dezcal] is the non-branded descaler cafes already use. Citric-based, no vinegar smell, sold as single-use powder packets or a liquid. Consumer powder (typical 28 g / 1 oz packet): dissolve **one packet in 32 oz (1 L) of lukewarm water**, pour into the empty reservoir, brew the tank through with no pod, then run **at least two to three** full tanks of clean water. Liquid: Urnex’s in-machine dose is **1 oz per liter** of tank capacity.

Dezcal will not automatically be “the” cycle a Keurig descale light is waiting for. If the light is the whole problem, their bottle is still the cleaner bet. If the machine is a generic drip or you already descale a kettle with citric, Dezcal is the one jar that covers more than Keurig.

Do not pour CLR, Lime-A-Way, or shower descaler into a coffee maker. CLR’s own FAQ has a coffee-maker dilution, and it also says check the manufacturer first. Keurig is not that manufacturer.

## If it still brews slow, or the light will not die

- Repeat the full cycle with [Keurig Descaling Solution], not a second vinegar bath
- Needle the exit needle (the one that punctures the pod) — scale and coffee grounds clog it independently of the heater
- Replace a cracked reservoir gasket rather than soaking longer
- Some models need a button combo to exit descale mode after the rinses. That combo is in *your* manual, not a universal cheat. Do not hold random buttons hoping.

## What not to do

- Do not mix vinegar or any descaler with bleach. Acid plus bleach makes chlorine gas (Washington State DOH).
- Do not use apple-cider or balsamic anything.
- Do not skip the 30-minute sit. Brewing vinegar through once is a rinse, not a descale.
- Do not stop at four water brews because an old Mini PDF said four. Taste the steam. Twelve is the modern floor.
- Do not descale with a pod in the holder.

## FAQ

**Does vinegar void a Keurig warranty?**
Keurig does not publish a one-line “vinegar voids it” on the current descale shop page. Current manuals name their solution. Older keurig.com PDFs list vinegar as Method 2. If you are in-warranty, use [their bottle] and keep the receipt. That is the honest version.

**Vinegar or descaling solution?**
Same mineral job. Vinegar is cheaper and smells for a day. Their solution (and Dezcal) are citric, rinse cleaner, and match the machine’s own script. Buy the bottle once; keep vinegar for shower doors.

**How often?**
Keurig: every 3 to 6 months, or when the light says so. Hard water: closer to 3.

**Can I use the citric acid from the shower-head article?**
You can, mixed as a descaler (Keurig’s Mini sheet even had a citric-acid path). [Dezcal] is pre-measured. Do not guess tablespoons into a $150 brewer if a packet exists.
