## Pull the cover first when possible.

**Pull the cover** first when the screws or snap ring will move. A removed plate soaks in a bowl. A plate still on the wall gets a **wipe**, not a flood down the hole. Honesty: **pull the cover first when possible.** Do not start by pouring acid into the overflow opening.

1. Confirm the part is the **overflow cover / faceplate**, not the [drain stopper](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-bathtub-drain-stopper), not the [bathtub surface](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-bathtub). Lay a towel in the tub so a dropped screw does not chip enamel or scratch acrylic.
2. Photograph the plate and the screw positions. Two-hole plates usually take two screws. Snap-on covers pull straight after you find the release. Trip-lever plates come off with the lever still in the plate — do not yank the rod behind the wall and call it this job.
3. **Pull the cover.** Unscrew by hand or with a screwdriver that fits. Lift the plate off the overflow gasket. Set screws in a cup. **Do not force seized screws.** Chip risk on enamel. Crack risk on acrylic. If the screws will not turn with light pressure, stop the pull. Wipe the face in place. Do not drill the tub wall. Do not pry the overflow elbow off the tub.
4. Wipe hair, soap film, and grit off the **removed cover** mechanically. Paper towel. Soft cloth. Dump the gunk in the trash — do not rinse a wad into the overflow opening. Do **not** start a mineral soak on top of a hair clump.
5. Leave the overflow **gasket** on the pipe unless it came off in your hand. Rubber does **not** sit in a long acid bath. A swollen overflow gasket leaks.
6. If the cover will not come off: vinegar **wipe of the face only**. Soft nylon brush on the slots and screw heads you can reach. Rinse the cloth. Dry the face. **Do not flood the overflow tube** to make up for a stuck plate.

A cover you can pull is the whole point. Acid in the overflow pipe does not clean the faceplate better than a bowl soak. It just sends strong acid down a tube you cannot rinse on a clock.

## No-flood honesty: do not pour strong acid down the overflow tube.

Honesty first: **do not flood the overflow tube with strong acid.** The hole behind the cover is a pipe. It is not a kettle. You cannot put a two-minute clock on a tube you just filled. You cannot rinse every wall of that pipe on command. Strong acid down the overflow can sit on the gasket, the overflow elbow, the tub wall behind the plate, and the drain shoes you cannot see.

Pull. Soak the plate. Brush the plate. Dry the plate. The tube stays a drain path, not a chemical tank.
