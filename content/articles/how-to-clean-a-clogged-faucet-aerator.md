A kitchen tap that used to roar and now dribbles is usually not the valve. It is the aerator: a small screen and a washer screwed into the spout, packed with calcium, magnesium, and grit. Unscrew it, soak it, brush the mesh, put it back in order. If the screen is torn or the plastic is shot, replace the whole insert. That is the job.

## What you are looking at

The aerator mixes air into the stream so it does not splash. Hard water dries in the mesh. Sediment sits on top of that. Flow drops, the stream sprays sideways, or it comes out as a needle.

Two shapes:

- **Exposed collar** — you can see a chrome or plastic ring on the tip. Unscrew by hand, counterclockwise as you look *up* at it.
- **Cache / hidden** — flush with the spout, castle-tooth notches on the face. Use the aerator key that came with the faucet. Do not chew it with needle-nose.

## Unscrew it (cloth first)

1. Close the drain or drop a towel in the sink. The washer is the part you will lose.
2. Hand-loose if you can. Dry the collar. Rubber jar gripper helps.
3. Still stuck: wrap the collar in a cloth, then pliers.
4. Photograph the stack as it comes apart so you can reverse it.

A vinegar-soaked cloth around a calcified collar for 30–60 minutes will often break the mineral weld.

## Soak: vinegar for time, CLR for crust

**Vinegar** (distilled white, about 5% acetic): cover the parts in a cup. 20–30 minutes for haze, up to an hour for white crust. Toothbrush both faces of the screen. Rinse until the mesh is bright.

**[CLR Calcium, Lime & Rust Remover]** when the mesh is a mineral cookie. Mix **equal parts CLR and warm water**, submerge, **hold 2 minutes**, then rinse. Repeat the 2-minute pass if needed. Do not bag undiluted CLR onto a mounted faucet and walk away. Keep it off brass, copper, aluminum, natural stone, and oil-rubbed bronze. Never mix with bleach.

After either soak: run hot water through the empty spout for 30 seconds, then reassemble in photographed order. Hand-tight plus a snug cloth-wrap turn.

## Bag-on-tap if it will not come off

1. Rubber band on the spout neck first.
2. Sandwich bag, enough vinegar to submerge the face.
3. 30–60 minutes. Not overnight.
4. Dump, run hot water 30 seconds, toothbrush the face in place.

If flow is still dead, get the key or [replace the insert].

## When to replace instead of soaking again

Replace after two timed soaks if the screen is torn, the housing is cracked, the washer is flattened, or a cache insert is chewed up.

## What not to do

- Do not leave CLR on longer than 2 minutes.
- Do not mix vinegar or CLR with bleach.
- Do not jam pliers into a cache aerator.
- Do not lose the washer down the drain.

## FAQ

**How often?**
Hard water: every few months, or when the stream goes sideways.

**Vinegar or CLR?**
Vinegar if you have time. [CLR] if you can feel crust.

**The water is still slow after a clean aerator.**
Then it is the supply, the shutoffs, or the cartridge. Not this page.
