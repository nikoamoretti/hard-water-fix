## Brown is oil. White is mineral.

Look at the film in daylight.

**Brown, tan, or a rainbow sheen** that soap cuts is coffee oil. Beans leave lipids on glass and on a stainless liner. Warm water. A drop of dish soap. A bottle brush. Rinse. That is not a hardness job.

**White, chalky, or cloudy rings** that soap does not move are mineral. Heat drops calcium carbonate. The Conversation (Nathan Kilah): vinegar reacts with and dissolves limescale. Distilled white vinegar is about 5% acetic acid. Grocery bottle. Pantry. No branded descaler in this pot.

Both films: soap first, then vinegar on the white. Do not mix vinegar and baking soda in the carafe. The fizz is carbon dioxide leaving, not extra scale power. That mix already lives on [baking soda vs vinegar for hard water stains](/articles/baking-soda-vs-vinegar-hard-water-stains).

If you want the hardness number, [test the tap at home](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home). This page does not size a softener.

## Vinegar soak for the white film

Cool carafe. Empty. Glass or thermal stainless liner. Not the machine.

Cuisinart DCC-3200: wash the carafe and lid in warm, soapy water, or the dishwasher upper rack only. That is daily oil, not the mineral soak. The Spruce leftover-stain pass: vinegar, sit at least 30 minutes, soapy water, bottle brush. This page uses 30 minutes to a few hours. Not overnight as the default.

1. Warm soapy water first if brown oil is still there. Bottle brush. Rinse.
2. Distilled white vinegar. Grocery. About 5%. Not apple cider.
3. Fill enough to cover the white film. Full if the haze runs up the wall. Room-temperature vinegar. Do not pour boiling vinegar into a cold glass pot.
4. Sit **30 minutes**. Stubborn chalk can sit a few hours. Re-wet the walls if the level drops.
5. Bottle brush. Nylon. Cuisinart DCC-3200: do not clean the carafe with cleansers, steel wool pads, or other abrasive materials. Scratches on glass are crack starts.
6. Dump. Rinse with tap until it does not smell like salad. Then a last rinse with [distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) so tap minerals do not dry back on as spots.
7. Air-dry inverted, or wipe dry. Tap droplets on a clean wall are tomorrow’s rings.

If two timed soaks lift the chalk and the glass looks clear while wet, stop. You had film.

A thermal stainless carafe is a liner, not a pan. Same vinegar. Same nylon brush. Do not jam a pad into the vacuum wall.

## No boiling into cold glass

Glass carafes crack from thermal shock: a hot wall and a cold wall expanding at different rates. Cuisinart DCC-3200: do not set a hot carafe on a wet or cold surface. Do not use a cracked carafe or a carafe with a loose or weakened handle.

Read Consulting (Dr. Thomas Read): borosilicate coffee carafes failed after abrasive-pad scratches on the outside bottom, then thermal cycling between a hot brew and a cold rinse. The crack started at the scratch. The wavy path was thermally driven.

Cool the pot on the counter before a cold rinse. Do not dump boiling liquid into a carafe that just came out of a cold sink. Do not park a hot glass pot on a wet board. Nylon brush, not a scouring pad. Stainless thermal does not shatter the same way. It still does not want a pad on the liner.

## If the haze looks the same wet and dry, that can be etch

Do the wet test on a dry, uncleaned patch, in daylight. Run a fingertip across the haze. Dampen that same patch. Look again.

White crust that feels chalky and fades while wet is mineral film. Vinegar is still the job. If the frost is identical wet and dry, the glass may already be etched. Household acid will not un-etch glass. That fork already lives on [cloudy dishwasher glasses: etching or hard water](/articles/cloudy-dishwasher-glasses-etching-or-hard-water). Do not keep soaking etched glass.

A dishwasher can etch a glass carafe the same way it etches wine glasses. If the booklet allows the upper rack, that is wash, not a restore.

## What not to do

- Do not treat brown oil as hard water. Soap first.
- Do not mix vinegar and baking soda in the carafe. [Baking soda vs vinegar](/articles/baking-soda-vs-vinegar-hard-water-stains) is the mix page.
- Do not use abrasive pads, steel wool, or scouring powder on glass or a stainless liner.
- Do not pour boiling liquid into a cold glass carafe. Do not set a hot glass carafe on a wet or cold surface.
- Do not soak overnight as the default. Thirty minutes to a few hours.
- Do not keep aciding a frost that looks the same wet and dry. That can be etch.
- Do not run this soak as a machine descale. The pot is not the heater.
- Do not finish with tap and call it done. Last rinse is [distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) so spots do not re-form as it air-dries.

## FAQ

**Will vinegar remove hard water stains from a coffee carafe?**
Often, if they are still mineral film. Distilled white vinegar, 30 minutes to a few hours, bottle brush, rinse until there is no salad smell, last rinse with distilled water. Brown film is oil. Soap that first.

**Why not mix baking soda with the vinegar?**
The fizz is CO2. Kilah: the mix is a visual placebo for scale. Use soap on oil. Use vinegar on mineral. Separately. Live comparison: [baking soda vs vinegar for hard water stains](/articles/baking-soda-vs-vinegar-hard-water-stains).

**Glass or stainless?**
Same vinegar. Same nylon brush. Glass also has thermal shock. Stainless thermal is a liner. No pad on either.

**Can I put the carafe in the dishwasher?**
Only if *your* booklet says so — Cuisinart DCC-3200: upper rack only. A dishwasher will not replace a vinegar soak, and it can etch glass. Wet-same frost: [cloudy dishwasher glasses](/articles/cloudy-dishwasher-glasses-etching-or-hard-water).

**The CLEAN light is still on.**
That is the machine. Not this pot. [Cuisinart](/articles/how-to-descale-cuisinart-coffee-maker) or [Ninja](/articles/how-to-descale-ninja-coffee-maker), depending on the box. Comparison: [vinegar vs descaling solution](/articles/vinegar-vs-descaling-solution-coffee-maker).

**Why distilled for the last rinse?**
Tap is how the spots got there. [Distilled water](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07VD4KN28?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) has no calcium or magnesium to dry as rings.

**How hard is hard?**
USGS: 0–60 mg/L as CaCO3 is soft; 61–120 moderately hard; 121–180 hard; above 180 very hard. [Test at home](/articles/how-to-test-water-hardness-at-home) if you want the number.
