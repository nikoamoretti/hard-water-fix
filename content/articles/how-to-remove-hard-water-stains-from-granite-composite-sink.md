## Name it first. Resin bowl is not a slab.

## Baking-soda paste first. Mechanical, not a descaler.

Baking soda. Grocery box. Pantry. No Amazon link.

This is grit, not acid. It lifts film. It does not dissolve scale the way vinegar does. Ruvati: a baking soda and water paste can be your best friend; apply it, let it sit a few minutes, then scrub gently with a non-abrasive brush or cloth. Home Improvement Way: mix to toothpaste thickness, 10 to 15 minutes, do not let it dry completely. NAITRON stain guide: paste, 10–15 minutes, soft sponge, then rinse and dry.

Do not mix the paste with vinegar as the clean. Acid plus bicarbonate is salt, water, and CO2. The fizz is gas, not extra scale power. That mix already lives on [baking soda vs vinegar](/articles/baking-soda-vs-vinegar-hard-water-stains).

If someone already used bleach in this bowl, rinse with plain water until it does not smell like bleach. Then start. Never mix leftover bleach with a later vinegar cloth. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

1. Mix baking soda with a little water to a thick paste. Toothpaste thick.
2. Spread it on the haze only. Wet basin. Do not dump dry grit.
3. Sit 10–15 minutes. Re-wet if it starts to crust dry.
4. Wipe with a soft sponge. Circular. No green pad. No steel wool.
5. Rinse. Dry with a second cloth.

Repeat a fresh paste if a fingernail still catches chalk. Stop if the patch is smooth and dull. That is the coat, not leftover scale.

## Wear vs crust. Dull after cleaning is not mineral.

Raised, chalky, a fingernail catches: crust on the gel or topcoat. The paste is for that. A maker-allowed vinegar cloth is for leftover mineral, not for gloss.

Smooth and dull after the crust is gone: worn topcoat. Old powder, a Magic Eraser habit, acid left too long, or the factory coat worn through. Cleaning will not polish that back. A cloth does not refill resin.

Southern Home: after hard-water film and harsh cleaners, a deep clean did not restore the look; a thin coat of mineral oil made it look better and sheds water. That oil is cosmetic. NAITRON: mineral oil is temporary shine; it does not seal pores; it wears off. Home Improvement Way: conditioning oil after a deep clean is optional luster, not a descaler.

Do the feel test before you add acid.

1. Rinse. Dry.
2. Feel it with a fingernail.
3. Raised and chalky: another paste. Then vinegar only if that maker wrote it. Stop on their clock.
4. Smooth and dull, same wet and dry: stop. That is the coat, not leftover scale. Oil if you want the look. It is not a mineral fix.
