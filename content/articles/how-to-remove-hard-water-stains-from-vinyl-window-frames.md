1. Dry the frame first. Wipe standing water so the mix sits on film, not diluted puddles. Mask or wipe the glass edge if you want to keep drips off the pane.
2. Mix pantry distilled white vinegar **50/50** with water. Dampen a soft cloth — do not pour a puddle into the track.
3. Wipe the stained vinyl. Short dwell: a few minutes of wet contact, then wipe. Stay with the cloth. Do not soak overnight. Do not flood **weep holes**.
4. Soft wipe in straight lines. No green pad. No abrasive powder. No steel wool on vinyl.

6. Check the **weep holes** at the sill / track. Soft toothbrush or a folded cloth corner only. Keep them open so water can leave. Do not pack grit, wax, or sealant into the weeps. Do not soak them with acid and walk away.

## Hard-water signs on vinyl window frames

Know the film before you open the jug.

- Faint white haze after sprinkler days or a wash: early mineral film. 50/50 vinegar wipe and a dry habit stretch the gap.
- Raised chalk a fingernail catches on the sash or sill plastic: carbonate scale. Short vinegar pass. Soft wipe only.
- Powdery dull vinyl that scrapes as pigment, not mineral: UV chalking. Cleaning removes film on top; it does not repaint faded plastic.
- Wet stripe in the track that never dries: check weep holes. A clogged weep holds water that scales faster than an open sill.
- Spots on the **glass**: wrong page — use [windows](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-windows). Do not Rain-X the vinyl.

This is still the vinyl frame. Do not turn it into a whole-house [siding](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-vinyl-siding) wash or a glass polish.

## Weep holes stay open — do not soak them

Vinyl window sills and tracks drain through small weep holes. Soft-clear them with a folded cloth corner or a soft toothbrush after the wipe. Do not force a pick that gouges vinyl. Do not fill them with caulk “to look cleaner.” Do not flood them with vinegar or CLR and leave a soak. Blocked or acid-soaked weeps leave a permanent wet band that grows fresh hard-water film and can feed leaks into the wall.

If water sits in the track after every rain or wash, clear the weeps before you blame the vinegar cloth for “not working.” Scale on a wet stripe returns until the water can leave.
