The white crust on the heating plate is limescale: calcium carbonate from hard water. A mild acid dissolves it. [Food-grade citric acid] is the bottle to keep in the cupboard — stronger than vinegar on scale, almost no smell, and the same jar already covers a shower head. Distilled white vinegar works. You will taste it in the next two pots of tea unless you rinse like you mean it.

Unplug. Cool. **Never put the base in the sink.** The kettle is a bowl with a heater in the floor. Only the interior gets liquid.

## Citric acid (the default)

1. Unplug. Empty. If the kettle has a filter in the spout, pull it and soak it in the same solution in a cup.
2. Fill to about half with water (or 500 mL if that is clearly marked).
3. Stir in **1–2 tablespoons** of [food-grade citric acid powder]. Two tablespoons for a crust you can see; one for a light film. Food-grade only.
4. Boil. Switch off. Let it sit **20–30 minutes**. Do not walk away overnight.
5. Pour out. Rinse three times. Wipe the floor with a soft sponge. No steel wool.
6. Fill with fresh water, boil once, dump. That is the taste rinse.

Repeat one timed cycle if the plate is still chalky. Two failures usually means the flakes are sitting loose — wipe, do not keep boiling acid.

Hard-water houses descale kettles monthly.

## Vinegar if that is what is in the pantry

Fellow: **half vinegar, half water**, boil, sit **at least 30 minutes**, dump, rinse thoroughly. Good Housekeeping goes weaker: **1 part distilled white vinegar to 3 parts water**, about a third full, boil, sit **20 minutes**, rinse several times, then an optional water-only boil.

Use distilled white vinegar, about 5%. Not apple cider. The smell is why this is Method 2.

## Dezcal if you want a packet, not a jar

[Urnex Dezcal] is the ready-made citric descaler. Consumer powder: dissolve **one packet in 32 oz (1 L) of lukewarm water**, pour into the kettle, heat per the packet, sit, dump, then at least two full rinses.

## What not to do

- **Do not immerse the base** or run the kettle under the tap.
- Do not use abrasive pads on a stainless or coated interior.
- Do not mix any descaler with bleach.
- Do not ignore the min-fill line.

## How often

When you can see white on the floor, or boil time stretches. A monthly citric pass in a hard-water kitchen is cheaper than a new kettle.

## FAQ

**Is the crust rust?**
Usually no. Limescale is off-white or tan. Citric still lifts mineral. If the floor is pitted metal, stop scraping.

**Can I use lemon juice instead of powder?**
Good Housekeeping says 2 tablespoons of lemon juice in a half-full kettle, same boil-and-sit. Juice has sugar. Powder is cleaner.

**The kettle auto-shuts off before a full boil.**
Scale on the sensor. Sit 30 minutes, rinse, try again. If it still will not heat, that is a switch, not this page.
