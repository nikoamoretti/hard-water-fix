These are not two strengths of the same acid. [Bar Keepers Friend](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07JGH35XZ?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) Powdered Cleanser is **oxalic acid plus a glass-oxide abrasive**. You wet the surface, scrub, and rinse within a minute. [CLR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) Calcium, Lime & Rust Remover is a **lactic-and-gluconic liquid**. You dilute it 50/50 with warm water, sit **two minutes**, and rinse with cold water. Pick by the surface and the stain. There is no overall champion.

## The 10-second pick

USGS: hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heat them and they drop out as calcium carbonate. Soap plus calcium is scum. Rust is iron oxide; oxalic is built for that.

| What you have | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rust, cooked-on film, or mineral on stainless, porcelain, or cookware you can scrub | [Bar Keepers Friend](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07JGH35XZ?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) Powdered Cleanser | Oxalic + mild abrasive. Wet rub. Rinse within **1 minute**. |
| Raised limescale you can wet and rinse without grinding (glass, shower doors, labeled fixtures) | [CLR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) | Lactic + gluconic. **2 minutes**, then a cold rinse. No grit. |
| Chrome faucet | Soft Cleanser — [chrome faucet how-to](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-chrome-faucet) | Same oxalic family, less grit. Powder is not the faucet default. |
| Film or crust on a glass shower pane | Vinegar or CLR — [vinegar vs CLR](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains) | Do not paste BKF powder on a pane you do not want to haze. |
| Marble, granite, other natural calcium stone | Neither | Acid etches calcite. Both brands say stay off. |

If it is greasy *and* gritty, take the soap scum off first. Scum shields the mineral.

## What each bottle actually is

**[Bar Keepers Friend](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07JGH35XZ?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) Powdered Cleanser** brand ingredients: glass oxide abrasive, oxalic acid, surfactant. No chlorine bleach. Official method: wet the surface, sprinkle, rub gently, **rinse thoroughly within one minute**, wipe dry. Do not let it sit longer. The powder was never meant to be used dry.

That is a **scrub**. Oxalic dissolves rust and mineral. The grit lifts what the acid loosened. BKF’s FAQ: powder and Soft Cleanser are **not** the same product. Powder has more abrasive. Soft Cleanser is pre-diluted — oxalic plus citric, already wet — and is the bottle already on the chrome-faucet page. Do not treat the two as identical grit.

**[CLR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) Calcium, Lime & Rust Remover** is a dedicated descaler. Brand-listed chelators: lactic acid and gluconic acid. EPA Safer Choice recognized. Official method: equal parts CLR and warm water, spot-test, apply, sit **no longer than 2 minutes**, rinse thoroughly with cold water. Repeat short cycles. Full strength is allowed on a stubborn patch, still two minutes. Never a long soak. Label-suitable when used that way: chrome, fiberglass, porcelain, stainless steel, glass.

## Where Bar Keepers Friend wins

Stainless sink or cookware with heat tint, rust freckles, or a mineral film you can feel. Intact glazed porcelain. A rust bloom on a glazed sink you can scrub and rinse. The powder is the cooktop / stainless / sink grit.

1. Wet the surface. Gloves if you are doing more than a spot.
2. Sprinkle a little [Bar Keepers Friend](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07JGH35XZ?tag=hardwaterfi04-20), or make a wet paste so dust stays down.
3. Rub with a damp cloth or non-scratch sponge. Work with the grain on stainless.
4. **Rinse thoroughly within one minute.** Do not let the paste dry on a glaze or on stainless.
5. Wipe dry. Repeat a fresh pass if needed. Do not extend the clock.

BKF lists cookware, stainless, porcelain, chrome — and glass. That last one does not make powder the bottle for a shower pane you care about. Skip powder on a chrome faucet you like; Soft Cleanser is the lower-grit cousin on the [chrome faucet](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-chrome-faucet) page.

## Where CLR wins

Raised limescale you can wet, wait, and rinse — glass, shower doors, chrome fixtures on the label — without grinding grit into the surface. That is [CLR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20).

1. Ventilate. Gloves. Mix **equal parts CLR and warm water**.
2. Spot-test. Towel off stone, brass, copper, aluminum, oil-rubbed bronze, and anything painted, coated, or sealed.
3. Apply with a cloth, sponge, or brush. **Two minutes.** Do not let it dry.
4. Rinse with cold water until it is gone.
5. Repeat at 50/50. Full strength only if crust remains, still two minutes, still a full rinse.

Glass film and crust belong on [vinegar vs CLR](/articles/vinegar-vs-clr-for-hard-water-stains) and the [glass shower door](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-glass-shower-doors) how-to. Soft Cleanser is marketed as a shower-door cleaner; powder is still the wrong grit on a pane you do not want to haze. CLR is the wrong bottle on aluminum (brand FAQ: it can take the finish off and pit it). Shower-door tracks are often anodized aluminum — that job is [how to clean shower door tracks](/articles/how-to-clean-shower-door-tracks-hard-water), not a CLR soak of the frame.

## Shared stay-off

**Marble, granite, limestone, travertine, cultured marble, other natural stone.** Acid dissolves calcium carbonate. Those stones *are* calcium carbonate. Natural Stone Institute: calcareous stone is acid-sensitive; lemon, vinegar, and other acids etch. BKF: never on granite or marble. CLR: no natural stone, marble (including synthetic), or granite. A spill on marble is a stone pro, not a wipe and a hope.

**Do not let BKF dry** — one minute, then a thorough rinse, glaze and stainless included. **CLR two-minute cap** — do not soak or wrap and leave. **Aluminum:** CLR stay off; BKF lists it but says test first on anodized or brushed metal. **Neither mixed with bleach.** Washington State DOH: bleach plus an acid makes chlorine gas. Vinegar is on their acid list. BKF is oxalic. CLR is lactic and gluconic. Both brands say the same.

Unsealed or colored grout, painted or coated finishes, oil-rubbed bronze, and anything you cannot rinse on the clock: neither.

## What not to do

- Do not pick a winner and use it on every surface.
- Do not let [Bar Keepers Friend](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07JGH35XZ?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) dry on a glaze or on stainless.
- Do not leave [CLR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4G2BJKX?tag=hardwaterfi04-20) past two minutes.
- Do not put either on marble or granite.
- Do not paste BKF powder on a glass shower door you do not want to haze.
- Do not put CLR on aluminum.
- Do not mix either with bleach.
- Do not treat powder and Soft Cleanser as the same grit.
- Do not wrap a chrome faucet overnight — that method lives on the [chrome faucet](/articles/how-to-remove-hard-water-stains-from-chrome-faucet) page.

## FAQ

**Are they interchangeable?**
No. One is oxalic plus grit you scrub. The other is a lactic/gluconic liquid with a two-minute dwell. We did not run a lab.

**Powder or Soft Cleanser?**
Powder is this page’s pick: cookware, stainless, porcelain, rust you can scrub. Soft Cleanser is the same oxalic family, pre-diluted, less abrasive, and the bottle for chrome. BKF’s FAQ says they are not intended to be identical.

**Will Bar Keepers Friend scratch stainless?**
Used wet, with the grain, rinsed in a minute: BKF lists stainless. A dry sprinkle or a green pad will scratch.

**Can I use either on marble or granite?**
No. Both brands say stay off. Acid etches calcareous stone.

**What about a glass shower door?**
Use the vinegar-or-CLR ladder, not a BKF powder paste on the pane.

**Can I mix either with bleach?**
Never.
