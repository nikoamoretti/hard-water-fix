## Why acid still etches a slate shower

The crust you want gone is mostly calcium carbonate. The tile is usually siliceous. The sealer is not. The veins may not be. Dynamic Stone Tools: white vinegar is acetic acid, pH about 2–3; it etches and permanently damages calcareous stone on contact. Lemon is citric, same damage. CLR is highly acidic and causes severe etching. Commercial bathroom descalers are almost all acids.

Do not use vinegar “just for a second.” CyShower and other mills still reach for lemon or vinegar on slate. Do not follow that acid aside. NSI: use a neutral cleaner, then rinse and dry. The dull patch after acid is a hole in the finish or the sealer. Cleaning will not fill it.

Tile Council of North America: cement grout is dissolved by acids. The joints between slate tiles are cement. Vinegar that “just hits the grout” still hits the clefts next to it.

Never mix leftover vinegar or a rust remover with bleach. Poison Control: mixing bleach with an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Washington State DOH names vinegar as one of those acids, and rust-removal products too. Leave the area and breathe fresh air if you already mixed them. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

## Plastic scraper. Baking-soda paste. Distilled rinse. Dry.

Raised white crust a fingernail catches. Mineral sitting on sealed stone. Not an etch yet.

Dynamic Stone Tools: for moderate to heavy scale on stone, mechanical removal is the safest first approach. Dampen the deposits. Use a plastic scraper, never metal. Work from the edges toward the center. Lift the bulk before more chemistry.

1. Spot-test a hidden tile of the same stone.
2. Dampen the crust. Hold a plastic scraper almost flat. Work the crust, not the cleft.
3. Lift only what stands up. Stop the second you see new haze that stays smooth when wet.

Leftover film in the clefts. Baking soda and water make a paste. Pantry. No Amazon link. That paste is mechanical. It is not acid.

1. Mix baking soda with a little water. Peanut-butter thick.
2. Spread it on the crust and into clefts and grout only. Not a wall flood.
3. Sit 5 to 10 minutes for a thin ring. For a deep cleft stain, cover and let it dry, then lift. Do not scrub dry powder into honed or cleft slate.
4. Wipe with a damp cloth. Plastic scraper on leftover paste. Soft brush in the joints.

## Etch vs crust. Cleaning will not undo an etch.

White crust is mineral sitting on sealed stone. A dull patch after acid is a hole in the finish.

NSI: etch marks are acids left on the surface of the stone. Contact a stone dealer or a professional stone restorer for refinishing. OUTERclé: mineral buildup sits on top and can often be removed; etching is a chemical reaction with the stone, a dull or rough area that cleaning products cannot restore. An etch is missing stone or sealer, not a stain.

Do the wet/feel test before you pick a tool.

1. Wet the mark with a drop of distilled water.
2. Feel it with a fingernail.
3. Raised, chalky, catches a nail: crust on top. You can try to lift it.
4. Smooth and dull, same wet and dry: the finish is gone. That is an etch. Stop. Stone pro.

Do not keep scrubbing a smooth dull patch. A cloth will not restore the gloss. More acid will make the hole bigger.
