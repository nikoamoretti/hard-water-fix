## Unplug. Cool. Vinegar 1:1 in the pot. Wipe the plate. Rinse. Plain-water cook after.

Pantry distilled white vinegar. Equal parts water in the removable pot — or the tank fill **your** booklet prints if the model has a separate water reservoir. Soft sponge or soft cloth on nonstick. No Amazon vinegar URL. No “improve it” with CLR, a Keurig bottle, Breville / Dezcal powder, lemon, baking-soda grit, or a citric SKU as the default.

1. Unplug. Cool completely. No hot splash on a warm plate. Pull the inner pot and the lid (and the steam vent cap if the booklet allows).
2. Mix **1:1** distilled white vinegar (5% pantry bottle) and water. Pour into the **inner pot** only — or fill the maker’s water tank the way that sheet directs. Do **not** pour vinegar into the electric base, the hinge, or under the heating plate.
3. Soften: let the dilute vinegar sit in the pot **15–30 minutes** on a cool counter (or run a short cook / soak cycle only if **your** booklet allows vinegar in a powered pass). Stay in the room. Do not overnight-soak a nonstick bowl in strong acid as a habit.
4. Soft sponge or soft cloth on the pot walls and floor. No green pad. No steel wool. No powdered cleanser on nonstick. Corners and the water-line ring hide scale.
5. Empty the spent liquid into the sink. Rinse the pot thoroughly with clean water so no pickle film remains on a food-contact bowl.
6. Wipe the **heating plate** and the rim where the pot sits — damp soft cloth with the same 1:1 if chalky, then a clean damp rinse wipe, then dry. Soft sponge only. Never flood the base. Never pour liquid into the well under the plate.
7. **One plain-water cook after.** Return the rinsed pot. Fill with plain water to a normal cook line (prefer pantry distilled for this rinse if the tap is hard). Run a cook / steam cycle empty. Smell the pot. Water only. Not salad dressing. Discard that water. Dry the pot and plate before the next rice batch.

Skip that plain-water cook and the next pot of rice tastes like vinaigrette. If you still smell vinegar, run a second plain-water empty cook before food goes back.

Never mix leftover vinegar with bleach. Poison Control: bleach plus an acid, including vinegar, forms chlorine gas. Leave the area. Fresh air. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222**.

## Hard-water signs in a rice cooker

Know the film before you open the vinegar.

- Faint white haze on the pot wall after rice is emptied: early mineral film. Short 1:1 soft wipe and a dry habit stretch the gap.
- Raised chalk a fingernail catches at the water line or on the pot floor: carbonate scale. Soften 1:1 in the pot. Soft sponge. Rinse. Plain-water cook after.
- White dust or rings on the heating plate under the pot: wipe the plate — do not pour acid into the base to “soak” it.
- Longer heat-up, weak steam, or a cooker that struggles to finish: scale may be insulating the pot floor or the plate contact. Descale the pot. Soft wipe the plate. Then one plain-water cook.
- Brown or rusty tint on a stainless pot or plate rim: can be iron or heater discoloration, not only calcium. Stay on pantry vinegar first. Do not jump to bathroom lime gel in a food pot.

## Dry the plate — or fresh chalk returns

The vinegar pass removes chalk that is already there. Rinse water left in the pot or wet rings left on the heating plate become the next white bloom. Soft dry the pot and the plate after every descale, not only after a vinegar soak. Hard municipal water flash-dries into spots overnight.

If white rings reappear the morning after a clean pass, the vinegar worked — the air-dry did not. Towel the pot and plate next time. A pantry distilled fill for the post-vinegar cook helps when the tap tests hard. Do not paste an Amazon distilled gallon. Do not reach for CLR because “vinegar failed.” Check the dry habit and the plate wipe first.

## Prevention on a rice cooker — dry habits only

Prevention is the next cook, not a harsher acid.

- Empty leftover cook water. Wipe the cool pot dry when you can. Standing tap dries into fresh scale overnight.
- Soft wipe the heating plate when you see white dust — damp cloth, then dry. Never flood the base.
- Stick to pantry vinegar **1:1** in the pot for routine chalk — soften, soft sponge, rinse, plain-water cook after. No second acid as a habit.
